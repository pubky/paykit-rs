# Paykit v0.2 specification (Path A)

**Status.** Review candidate, not frozen. The reference prototype implements the spec but the rules this revision adds, which Section 18 lists ("Where the reference prototype differs"); `paykit-lib`, with `pubky-switch-core` for the Switch's rules, implements all of it, and their public vector tests rebuild and check every vector of both sets (Section 18 names the commit and the command). The wire format (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md`) is complete, with test vectors; an independent implementation, shared separately and written from the wire format alone, reproduces every vector of both sets but the cases the vectors READMEs list as generated by `paykit-lib` and `pubky-switch-core`, which it hasn't run; those crates' public vector tests rebuild and check every vector of their set. The spec is frozen once the team has decided the open questions and the team decisions (Section 17), and an independent human crypto review and the measurements on real infrastructure are done (Section 18).

**Built on the Pubky Switch.** Paykit v0.2 is a protocol on the Pubky Switch, an inbox and coordination service for Pubky identities specified on its own in [`spec/spec.md`](https://github.com/pubky/pubky-switch/blob/c218851541ced7e49ddc0df9754db7b70da3a4f8/spec/spec.md) ("the Pubky Switch spec") and [`spec/wire-format.md`](https://github.com/pubky/pubky-switch/blob/c218851541ced7e49ddc0df9754db7b70da3a4f8/spec/wire-format.md) ("the Switch wire format") of the pubky-switch repository, at commit `c218851541ced7e49ddc0df9754db7b70da3a4f8`. The Switch gives Paykit its identities and `identity.json`, app certificates and records, receive keys, Shared Data Keys and key bundles, the sealed and signed message, admission, claims and shared records, push, export and import, and identity management. This spec defines what Paykit adds on top: its scopes and the switch permissions each needs, its message kinds, its collections and their policies, the payment flows, Allowances, recurring requests, labelled selling scopes, the retention of payment evidence and the migration from v0.1. Where Paykit relies on a rule of the Switch, it cites it as "Pubky Switch spec, Section N" or "Switch wire N". Every such citation, and every Switch vector that Paykit's vectors use, means that commit, `c218851`: a later change to the Switch applies to Paykit only once a revision of this spec names its commit here. The pin is a citation of the Switch spec and its vectors at that commit, and says nothing about which calls the reference service serves at that commit.

**What this pin adopts.** This revision moves the pin from `c553428` to `c218851`, adopting:

- **Provider loss:** the recovery document builds on the newest version the signer signed; who may declare a loss and declaring it again; staged admission until the signer ends it; and uploads after a loss, with one `upload` transition for each and an answer that names what changed, what diverged and what counted for nothing. A Paykit app uploads the evidence it keeps (Section 7.2) as it did, and a write it sends the old service after the freeze gets `410 moved`, so it asks the new service again. A claim rebuilt from uploads is marked `rebuilt` in its view and lists only the entries written at the service, and an uploaded SETTLED proof for an obligation the new service holds open makes the claim divergent, doubtful, with no automatic payment (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6; Switch wire 6.8, 7.4.2, 7.4.8, 7.4.14).
- **Restore:** an entry of a claim compacted since the backup shows only as a stub, and the claim itself is read from the switch (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4; Switch wire 6.8, 7.5).
- **The writer's certificate:** a write whose record view carries a certificate that verifies for its writer is judged with that one, the certificate the service checked the write against, whatever certificate the writer holds now (Switch wire 6.2 step 1). Paykit's checks of a write's standing use it (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.5, Last ledger version).
- **Unreadable items and parts:** an entry left with no current target becomes an unreadable item also when an `identity.json` delivery ends an app's standing; a key bundle part is kept only until the app has passed it and holds the keys through it (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 3.5 and 8.2).
- **Open questions:** the switch has two more open questions, 26, bounding `renewed`, and 29, uploads from a dishonest app after a provider loss, so Paykit's own open questions are numbered 27, 28 and 30, the numbers being shared with the switch's (Section 17).
- **The reference client at this commit:** a batch the app keeps for a later try has one identity for its whole life and a terminal state with its own time, readable by batch, by message and by attempt, and an answer that a batch, message or attempt is unknown never means it was never sent; before dropping a message for its sender's standing, the client reads the sender's `identity.json` again, at most once a minute per sender, and holds the message while it can't (Switch wire 5.1, check 7); and a protocol may pass an entry without processing it, so a later re-send of the same message reaches it.
- No vector of Paykit's set changed. Between the two commits the Switch's set changed only in `http.json`, which gained cases, and in its `README.md`'s notes on those cases; Paykit's vectors use no `http.json` case.

The pull request that made this revision lists every adopted change and what it changes in Paykit.

**What the previous pin adopted.** The revision before moved the pin from `1259961` to `da130a3`, adopting:

- **Retries and refusals:** which answers let an app send the same bytes again and which are final for them (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.4); an effect the service refuses for good, or one that doesn't fit one batch, is stored locally, passed with the cursor and reported to the protocol, durably and possibly twice (Section 8.2), and a Paykit App therefore keeps a verified cancellation or `allowance_end` in its own state and stops on it, whether or not its record write succeeds (this spec's Sections 8.1 to 8.3); a send refused with `403 not_admitted` or `403 not_current` stays queued for at most 30 days (Section 11).
- **Cursor and receive keys:** the cursor only moves forward, so a batch sent again or replayed can't move it back (Sections 6.1 and 8.3); an app deletes a retired receive key by its own read position (Section 3.6).
- **Re-sends:** an unreadable item left by a departed app is asked for with the same floor as the other items, never for a time before the time of the cursor, less the clock tolerance (Section 8.2), as Section 9 and `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.12 now say.
- **Identities and certificates:** the service takes the signer's renewal for an app whose certificate has expired (Section 3.2); after a rollback alarm the signer releases nothing that relies on its newest version (Section 4.3); an app processes no message from an identity it has seen equivocate (Section 11); an app takes an unchecked erasure as one only after 24 hours, with an open question on the switch (Sections 12.7 and 19, question 25).
- **Provider changes:** kept batches after a move or a key change in place, kept attempts after a provider loss, and received effects kept past the 30-day batch window (Sections 12.5 and 12.6); after a provider loss an app uploads before any write but its registration (Section 12.6).
- **Wire format:** answer size bounds and a byte budget for feed and record pages (`max_page_bytes`, Switch wire 7.1 and 9.2), the binding of every other write's proof (Switch wire 6.5), the unpinned public read of `identity.json` (Switch wire 7.1 and 7.4.15), Paykit's scope roots in machine-readable form (`spec/reserved-roots.json`, Switch wire 3.1), and new rejection vectors (`operations.json` `shape_rejections`).

The pull request that made that revision lists every change it adopted and what it changed in Paykit.

**Design rule for implementations.** Each user action needs at most one call to each Pubky Switch involved (the counterparty's, for the message, and the identity's own, for claims and shared state), sent in parallel, before the user sees its result, plus local bookkeeping. Follow-up messages, such as the acceptance after an attempt, go in the background. The only other calls allowed are the background and parallel Homeserver calls listed in Section 14, and one re-send when the counterparty's inbox answers `stale_key` because a recipient app has just replaced its receive key or the recipient has added an app (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 3.6 and 8.1). A first message to a new counterparty also reads its directory, writes its conversation entry and may deliver the sender's `identity.json`, in one parallel round before the append (Section 14 and Pubky Switch spec, Section 7).

## Terms

Paykit uses the terms of the Pubky Switch spec (its Terms): identity, signer, app, `identity.json`, scope, Shared Data Key, inbox, feed, claim, shared record, operation and the rest. Paykit's own:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Paykit App** (app) | An app of the Switch acting for an identity in Paykit: one application with its own keys, certificate and Paykit scopes, at least one. Its certificate may also hold other protocols' scopes (Section 3.2). An app may run on several devices; Paykit treats it as one app (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.1). |
| **Version** | Besides the versions of the Switch, Private Payment Lists have versions of their own (Section 5). |
| **Billing Period** | One period of a recurring request, computed as in Section 8.2. |
| **Executor** | The one app that an Allowance record names, with the app's own signed grant, to pay automatically under that Allowance (Section 8.3). |
| **Paykit Profile** | Optional public display metadata of an identity at `/pub/paykit/profile.json`, with v0.1 semantics. |

## 1. Goals and product restrictions

- **Goals:**
  - Payments between Pubky identities; Pubky is the only identity layer required.
  - About one round trip per action, independent of contact count or history size.
  - Several apps per identity, isolated by scope, sharing only what their scopes allow.
  - Payment-method agnostic: the protocol never interprets rails, addresses, amounts' meaning, or proofs.
  - Feature agnostic: the switch offers generic primitives (claims and shared records). Features such as Allowances are built in apps, without changes to the switch.
  - An identity can move to another provider without the old operator's cooperation.
- **Product restriction:**
  - v0.2 has **no unsolicited first contact by Pubky ID alone**.
  - A sender must be on the recipient's allowlist: a contact, or a conversation the recipient started or invited, for example at a checkout.
  - Strangers can't message without an invite. An identity can hand out invite codes, for example a shop's QR code, and, where the user allows it, strangers can ask to connect (Pubky Switch spec, Section 7). Messaging without either is deferred.

## 2. Components

Paykit runs on the components of the Pubky Switch (Pubky Switch spec, Section 2): the signer, the identity's apps, the Homeserver, the Pubky Switch and the push relay.

- **A Paykit App** keeps its own keys, scopes, local data, outbox and cursor, as every app does, and its payment journal.
- **The Homeserver** also holds the records of Paykit apps with public payment endpoints, and the Paykit Profile (Section 4).

## 3. Scopes and authorization

### 3.1 Scopes

Paykit's scopes are names under the Switch's scope rule (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.3): a name, or a name and a sub-scope. Each Paykit App's certificate lists the Paykit scopes it holds:

| Scope | Allows | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| `pay` | Act as payer. Send acceptance, rejection, payer cancellation, proof, Private Payment List requests and resend requests. Receive requests, period details, payee cancellations, receipts, Private Payment Lists and resend requests. Read and write claims. Read and write `payer_activity` and `payment_lists` | `contacts` |
| `request` | Act as payee. Send requests, period details, payee cancellations, receipts, Private Payment Lists and resend requests. Receive acceptance, rejection, payer cancellations, proofs, Private Payment List requests and resend requests. Read and write `payee_activity` | - |
| `request:<label>` | Act as payee under one label: what `request` allows, with every request sent under the label, the payer's replies received only for that label's requests, and only that label's `payee_activity` records read and written | - |
| `allowance` | Send and receive `allowance_*` messages and resend requests for them; send `executor_notice` to an own executor; read and write `allowances`; read `allowance_ledgers` and claims | - |
| `allowance_execute` | Act as an Allowance executor; receive `executor_notice`; write `allowance_ledgers` | `allowance`, `pay`, `contacts` |
| `contacts` | Read the contacts, conversation entries and deny list at the switch | - |
| `manage_contacts` | Change the contacts and the deny list | `contacts` |
| `ext:<prefix>` | Send and receive the extension kinds `<prefix>.<name>` (Section 3.3) | - |

- A certificate lists every scope explicitly, including required ones. The signer never issues a scope without the scopes it requires. Paykit's readers, apps and payers, also refuse a certificate that lacks a scope another requires, or holds more than 8 extension scopes or a malformed one (such as `ext:paykit` or `ext:cap`, whose prefixes hold no `-`), or more than 8 labelled selling scopes; they ignore other names they don't know, which a later version may add, and other protocols' scopes (Section 3.2). The signer refuses an app request naming a scope it doesn't know (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 3.1 and Switch wire 3.9).
- **`pay` and `allowance_execute` require `contacts`,** so that every app that pays reads the deny list and pauses payment to a counterparty on it (Section 8.1). In v0.1 a block held for every paying app, since the apps of an identity shared one state (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Block A Peer"); a payer app that can't read the deny list would pay a counterparty its user blocked without telling the user. The cost is that every paying app can read the identity's contacts, conversation entries and deny list, as every v0.1 app could.
- **Extension scopes:** one scope `ext:<prefix>`, for example `ext:paykit-cap`, covers every message kind whose name starts with that prefix. It is Paykit's own: it has no keys, and its bound is Paykit's. An app narrows it to the kinds it handles with its record's `accepts` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.4), which it signs itself, so a new extension kind needs no new certificate and no prompt at the signer. A certificate holds at most 8 extension scopes. A prefix always holds a `-`, as `paykit-cap` does, so it is never a protocol's name, which is the prefix of that protocol's kinds and holds no `-` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.3): `paykit` is Paykit's name, and `ext:paykit` and `ext:cap` are malformed (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Sections 3.1 and 4.3).
- **Labelled selling scopes:** `request:<label>`, for example `request:shop1`, lets a hosted selling app, such as paykit-server, act as payee for its own sales only. The label is 1 to 32 characters of `a-z`, `0-9` and `-`, starting with a letter or digit, the syntax of a sub-scope (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.3), and, unlike an extension scope's prefix, needs no `-`; a certificate holds at most 8. Payers and the switch see the label, so it should be opaque, like `shop1`, never a name (Pubky Switch spec, Section 15). Labelled selling scopes are one use of the scope rule above: plain `request` covers every label, so an app holding it reads and writes every label's records and gets the payers' replies about every label's requests, and the identity's own wallets show all its sales. So a certificate holds `request` or labels, never both: the signer never issues one with both, it refuses an app request naming both, and every reader refuses such a certificate. A labelled app reads and writes only its label's `payee_activity` records (Section 6), under its label's keys (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.5), and gets the payers' replies only about its label's requests (Section 3.4). It has the switch permissions of `request`.

### 3.2 Switch permissions

- **Switch permissions** that each Paykit scope needs, for the certificate's `permissions` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2). This table is Paykit's: the switch enforces the signed list and never derives it. What each permission lets an app do is the Switch's (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2).

  | Switch permission | Given with |
  |---|---|
  | `claims.read` | `pay`, `allowance` |
  | `claims.write` | `pay` |
  | `lists.read` | `contacts` |
  | `lists.manage` | `manage_contacts` |
  | `conversation.add` | `pay`, `request`, any labelled selling scope, `allowance`, any extension scope |

- **Paykit's verifiers check the list.** The signer sets `permissions` from the scopes, using the table above for Paykit's and each other protocol's table for its own (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2). Paykit's verifiers, at apps and at payers, derive again from Paykit's scopes the list they need, and require every permission of it. When the certificate holds no other protocol's scope, they also require `permissions` to be exactly that list, and refuse a certificate whose `permissions` are any other list (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 3.1). A change to that table therefore needs a new certificate version (`v`).
- **Several protocols in one certificate.** A certificate may hold the scopes of other protocols beside Paykit's, so one app, such as a wallet that also chats, takes part in each of them under one App ID (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2). A scope's root is its name, the part before any `:` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.3). Paykit's roots are reserved for it: `pay`, `request`, `allowance`, `allowance_execute`, `contacts`, `manage_contacts` and `ext`, the names of Section 3.1. A scope with one of these roots is Paykit's, also one Paykit gives no meaning, such as `pay:x` or a plain `ext`, which it ignores and which needs no permission. A scope with any other root is another protocol's, such as `chat`. So:
  - Paykit's verifiers require every permission Paykit's scopes need, whatever the other protocols' scopes beside them; the list may then hold more, the other protocols' permissions.
  - Paykit's scope rules (Section 3.1) apply in full to its own scopes, and its bounds, 8 extension scopes and 8 labelled selling scopes, count only them.
  - Another protocol's scopes, and the switch permissions they bring, authorize nothing in Paykit: each Paykit message still needs the Paykit scope Section 3.3 names for it, and an app whose certificate holds no Paykit scope isn't a Paykit App and sends and receives no Paykit kind.

### 3.3 Message kind to scope

| Kind | Sender needs | Recipient app needs |
|---|---|---|
| `payment_request`, `payment_period_details`, `payment_receipt`, `private_payment_list` | `request` | `pay` |
| `payment_request_acceptance`, `payment_request_rejection`, `payment_proof`, `private_payment_list_request` | `pay` | `request` |
| `payment_request_cancellation` | `request` or `pay` | the other one |
| `allowance_proposal`, `allowance_acceptance`, `allowance_rejection`, `allowance_end` | `allowance` | `allowance` |
| `executor_notice` (own apps only) | `allowance` | `allowance_execute` |
| `resend_request` | Any: every current Paykit App of the counterparty, one holding at least one Paykit scope, may send it | Any Paykit App; the answer re-sends only kinds the requesting app's certificate receives, and a payer's replies only under the label rule of Section 3.4 (Section 9) |
| An extension kind `<prefix>.<name>` | `ext:<prefix>` | `ext:<prefix>` |

- **Labelled selling scopes** send and receive the kinds of `request`. A `payment_request` carries the signed `label` of the scope it is sent under, and recipients drop one whose sender holds neither `request` nor `request:<label>` for its label, and one without a label whose sender doesn't hold `request`. So an app holding only labelled scopes sends every request under one of its labels. The payer's acceptance, rejection, cancellation and proof copy the request's label (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.3).
- **Extension kinds** carry no Paykit payment semantics: no payment relies on one, they carry no destination, and no Paykit collection records one. An app that handles one passes it to its extension as it arrives; an app that doesn't know it ignores it, like any kind it doesn't know (Switch wire 5.1, check 10). Its body follows the JSON rules of every message, the integer rule included, so an extension writes a decimal such as a price as a string, outside the body's opaque `metadata` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.1; `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.3 and Switch wire 1.1).

### 3.4 Authorization rules

Paykit follows the Switch's authorization rules (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.4), and narrows the senders and recipients of a payer's replies by label:

- **Senders.** A payer sends its acceptance, rejection, cancellation and proof for a labelled request only to the payee's apps holding `request` or that label, and for an unlabelled request, or a payment without one, only to those holding `request`. So a hosted app never receives the payer's replies about the identity's own requests. A payee's cancellation carries no label and goes only to the payer's apps holding `pay`: a cancellation's recipient holds the other role than its sender (Section 3.3).
- **Recipients** also drop a payer's reply that the rule for senders above wouldn't have sent them: an acceptance, rejection, cancellation or proof with a label, unless they hold `request` or that label, and one without a label unless they hold `request`. A cancellation without a label from an app holding `request` or a labelled selling scope may be the payee's, and passes at an app holding `pay` instead; `pay` to `pay` and `request` to `request` are dropped.
- **Homeserver grants.** Besides the paths every app's grant covers, a Paykit App's grant may cover the Paykit Profile:
  - `/pub/paykit/profile.json` and `/pub/paykit/blobs/`, read and write, only for an app the user allows to edit the Paykit Profile. The blobs hold the Profile's files, such as an image its `image_uri` names, as in v0.1 (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Paykit Profile And Contacts").
- **From v0.1:** v0.1 apps hold `/pub/paykit/:rw` grants. Such a grant covers the Paykit Profile and its blobs and none of the paths above. Before the signer publishes an identity's first v0.2 `identity.json`, it revokes every such grant, or replaces it with one scoped as above. The paths above are those every app's grant covers (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.4) and the Paykit Profile with its blobs.

### 3.5 Keys of Paykit's scopes

- **Label keys.** A labelled selling scope `request:<label>` is a sub-scope of `request` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.5): its keys, the label keys, derive from the `request` key. Which scopes have keys is Paykit's: its scopes of Section 3.1 and their labels do, extension scopes don't, and nor does any other sub-scope of a Paykit scope (such as `pay:x`), which Paykit ignores (wire 3.1).
- Extension scopes have no scope keys: no shared record holds an extension kind, so key bundles and the approval carry keys for the other scopes only (Switch wire 3.5 and 3.10).

## 4. App records

A Paykit App's record is the Switch's app record (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.2). Its `accepts` lists Paykit's kinds, and its Paykit member, `protocols.paykit`, holds its `capabilities` and `public_endpoints` (wire 3.2). These rules are Paykit's, and the switch checks none of them:

- **Expiry:** a record with public endpoints carries a signed `expires_at`, chosen by the app, and Paykit's readers refuse one without it; at most 30 days ahead is recommended. The app publishes a renewed record before then, once less than half its lifetime remains, and the same rule applies to the `expires_at` of the Private Payment Lists it sends (Sections 5 and 8.4). Payers refuse public endpoints from an expired record. So an identity whose publishing app doesn't run for longer than its record's lifetime, about a month at the recommended expiry, can't be paid publicly (tips, a profile's pay button) until the app runs again; v0.1's endpoint files had no expiry. A record without public endpoints may set it to null.
- **Record age limit:** the app key signs `expires_at`, so a stolen app key could sign a record that never expires. Each identity therefore sets, in its `identity.json`, the longest time payers trust one of its app records after it was signed (`record_max_age`, Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3; 90 days when absent). A payer uses a record's public endpoints only until the earlier of its `expires_at` and its `updated_at` plus that limit, and not at all when its `updated_at` is more than 5 minutes ahead of the payer's clock. The signer signs `identity.json`, so a stolen app key can't extend the limit. The app renews its record within the limit too. An identity whose publishing app doesn't run for longer than the limit has no payable public endpoints until it does.
- **`capabilities.endpoint_identifiers`:** the Payment Endpoint Identifiers the app can pay to, so payees can offer matching destinations.
- **Public payments are optional.** An app may leave `public_endpoints` empty. The identity's public Payment List is the `public_endpoints` of every current app, each listed under its app; payers may use any of them. An identity whose records list none has no public Payment List.
- **Public endpoints are read from the Homeserver.** They are public, non-interactive data: anyone reads them without authentication or the switch, and they stay readable while the switch is down or the identity is moving to another provider. A payer also compares the Homeserver's `identity.json` with the switch's copy, in parallel and waiting on it at most a short grace, and pays without the switch's answer only as Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3 allows.
- **Payers check every destination's app** before paying (Section 7.2): the app that published a public endpoint, or sent the request, period details or Private Payment List, must be current in its identity's `identity.json` with the certificate it presented (listed, or renewed and still counting, Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2); that certificate must allow `request`, and a listed certificate must not have expired. The record or Private Payment List carrying the destination must not have expired either (Section 5). A removed app's destinations are ignored, even if its record is still on the Homeserver.
- **Withdrawing a destination:** stale copies of a record or a Private Payment List can't be recalled before they expire. A payee whose destination is compromised therefore removes it and also revokes the app, or replaces it with a new app (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.2), so that payers stop paying to it once their 10-minute copy of `identity.json` shows the app removed.
- **What is public:** an app publishes its record on the Homeserver when it has public endpoints, or when another protocol it takes part in needs public data (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.2). Anyone can read a published record whole: its accepted kinds, its label, its certificate with its scopes and permissions, and its Paykit member with the app's capabilities, also when its `public_endpoints` is empty. An app that publishes no record on the Homeserver appears publicly only as an app ID and certificate hash in `identity.json`, so anyone can see how many apps an identity has, but not what they are. Admitted senders get the full records from the directory.
- **Fresh before paying:** an app fetches or revalidates its own identity's `identity.json` in parallel with each `attempt`, unless it did so in the last 60 s. It moves money only when it holds such a copy and that copy names the service whose key signed the attempt's transition proof (Section 7.2); a payment made while its own switch doesn't answer, which has no attempt yet, needs a copy naming the service the app uses (Section 8.4). If the Homeserver is unreachable for longer, the app makes no new attempts. An open event stream doesn't replace this check, because a stalled stream looks the same as a quiet one.
- **The Paykit Profile** is optional public display metadata of the identity, at `/pub/paykit/profile.json` on the Homeserver, written by the signer or an app the user authorizes (Section 3.4).
- **App storage.** Besides what every app keeps (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.1), a Paykit App keeps Paykit's state: derived state such as Allowance budgets, its payment journal and the Private Payment Lists it publishes.

## 5. Message kinds

Kind strings are `paykit.` followed by the names below, or extension kinds `<prefix>.<name>` under an extension scope, which carry no Paykit payment semantics (Section 3.3). Message bodies use the field definitions of the v0.1 SDK specs at commit `53b8c8cb2735c0845232fbdf34bc83052bf88404`: `specs/payment-requests.md` for requests, recurrence, Billing Periods, acceptance, rejection, cancellation, proofs and receipts; `specs/payment-conversion.md` for conversion terms, quotes and deadlines; `specs/allowances.md` for Allowance terms and messages; and `specs/paykit-sdk.md` for Private Payment List versioning. Those files title their protocol "Payment Request Protocol v0.2"; here they are the v0.1 bodies. This spec defines transport, authorization, storage and lifecycle, and changes the bodies as listed below. The exact v0.2 bodies are in `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.

| Kind | Notes |
|---|---|
| `payment_request` | Carries its amount and `payment_reference`, and usually its destination (`payment_endpoints`), which the payer must then use; without one, the payer pays to any of the payee's current endpoints, or only those of the payee app named in `required_app_id` (Section 8.1). A request carries at most one of `payment_endpoints` and `required_app_id`. Optional: proposal expiry, `recurrence`, conversion terms, payment deadline, and `per_period_endpoints` for a recurring request (Section 8.2). |
| `payment_request_acceptance` | One-time request: sent once the attempt is confirmed (Section 7.2), while the payment runs; not awaited. Recurring request: sent once, when the user, or an executor under an Allowance, accepts the subscription, before any payment, and recorded first (Section 8.2). The proposal expiry bounds the acceptance. |
| `payment_request_rejection` | Recorded before it is sent (Section 8.1). |
| `payment_request_cancellation` | From either side. A payer records its cancellation before sending it (Section 8.1). |
| `payment_period_details` | New in v0.2; replaces v0.1's conversion quote. The payee's details for one Billing Period of a recurring request: its `payment_endpoints`, its conversion rates (cross-asset only, as v0.1 quotes), or both, with an `expires_at`. Its rates apply to a payment whose verified payment time is from its `created_at`, cut to whole seconds, through `expires_at`, as a v0.1 quote's do from its `valid_from`. Each stays valid until its own `expires_at`, and the proof references the one it used (Section 8.2). |
| `payment_proof` | References the request and Billing Period, or none for a payment without a request, and the period details or Private Payment List version it paid to. Carries the `attempt_id` it reports and an opaque proof. Required after paying a single-use endpoint (Section 8.4). |
| `payment_receipt` | Optional; signed by the payee. References the proof it confirms, and so the attempt. May carry the payee's `metadata`, and, for a payment without a request, a `payment_reference`, as in v0.1. |
| `resend_request` | Asks the counterparty to re-send messages by `msg_id`, request, Billing Period, or a range of acceptance times. Best effort. |
| `private_payment_list` | Latest state per publishing app. Its version is the publishing time in milliseconds, and the app always sends a higher one than before, even after a restore. It carries a required `expires_at`, which the publishing app computes from its current time, not from the version. Payers use the list only until the earlier of `expires_at` and 30 days after their inbox accepted it, so a stolen app key can't sign a list payable for long, and refuse it after that. Endpoints are reusable unless marked `single_use` (Section 8.4). |
| `private_payment_list_request` | Asks for the current list and names the version the sender holds. Best effort. |
| `allowance_proposal`, `allowance_acceptance`, `allowance_rejection`, `allowance_end` | The acceptance names the executor app at acceptance, for information; a later handoff doesn't change it. |
| `executor_notice` | New in v0.2, between apps of one identity only. Tells an executor that an Allowance ended (`reason: ended`) or was handed to another app (`reason: handed_off`), naming the Allowance's record key. Its only effect is on the recipient's consent list (Section 8.3). |

- **Differences from the v0.1 bodies and rules:**
  - `required_app_id` is optional and only allowed without `payment_endpoints`, whose owner is always the sending app (`from.app_id`).
  - Dropped: `event_id` and `payment_request_id`, replaced by `msg_id` and `refs`; Encrypted Links, Receipt Access and Delivery Confirmations; the 1000-byte limit; and FIFO message order, which Section 8.1 replaces.
  - `per_period_endpoints`, `payment_period_details` (which replaces the conversion quote and can also carry a period's destination) and `executor_notice` are new. So is `label`: a request sent under a labelled selling scope carries it, and the payer's acceptance, rejection, cancellation and proof copy it (Section 3.3). Private Payment List endpoints may carry `single_use: true`, and a list carries `expires_at`.
  - Billing Periods follow one rule, the anchored period rule of `specs/allowances.md` (Section 8.2), where v0.1 left expansion open.
  - Readers ignore unknown fields in message bodies (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.1), where v0.1 rejected them.
- **Size:** the JSON of each kind stored in activity records (Section 6) is at most 16 KiB.
- There is no refund message; a refund is a normal Payment Request in the other direction.

## 6. Paykit shared records

| Collection | One record per | Contents | `read_scope` | `write_scopes` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `payer_activity` | Request received; for a recurring request also each Billing Period; or payment made without a request | **Request record:** the request, acceptance, rejection, cancellations and the request's status, and for a recurring request the Billing Periods that may have an open attempt (Section 8.2). **Period record:** the period details, proof sent, receipt received and the period's status. A one-time request has only a request record, holding both | `pay` | `pay` |
| `payee_activity` | Request sent; for a recurring request also each Billing Period; or payment received without a request | The same, from the payee's side | `request`, or `request:<label>` for a request sent under that label | `request`, or `request:<label>` |
| `payment_lists` | Payee and publishing app | The latest Private Payment List received and the single-use endpoints used | `pay` | `pay` |
| `allowances` | Allowance, on either side | Proposal, accepted terms, the executor's grant and every grant it has held, status, end or rejection (Section 8.3) | `allowance` | `allowance` |
| `allowance_ledgers` | Allowance | Its ledger entries and running totals (Section 8.3) | `allowance` | `allowance_execute` |

- **Fixed policies:** each collection's records use the policy above. A request sent under a label has its `payee_activity` records, its request record and for a recurring request its period records, under that label's policy: `read_scope = request:<label>`, `write_scopes = ["request:<label>"]`. Apps ignore any record in these collections whose policy differs, and a labelled one whose request, or payer's message, names another label or none, and a fixed one whose request names a label, so no app can plant a record readable under another scope or another label.
- **One message per role:** an activity record stores the messages that make up its state, one per role. A request record holds the request, the payer's response (acceptance or rejection) and a cancellation from either side; a period record holds the period details, the proof and the receipt; a one-time request's record holds the request's roles and its proof and receipt; a record of a payment without a request holds its proof and receipt. A later message for a role already held is still processed when it arrives, for example a second proof, which shows a duplicate payment, but isn't stored. A record of a payment without a request is keyed by its settlement (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3), so a second proof there names the same settlement: a replay, not a second payment, and credited once (Section 8.4). The one exception is period details: a period record keeps every details it has held for its period, at most 8 (Section 8.2). Allowance records hold their proposal, response and end the same way (Section 8.3). The kinds activity records store are at most 16 KiB (Section 5), and a record lists only a bounded number of `msg_id`s (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11), so an activity record stays within the 256 KiB limit for a shared record value without any message count, however many messages repeat a role it holds.
- **Deleting finished activity:** an app may delete an activity record (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3) once the user no longer wants it, with one exception: a payer never deletes its record of a paid obligation without a deadline (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2), one that carries the proof of its payment. The user may archive that record, which hides it in the app, but the record stays: the claim is gone 400 days after its last entry (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3), and from then on the record, not the claim, stops a second payment of a re-sent copy (Section 7.2). It stays whole, since a reader ignores a write that drops a message it has seen (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2). The cost is one record per payment without a deadline, kept for the identity's life. A record of an obligation with a deadline may go: its claim, or its tombstone, still shows the payment as done until 30 days after the deadline, after which a later copy can't be paid anyway (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3). A period record's deadline comes from its request, so the payer keeps a recurring request's record while it keeps any of the request's period records; a period record whose request record is missing counts as one without a deadline.
- Collection IDs and record keys are derived as in Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3. Values are sealed under the read scope's key, a label key for a labelled policy (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 3.5 and 13.4).
- **Destinations come only from the payee.** An app pays only to endpoints in a verified message from the payee's current app, or to the payee's public endpoints (Section 7.2). No own app can redirect a payment by writing shared state; at most it can hide records, replay older messages from the same counterparty to an app that never saw newer ones, or act within its own scopes.

## 7. Paying

### 7.1 Claims of payments

- **Obligation IDs.** Apps derive a request's obligation ID from the payee, the request and the Billing Period's start, blinded with the naming key, and a payment without a request's from its attempt ID (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3), so they all find the same claim; the service treats the claim key as opaque.
- **A second wallet** that writes `attempt` while another attempt is open gets `409 conflict` with the current state, and shows "being handled by your other wallet".
- **Deadlines:** an attempt on a one-time request carries `deadline: d + 1 ms`, where `d` is the earlier of its proposal expiry and its payment deadline. An attempt on a Billing Period carries it with `d` the period's start plus the payment deadline's `seconds`. A request with neither carries no `deadline`. `d` is taken at millisecond precision, the precision of every time comparison (Switch wire 1.6). The service rejects an `attempt` once its clock reaches the `deadline`, so `d` is the last instant at which it takes one. The first `attempt` on a claim pins its deadline, including none: the service refuses a later `attempt` that names another (`409 conflict`), and the claim view shows the pinned deadline, so apps can compare it with their own. The service thus bounds, on its own clock, when an attempt may start; the payee still checks v0.1's inclusive deadline against the verified payment time, read as for the rates of period details (Section 8.2). The proposal expiry of a recurring request bounds only its acceptance (Section 8.2).
  - **Not before the request:** the payee also bounds the verified payment time from below, as in v0.1 (`specs/payment-requests.md`, "Payment time and attribution"). Evidence whose verified payment time is earlier than the time the payee recorded its own request, by its own clock, less a tolerance its payment method sets (`specs/payment-conversion.md`, "Payment deadlines"), doesn't prove the request: the payee holds or flags it and doesn't mark the request, or the Billing Period, paid on it alone. The funds still count as received.
  - An app makes no attempt once the deadline is at or before the highest service time it has verified (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1). A transition proof that stamps an attempt at or after its deadline is misbehaviour: the app pays nothing, writes `failed` and makes no new attempt through that service until the user resolves it, as for a rollback (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1).
  - Each app computes the deadline itself, from the request, and keeps evidence for an obligation, a SETTLED proof or the signed answer that stands for one (Section 7.2, Kept proofs), at least until the later of the pinned deadline and its own, plus the 30-day reconciliation window during which the service keeps the tombstone, and longer for a claim pinned to "none" (Section 7.2 and Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3), so it can still compare, upload and repair it. A pin earlier than the app's own deadline, or any pin where the app computes none, is an alert, and the obligation is doubtful: the tombstone may already be gone while the payment it records still counts. A later pin is a conformance error. Either way the service refuses this app's own `attempt`, which names its own deadline.
- **No automatic payment of doubtful claims:** a claim with an `unknown` or orphaned attempt, or whose next attempt would be flagged `possibly_duplicate` (row 1), is attempted again only by a user who has seen "may already be paid", never automatically.

### 7.2 Paying order

Every payment follows these steps, whether it pays a one-time request, a Billing Period, a request under an Allowance or nothing requested (Section 8.4). The one exception is a payment without a request to a reusable public endpoint while the payer's own switch doesn't answer, which writes step 1's `attempt` after paying (Section 8.4, Without the switch).

1. **Write `attempt`**, conditioned on the version of the request record the app read (Section 6), so that a rejection or cancellation recorded since makes it fail, and on the deadline, if any (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2).
   - **Check the transfer first,** before any write. The payment method reports the asset, the amount and the largest fee it will transfer to the destination. The amount must equal the amount being paid: the request's, the period details', or the accepted quote's. A destination without an amount, such as an address or an amount-less invoice or offer, is paid exactly that amount. A destination that fixes another amount or asset, or whose amount the method can't tell before paying, is unusable for automatic payment; a manual payment shows the amount it would transfer and pays it only on the user's explicit choice. Fees don't count against Allowance limits, which count the amount paid, as in v0.1. A fee the payee can influence through the destination it chose must stay within a cap the payer app sets; above it, the destination is unusable for automatic payment, and a manual payment needs the user's choice.
   - **Reserve a single-use endpoint** in the same batch: when the destination is a single-use endpoint of a Private Payment List, the batch also writes the `payment_lists` record marking it used, at its next version (Section 8.4), whatever obligation is paid. If that write fails its version check, nothing is written: the app re-reads the record, and pays again to the next usable endpoint or asks for a newer list. An executor's batch adds its ledger and activity writes (Section 8.3), a payment without a request its activity record (Section 8.4), and an attempt on a Billing Period the request record doesn't list as attempted the write of the request record that adds it (Section 8.2).

   In parallel with the batch:
   - **check the destination:** the message or record carrying the destination verifies as in Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2, carries the destination, and hasn't expired: a record at its `expires_at` or its `updated_at` plus the payee identity's record age limit, a Private Payment List at its `expires_at` or 30 days after the payer's inbox accepted it (Section 5 and Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.2); a record also names the app whose path it was read from. A request or period details carrying it name the obligation being paid by their signed fields: the request is the one paid, and period details name it first in `refs` and the Billing Period paid; a Private Payment List isn't tied to one obligation. The app that sent the request, period details or Private Payment List, or published the public endpoint, is current in the payee's `identity.json`, cached for at most 10 minutes (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 4.2 and 4.3), and, for a public endpoint, the higher of the Homeserver copy and the payee's switch's copy when the payer had to fetch it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3). If that lookup failed or hasn't answered, a manual payment needs the user's explicit confirmation before any write, an automatic one isn't made, and an answer that arrives before money moves is still used;
   - revalidate the app's own `identity.json` if needed (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3).

   All three must succeed before any money moves. If any of them fails, the app writes `failed` instead of paying. An executor checks its Allowance again at this point (Section 8.3), and a payment priced by period details' rates checks that they are still usable (Section 8.2). An automatic payment isn't attempted at all while the device clock and the service's differ by more than the clock tolerance (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1, Clock bound).

   **Dispatch window.** The owner sets the attempt's `orphan_after` to at least the longest its payment method can take to complete or fail, plus the time it allows itself between the `attempt` and the dispatch (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2). It dispatches only while that allowance hasn't passed since the service accepted the `attempt` and its own certificate hasn't expired, judged with no wait between the judgment and the dispatch; past it, it submits nothing and writes `failed`. It repeats an idempotent dispatch only while the method's longest time still fits before the orphan age. The owner's own revocation inside the allowance isn't seen without a read; the user's confirmation that Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2 rows 5 and 6 require before another app resolves the attempt, and the `possibly_duplicate` flag (Section 7.3), cover it.
2. **Pay.** For a one-time request, send the acceptance at the same time, without waiting for it. The acceptance becomes sendable only once the payment method's call is made, a resume finds the payment in the method's history, or the user marks as paid (`done`) a payment that may have been dispatched: never while a check before the dispatch can still stop it, and never for a payment the method reported failed, so a payee never sees an acceptance of a payment that never ran.
3. **Write `done`** (basis `owner`) and update the activity record, in one batch. If the payment failed, write `failed` instead.
4. **Send the proof** to the payee.

- **Kept proofs:** before attempting, an app compares the claim with any SETTLED proof it keeps for that claim (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3). That proof is the transition proof of the entry that settled the claim, from the answer to its own write or a feed item that carried it (Switch wire 7.5). An app that got no such proof, such as one that first sees the claim once its entries are stubs, keeps instead the signed answer of its read showing the claim SETTLED (Switch wire 7.1 and 7.4.8), and compares and keeps that the same way. A service that shows FREE, or nothing, for a claim the app holds a signed SETTLED proof for is treated as a rollback (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1): the app makes no attempt through it and alerts the user. The one exception is a tombstone the service may already have deleted (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3): from its pinned deadline plus the 30-day reconciliation window, or, for a claim pinned to "none", from 400 days after the proof's entry, the time the switch keeps that tombstone after its last entry, which is no earlier. The app keeps the proof until the later of the bounds that apply: the later of the pinned deadline and its own, where there is one, plus the window; and, for a pin to "none", the 400 days. A pinned deadline where the app computes none (its request sets no deadline) is an early pin, and the app keeps that proof for good, since it may still pay the obligation. While the service may have deleted the tombstone but the app still keeps the proof, the pin differs from the app's own deadline (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2), early or "none" beside a deadline, so the app alerts and still treats the obligation as paid. A bound has passed, and the app may drop the proof, only when it lies before the earlier of the highest verified service time and the device clock plus 24 hours, so one far-future signed time can't make the app drop its evidence. Until then the app keeps it, compares it with the claim, and uploads it in a move or after a provider loss (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 12.5 and 12.6), when a faulty old service may have left the tombstone out.
- **The record shows it paid:** an app never attempts an obligation whose activity record it holds already carries the proof of a payment, whatever the claim shows. A claim without a deadline is gone 400 days after its last entry (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3), and then the record, not the claim, stops a second payment, for example of a re-sent copy of the request. So the payer never deletes the record of a paid obligation without a deadline; the user may only archive it (Section 6).
- The **attempt ID** is random. It is recorded in the app's payment journal before the write, and used as the payment method's idempotency key where the method supports one.
- **Only a verified answer lets money move.** If the answer to the `attempt` is lost or its service signature doesn't verify, the app doesn't pay; it re-reads the claim and continues from what a verified answer shows. The same holds for an answer whose transition proofs don't bind the batch: one proof per operation, each naming its operation by its stub's item hash, and its object (Switch wire 6.5). Before paying, the attempt's proof must also show the app's own attempt: the claim ATTEMPTING, with this app as owner and the attempt ID it wrote current and open, at the step after the one it expected. Otherwise the app pays nothing and writes `failed`. A signed answer that fails these checks is misbehaviour, treated as a rollback (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1).
- **Starting to pay** is recorded in the journal before the payment begins. After a crash, an app whose journal shows no payment started writes `failed`; otherwise it resolves the attempt from the payment method's history (Section 7.3). This crash rule holds only for the app's current journal. A journal restored from a backup may predate a payment made after the backup, so a restored app treats its open attempts as Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4 says.
- **What a restore loses.** A restore from a backup brings back the app's own state as it was at the backup (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4): what the app kept only in its own state since is gone, and so are the entries it passed since. The Switch recovers every lasting effect from claims and shared records, but a lasting effect kept only in the app's own state is in neither, such as a stop kept from a verified `payment_request_cancellation` or `allowance_end` whose record write had failed or was still pending (Sections 8.1 to 8.3). A restored app can't tell whether it passed entries after its backup: cursor moves aren't in the chain (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.3), and the service doesn't tell an app the cursor it holds for it. So after any restore from a backup, the app treats every open request and every Allowance as possibly stopped: it makes no automatic attempt on such a request or under such an Allowance, and a manual attempt only after its user has seen that the payee may have cancelled it. It sends each counterparty of an open request or Allowance a `resend_request` with `accepted_from` at the `accepted_at` of the last entry its backup's cursor passed, less the clock tolerance, and keeps a cancellation or end that comes back as Sections 8.1 to 8.3 say. The hold on a request or an Allowance ends when its record shows it cancelled or ended, which stops it for good and which no user confirmation overrides, or when the user confirms it. A counterparty that never answers leaves the hold in place until the user confirms; nothing releases it automatically. A stop the backup holds still applies, and no confirmation overrides it either: the hold is for stops that may have come after the backup. The rule binds a restore the app knows of, because the app made it; a store rolled back without the app knowing, such as a device image restored underneath it, is detected by nothing in the Pubky Switch this spec builds on (Section 17, question 28).
- **The restored certificate.** A Paykit App checks every certificate it runs under by Paykit's own scope and permission rules (Sections 3.1 and 3.2), which are stricter than the Switch's. At a restore it checks its backup's certificate before the restore writes anything, and doesn't restore from a backup whose certificate fails them. A certificate it takes afterwards, such as the renewed one its `identity.json` lists (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4), it checks before it acts under it, and it takes no Paykit action under a certificate that fails them: it sends nothing, pays nothing, and holds the entries it receives, unprocessed, until a certificate that passes replaces it. At a join, it checks the approval's certificate before it keeps anything.
- **Failed means definitively not paid.** Once a payment may have been submitted to the payment method, the owner MUST NOT write `failed` without authoritative evidence that it did not and cannot settle. A timeout, a disconnection, a crash or an ambiguous result MUST keep the attempt open: it blocks any replacement payment, keeps its Allowance reservation, and is resolved only through Section 7.3. As in the v0.1 SDK, the owner reports one of three outcomes: definitive failure, verified success, or nothing yet.
- **No second execution.** An app never carries out an attempt a second time, after a crash or otherwise, unless the payment method's idempotency on the attempt ID makes a repeat harmless or the journal shows the payment never started. When the method's history shows any record of the attempt, the app resolves the attempt from that record and never carries it out again, idempotent or not.
- **What a payment method declares:** each integration states whether it is idempotent on the attempt ID, and for how long it remembers the key; whether its history can show the outcome of a dispatch, and for how long it keeps it; and whether that history is authoritative across a restore of the paying device, because it lives with the account and not only on the device. An empty history means the payment never started only within the history's retention, counted from when the attempt was journaled, and, after a restore, only for a history that is authoritative across one. A dispatch is repeated only on an idempotent method, within its retention, and never from a restored journal. Otherwise a doubtful dispatch stays open, "may be paid", and is resolved only by a receipt or by the user. An owner that finds a payment it reported failed completed after all sends its proof, so the payee's receipt can correct the claim. The service keeps the claim for 30 days after its deadline (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3); a receipt after that can't be recorded on it, and the app that gets it shows the user that a payment marked failed was paid. It still records the receipt in its activity record (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.11). It takes the claim as gone once the switch holds none, or once the claim's pinned deadline plus 30 days is at or before the highest service time it has verified.
- **Doubtful window:** only a crash between paying and writing `done`, an outcome the method can't report, or a restore whose payment history can't tell (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4), leaves doubt. It always shows as "may be paid".

### 7.3 Reconciliation

- **Orphaned attempts** (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2) stay open until reconciled.
- **Resolutions:**

| Resolution | Who | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `done`, basis `evidence` (row 3) | Any own app with `claims.write` | The attempt the evidence names is done; claim SETTLED |
| `done`, basis `reconcile` (row 4) | Any own app with `claims.write`, user-confirmed, orphaned attempt | Attempt done; claim SETTLED |
| `unknown` (row 6) | Any own app with `claims.write`, user-confirmed, orphaned attempt | Attempt unknown; claim FREE; the next attempt is flagged `possibly_duplicate` |
| `failed` on an `unknown` attempt, or on an orphaned open attempt whose owner isn't current (row 5) | Its owner while current; otherwise any own app with `claims.write`, user-confirmed | Attempt failed, so a budget reservation on it is released; from a non-owner, the next attempt is flagged `possibly_duplicate` |
| The owner's later report (rows 2 and 5) | Owner of an `unknown` attempt, while current | Done or failed, as reported |

- **Only the payee's yes counts.** A receipt can settle an attempt, because the payee confirms it was paid. Nothing from the payee shows that an attempt was not paid: a payee gains from a second payment, and may not have seen the first one yet. A reminder, a re-sent request or a `resend_request` is no evidence either way.
- Features that account for payments, such as Allowance budgets (Section 8.3), derive their accounting from the outcome of each attempt.
- **An app that isn't current can write nothing,** not even outcomes. Its attempts are reconciled through receipts or by other apps.
- **Graceful removal:** the signer warns before revoking an app, or letting its certificate lapse (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2), while it still owns open attempts, which `GET /v1/{id}/apps` lists. Apps with `allowance` warn when an Allowance's executor stops being current (Section 8.3).

## 8. Payment flows and features

### 8.1 One-time request

1. The payee sends `payment_request`, usually with its destination, admitted by the payer's allowlist, for example after an invite, and writes its `payee_activity` request record in parallel.
2. The payer's apps with `pay` receive it, with a push. The first to process it writes the `payer_activity` request record.
3. One app pays, following Section 7.2.
4. The payee verifies the proof by its own method, updates its `payee_activity` record to paid, and may send a `payment_receipt`.

- **Destination:** a request with `payment_endpoints` is paid only to those. One with `required_app_id` instead is paid only to that payee app's Private Payment List or public endpoints, and that app must be current; a request carrying both fields is invalid. One with neither, for example from an app that receives no payments itself, is paid to any of the payee's current Private Payment Lists or public endpoints, under the rules of Section 8.4, including single-use reservation and the explicit choice between private and public payment; the proof names the endpoint it used.
- **Rejecting and cancelling:** a payer app records its rejection or cancellation in the request record, in a batch conditioned on the request's claim having no open or paid attempt (`claim_step`), and sends it only after that batch succeeds. An attempt is conditioned on the request record (Section 7.2), so one own app can't reject a request while another pays it. A recurring request is answered and cancelled only as Section 8.2 says, conditioned on its Billing Periods' claims, never on a one-time obligation.
- **Blocked counterparties:** blocking pauses what is open with a counterparty and revokes nothing, as in v0.1 (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Block A Peer"). While the deny list holds a counterparty (Pubky Switch spec, Section 7), a payer app, which always holds `contacts` (Section 3.1), makes no automatic payment or acceptance for it, and pays its open requests and Billing Periods, and makes a payment without a request to it, only after its user confirms, having shown the user that the counterparty is blocked. A resume's repeat of a dispatched payment (Section 7.2, What a payment method declares) is an automatic payment too: the user's confirmation was for the first dispatch, so the app makes no repeat while the deny list holds the payee, and the attempt stays open. Unblocking ends the pause.
- **How fresh the deny list is:** for a payment the user starts, with a request or without one, the app judges the deny list as it read it at most 10 minutes before, the age Section 7.2 allows its copy of the payee's `identity.json` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3). Only when its copy is older, or it holds none, does it read the lists first. For an automatic payment, a resume's repeat of a dispatched payment or an executor's payment under an Allowance (Section 8.3), it reads the deny list again before each dispatch. A block or an unblock the app wrote itself counts at once. A read that fails never counts as one that doesn't hold the counterparty: the app makes no payment to it on that read.
- **Labelled requests:** a hosted app holding `request:<label>` sends every request under its label and writes its records under that label's policy; the period details, receipts and cancellations of the request come from it as from any payee app. The payer's replies go to it and to the identity's apps holding `request`, which see the sale too (Section 3.1 and Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.4).
- **Messages that cross:** a payee that receives a valid proof for an attempt treats it as payment, even if it cancelled the request meanwhile; it may refund with a new request in the other direction. A payer that has recorded a payee's cancellation makes no new attempt, and one already attempting finishes and sends its proof. A payer app that has verified a payee's `payment_request_cancellation` MUST keep it durably in its own state before it does anything else with it: for a message it receives, in the commit that passes the entry, with no service operation in that step. That kept copy is the stop, not the request record: from then on the app MUST make no new attempt for that request, and a restart, or a record write that failed or is still pending, never lifts it. The app records the cancellation in the request record by a later write of its own as soon as it can; until then the identity's other apps may not know of it, the same exposure as a cancellation not yet delivered.
- **Replies before their record:** a payer's acceptance, rejection, cancellation or proof can reach a payee app before it holds the record of the request the reply names, for example a request another own app sent whose record this app hasn't read yet. The app keeps such a reply in its own state, under the spam rule, as a message that creates an activity record (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1), and places it in the record by a later write of its own once it holds the record. It keeps a reply at most 30 days from its acceptance, judged on the service time it verified, the time its sender keeps it (Section 9), and at most 64 such replies per sender identity, the identity its certificate names, and 128 in all, the counts by which Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2 bounds the entries of a sender whose standing couldn't be checked. Past a sender's 64, the app drops that sender's reply accepted earliest; past 128 in all, the reply accepted earliest of the sender identity that holds the most such replies, and among sender identities holding the same most, the lower sender identity. A reply dropped for these bounds is dropped without being processed, so a later re-send of the same message is taken. For each sender identity whose replies it dropped for these bounds, the app keeps one mark holding two times: the earliest acceptance time among those replies, which a re-send asks from, and the latest, which the mark expires by. A re-sent copy dropped again counts with its new acceptance time; a sender re-sends only messages it first accepted in the last 30 days (Section 9), so such copies move the latest time to at most 30 days after their first acceptance, and a mark that only re-sent copies feed ends at most 60 days after the replies it covers were first accepted. Once the app holds the record of a request whose counterparty holds a mark, it asks that sender to re-send what its inbox accepted from the mark's earliest time (`resend_request` with `accepted_from`, `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.12), and asks again at most once in any 24 hours (the Paykit SDK's interval) while the mark lives: a sender may leave selected messages out for its own limits, and its requester may ask again later (Section 9). A `resend_request` that doesn't reach the sender's inbox, refused or not yet delivered, counts as not sent: the app tries again at its next receive, and the 24 hours count from the last request its outbox delivered. Asking doesn't change the mark, and the re-send may leave out replies the sender no longer keeps, for which nothing is owed. A re-sent reply is taken as any reply: placed if the app now holds its record, kept or dropped again by these bounds if it doesn't, and not written again if its record already holds it (Stored entries, below), so a reply is never applied twice. A mark goes 30 days after its latest time, when the sender no longer keeps any reply it covers (Section 9), or, past 128 marks, when it is the mark whose latest time is earliest (on a tie, the one of the lower sender identity). A mark that goes by these bounds leaves nothing to ask for. A backup carries the marks with their times, so a restore neither loses nor resets the marks its backup held; a mark made after the backup goes with what the restore loses (Section 7.2), whose own requests for re-sends ask for those replies again. So a sender sees only ordinary `resend_request`s from each payee app, at most one in any 24 hours, while the app keeps dropping its replies and for 30 days after: an admitted sender can make a payee app send it no more than that.
- **Stored entries:** an app stores an entry locally, moves its cursor past it and tells Paykit when the service refuses its effect for good, or the effect doesn't fit one batch or the app's store, or the effect's fate couldn't be known, and when it drops an entry whose sender's standing couldn't be checked (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2). Paykit keeps the message of an entry whose effect was refused, or whose fate couldn't be known, and writes it into its record by a later write of its own as soon as the record can take it: a request into the payer's request record, a payer's reply into the payee's record of the request, a proof of a payment without a request into the payee's record of its settlement, which the write may open (Section 8.4), and a receipt into the payer's record of the payment. A Private Payment List is held until it is written, a newer list from its publishing app replacing an older one. The app never writes over a record value it can't read or open, which may hold what another own app stored. It tries for 30 days from the message's acceptance, the time its sender keeps it (Section 9), and may then give the message up; either way the message stays processed. The same later write compensates for an effect whose fate couldn't be known, since a record that already holds the message isn't written again, and once the app has written it, it declines a released copy of the entry (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2), so the effect isn't applied twice. For entries dropped unverified, it keeps a mark of their own per sender identity, with the same two times, bounds, expiry, backup and cadence as a dropped reply's (Replies before their record): it asks that sender at once to re-send what its inbox accepted from the mark's earliest time to its latest (`accepted_from` and `accepted_until`, `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.12), and again at most once in any 24 hours while the mark lives, and a message it already holds isn't applied twice.
- **Records not listed yet:** an app's copy of the shared records lacks records until it has listed them: from the moment it joins, after a restore, and after it takes a certificate that reads collections, or members of one, that the certificate it last listed under, or any certificate it held since, didn't, until it holds that certificate's scope keys and has listed them (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 3.5 and 8.3). Until then a record's absence from the copy decides nothing about a collection not yet listed. A received message of a kind that is recorded (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.3), whose effect is written into a record of such a collection or decided from whether the app holds one, is held unprocessed, as the store-retry hold holds an entry (Section 17, question 27), and is read again once the records are listed, so it is neither lost nor taken as new state. Today these are a payment request, period details, a payer's acceptance, rejection or cancellation, a payee's cancellation, a proof, a receipt, a Private Payment List, and an Allowance proposal, acceptance, rejection or end, whose records are in `allowances`; the rule holds as well for any kind recorded later.

### 8.2 Recurring request

- **Billing Periods** follow one rule, the anchored period rule of `specs/allowances.md` applied to the request's `recurrence`. Boundary `k` is `anchor` plus `k * every` units: for minutes, hours, days and weeks a fixed number of seconds; for months and years a calendar step computed from the original anchor, clamped to the target month's last day when the anchor's day doesn't exist. Period `k` is `[boundary(k), boundary(k+1))`.
  - The first Billing Period is the one containing `starts_at`; the last is the one containing the instant just before `ends_at`, when set.
  - Obligation IDs name a period by its start boundary in Unix seconds (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3). Messages name it by `{starts_at, ends_at}`, which must equal a computed period; anything else is rejected.
- **Accepting:** when the user accepts the subscription, or an executor accepts it under an Allowance, the payer records the acceptance in the request record, in a batch conditioned on the version of the request record it read and, when the request has a proposal expiry, on `server_time_in(null, proposal_expires_at + 1 ms)`, and sends it after that batch succeeds. The expiry is inclusive: its own millisecond is the last at which the acceptance is recorded. A rejection is recorded the same way, so an acceptance and a rejection can't both go out. Each period then has its own obligation, attempts, proof, optional period details, optional receipt and period record.
- **Paying periods:** accepting a subscription pays nothing by itself, as in v0.1 (`specs/payment-requests.md`, "Recurring Payment Requests And Subscriptions"). A Billing Period is paid automatically, by an executor under a matching, accepted Allowance, only then, and that payment's proof carries its `allowance_id` (Section 8.3). Without one, the paying app pays the period as a manual payment, whose proof carries no `allowance_id` (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.10); whether it asks its user before each period is the app's own policy, as in v0.1.
- **Period details:** for any period the payee may send `payment_period_details`, with that period's conversion rates, its destination, or both. Each carries `expires_at` and stays valid until then: a later one replaces none, as in v0.1 (`specs/payment-conversion.md`, "Payment Conversion Quote"). The payer may pay by any valid one, by default the latest, ordered by `created_at` and then `msg_id`; its proof references the one it used. A period has at most 8 period details: the payee sends no more, and the payer's period record holds them all (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3).
- **Rates and payment time:** as with v0.1's conversion quotes, the rates of period details apply to a payment whose independently verified payment time is from their start through their `expires_at`, compared at millisecond precision. Their start is their signed `created_at`, cut to whole seconds, which is v0.1's `valid_from`; the payee sets `created_at` to the time it signs them and never backdates them, as in v0.1. That time comes from the payment method's evidence (`specs/payment-conversion.md`), never from when the proof arrived. Both sides apply it. The payer pays by the rates only while its device clock and the latest service time it has verified are both at or after their start and before `expires_at`, checked again just before money moves (Section 7.2); otherwise it waits for valid period details, or pays to an endpoint of the requested asset. The payee credits a payment at those rates only if its verified payment time is from their start through `expires_at`. A payment verified outside that interval doesn't pay the period at those rates. As in v0.1, late evidence isn't malformed: the payee still credits the funds received and may label them underpaid, late, or both, and sends no receipt for the period; Paykit adds no refunds, disputes or resolution. A payment to an endpoint of the requested asset is paid 1:1 without rates, so this bound doesn't apply to it, and the destination of period details is used while they haven't expired, as above. An Allowance counts the requested asset and amount and can't authorize repricing, so a payment priced by rates is made only by hand (Section 8.3).
- **Destinations per period:** without `per_period_endpoints`, the request's destination serves every period. With it, the request's `payment_endpoints` serve only the first period, and the payer never attempts a later period before receiving period details with its destination.
- **Not before its start:** no app pays a Billing Period automatically before it starts, and the service enforces it: every attempt on a period carries `server_time_in(s, null)`, so the service applies it only from `s` on its own clock, whatever the payer's clock says. `s` is the period's start. A payer pays a period before its start only by hand, after its user confirms paying that period early; that attempt carries as `s` the time of the confirmation, the latest service time the app had verified then, so the service still never applies it before the user agreed.
- **Tombstones:** each paid period leaves a small tombstone, kept 400 days after its last entry when the request has no payment deadline (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3) and counted against the paying app's share of the claim limit. Without a payment deadline the payer also keeps the period's record for the identity's life (Section 6). So payees of frequent subscriptions should set one.
- **Attempted periods:** a payer's request record lists, in `attempted_periods`, the start of every Billing Period that may have an open attempt: at most 16, in ascending order, each once. An attempt on a period not listed writes the request record in the attempt's batch, adding the period, at the record's next version; the attempt is already conditioned on the version it read (Section 7.2 step 1). An attempt on a listed period doesn't write it. A write of the request record may drop a listed period whose claim has no open attempt, conditioned on that claim's step as the writer read it (`claim_step`), so a new attempt on that period meanwhile makes the write fail. Apps drop finished periods whenever they write the record anyway, for example when adding one. With 16 listed and none to drop, an app makes no attempt on a period not listed until one of them finishes. So every period with an open attempt is listed in the version of the request record its attempt's batch left, the one it was conditioned on or the one it wrote, and stays listed until its claim has no open attempt. This covers every period an attempt may pay: the current one, one paid early by hand (Not before its start, above), and a past one whose payment deadline, which can be longer than the period, hasn't passed.
- **Ending:** either side's cancellation ends the subscription. A payer records its cancellation in the request record, in a batch that writes the record at its next version, so conditioned on the version it read, and conditioned on the claim of every listed period being at the step read with no open attempt (`claim_step`), as in Section 8.1, and sends it after that batch succeeds. An attempt on a period not listed writes the request record, and one on a listed period moves its claim's step, so an attempt and a cancellation can't both succeed. A listed period with an open attempt refuses the cancellation: the app shows that the payment is in progress, as for a one-time request. A payee's cancellation of a recurring request stops the payer app as Section 8.1 says (Messages that cross): the app MUST keep a verified `payment_request_cancellation` durably in its own state before it does anything else with it, and from then on MUST make no new attempt on any of the request's Billing Periods, whatever its request record shows; a restart, or a record write that failed or is still pending, never lifts the stop. It records the cancellation in the request record by a later write of its own as soon as it can.

### 8.3 Allowances

Allowances are built entirely in apps on claims and shared records. The switch has no Allowance endpoints, objects or rules.

| Allowance concept | Primitive |
|---|---|
| Proposal, terms, the executor's grant and status | Shared record in `allowances` |
| Ledger | One shared record per Allowance in `allowance_ledgers`: an entry per attempt made under the Allowance, and running totals |
| Payment | The request's claim (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2) |

Record keys are derived from the counterparty and the `allowance_id` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3).

**Setting up.** At acceptance, the app that will pay automatically asks its user, checks the accepted terms against the signed proposal and acceptance stored in the Allowance record, and writes its **executor grant** there: its app ID and the terms hash (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3), signed with its own signing key over the identity, the counterparty, the `allowance_id` and its `confirmed_generation`, the current generation (Pubky Switch spec, Terms and Section 4.3). It must hold `allowance_execute`.

**Executor.** An app pays automatically under an Allowance only while all of these hold: the record holds its own grant for the current terms and counterparty; its **consent list**, kept durably in its own state, holds the Allowance; the request comes from that counterparty and is priced in the Allowance's own asset, as in v0.1 (Section 16); its copy of the deny list, read again before the dispatch (Section 8.1, How fresh the deny list is), is complete and doesn't hold that counterparty (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1); and the occurrence's record isn't marked manual-only (below). It adds an Allowance to the list when it writes the grant, and removes it for good when it sees the Allowance ended, rejected or handed off. No other app can make it pay: a grant can't be forged or moved to another counterparty, changing the terms voids it, and an end it has seen is final. **Manual only:** a payer app may mark an occurrence manual-only on its record, the request record of a one-time request or the period record of a Billing Period (`manual_only`, `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3), as in v0.1 (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Durable Allowance payment accounting"). The mark is never lifted, and no executor pays the occurrence automatically while its record holds it: the executor's batch writes that record at the version it read (step 3, below), so a mark written since makes the batch fail. Deferring an occurrence stays in the executor's own state (Section 16).

**Ends reach the executor.** Any app with `allowance` may end an Allowance (Section 17, question 11). An app that ends an Allowance, hands it off, or records an `allowance_end` from the counterparty also sends `executor_notice` to the executor through its own identity's inbox (Pubky Switch spec, Section 7), so the executor learns of it even if the record is rewritten afterwards. An app that has verified a counterparty's `allowance_end` MUST keep it durably in its own state before it does anything else with it: for a message it receives, in the commit that passes the entry, with no service operation in that step. That kept copy is the stop, not the Allowance record: from then on the app MUST make no new attempt under that Allowance, and a restart, or a record write that failed or is still pending, never lifts it. It still sends `executor_notice`, and records the end in the Allowance record by a later write of its own as soon as it can; until then the identity's other apps may not know of it, the same exposure as an end not yet delivered. A notice changes only the executor's consent list; ending the Allowance itself is the `allowance_end` exchanged with the counterparty. **After a restore** from backup, an executor asks the user to confirm each Allowance on its consent list again before paying under it, since its backup may predate an end. This relies on the record: if a compromised own app rewrote an ended Allowance as running before the restore, the user's confirmation is the only check left. Every executor batch carries `record_version(Allowance record) == r`, so once the record changes, an executor working from the older version fails and re-reads it. Handing off means the new executor writes its own grant, with its user's consent, replacing the old one; the previous executor stops at its next batch. Paykit coordinates which app pays automatically and what it reserved. Carrying out payments, honouring grants and enforcing the Allowance's limits are the integrating app's job, and the service doesn't check them.

**Valid terms.** Terms that aren't valid by `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.15 are refused when proposed, dropped when the proposal arrives, make an Allowance record that holds them invalid (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2), and are checked again before each automatic payment. An executor never pays under them.

**Ledger.** The ledger record holds an entry for each attempt made under the Allowance: the request's obligation, the `attempt_id`, value, asset and time `at`. The value is the amount paid, which the payment method transfers exactly (Section 7.2 step 1); fees aren't counted. It also holds running totals of spent amounts and payment counts for entries already compacted.

- **Who wrote it:** apps accept a ledger version only from the executor whose grant the Allowance record held at the version that write was conditioned on. The Allowance record keeps every grant it has held, oldest first, each with the record version that the write adding it built on (the version its writer read, Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3), which the writer knows before the service gives the new one; the last one is the current grant. Each grant's signature covers that version, so a history with a wrong version doesn't verify. Writers only append to it, and an app that has seen an entry ignores a write that changes or drops it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2); for an app that hasn't, a dropped entry only makes later ledger writes fail the check, which stops automatic payment rather than trusting a ledger. To check a ledger version, an app takes the last grant whose write built on a version older than the one the ledger write was conditioned on: it must verify for the identity, the counterparty and the Allowance, name the current terms hash, and belong to the writer. This works for versions the app never saw. A ledger write without that condition, or checked against a history that doesn't verify, isn't accepted either. A ledger written by any other app stops automatic payment under that Allowance until the user confirms again: the executor fails closed instead of trusting lower usage, and after the confirmation writes the ledger again (After a confirmation, below).
- **Building on a version accepts it:** a ledger write built on a ledger version accepts that version, so the executor builds on one only once it passes every check: the kept version (Last ledger version, below), no write of the Allowance record or the ledger by a removed app after its `distrust_from` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.2 step 3), Who wrote it, and the ledger rules (From one version to the next, below). This holds for every ledger write it makes: the executor batch, an outcome, an outcome copied from a claim, a compaction and a release. If the ledger fails them, the executor writes no ledger version on it, and the Allowance needs its user's confirmation again; an outcome batch then records the attempt's outcome on its claim alone.
- **A grant implies a ledger:** right after it writes the first grant of an Allowance, its executor writes the empty ledger, conditioned on the new Allowance record version. Until that write lands, the executor alone takes the absence as an empty ledger. From then on a missing or deleted ledger is untrusted, "usage history lost": no automatic payment until the user confirms again. A visible delete is judged by its writer like any other write. Only an absence that contradicts the version the executor kept is a rollback (Last ledger version).
- **From one version to the next:** an app that holds a ledger version checks each newer one against it and ignores and flags one that counts less (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2), and the executor checks the ledger it builds on against the last version it accepted. An entry keeps its obligation, `attempt_id`, value, asset and time. Its outcome only goes from none to `done` or `failed`, or from `failed` to `done`. It leaves only by compaction, once old enough: a `done` one into the totals, and one the reader held without an outcome or `failed` into the compacted failed list, or into the totals, since it may have become `done` before it was compacted. A compacted failed attempt leaves that list only into the totals, and the totals never grow by less than what left them. New entries may come in any time.
- **Time of an entry:** each executor batch carries `server_time_in(at, at + 60 s)`, with `at` taken from the server time of the service's latest response and the interval cut short at the next boundary of any anchored limit. The entry therefore lies in exactly one period of every anchored limit. For a rolling limit of length L it counts from `at` until `at + 60 s + L`. The service can't write inside the sealed ledger, so the executor states the time and the condition makes the service vouch for it: whatever an executor's own clock says, its entries are dated within 60 s of when the service applied them, so every app and every later executor counts each payment in the same period.
- **Usage:** each app derives usage over the entries as `specs/allowances.md` defines it, for every period limit, the per-payment limit and the lifetime limit, with server time in place of the wallet's admission time and watermark. Each entry counts by the outcome of the attempt it names:

| Attempt outcome (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2) | Counts as |
|---|---|
| `open` | Reserved |
| `unknown` | Reserved, until reported paid or failed |
| `done` | Spent |
| `failed`, written by the attempt's owner | Released |
| `failed`, written by another app | Reserved, until the user releases it |

- **`failed` isn't final:** a later `done` from the payee's receipt (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2 row 3) makes the attempt spent, whatever the ledger says. The service keeps the claim of a failed attempt until 30 days after its deadline (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3), and the ledger keeps a `failed` entry, or after compaction its `attempt_id` in the totals, visible to the claim-based counting at least as long, so a `done` that a late receipt records in that window spends. After the window the receipt can't be recorded on the claim: an executor that gets it marks its own entry `done` (a `failed` entry may become `done`), so the limits stay right, and every app that gets it shows the user that a payment marked failed was paid. A `failed` written by an app other than the attempt's owner, for example after the owner's certificate expired, frees nothing on its own: the executor shows the user the amount, and only the user's explicit release, which the executor records as `failed` in the entry, frees it. An entry's own `done` spends, and its own `failed` releases unless its claim now shows `done`. A compacted `failed` attempt counts the same way, in the lifetime amount, until the executor moves a later `done` of it into the totals.
- **One entry per attempt:** a retry after a failed attempt is a new attempt with its own entry, so every attempt is counted once.
- **Outcomes in the ledger:** the current executor writes each attempt's outcome into its entry as soon as it learns it, from the feed or, once the claim's entries are stubs, by reading the claim again (Switch wire 7.4.8 and 7.5), including outcomes of a previous executor's attempts, in the same batch when it records the outcome itself, so usage doesn't depend on claims that may since have been deleted (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3). It writes `done` from a claim, `failed` only when the attempt's owner wrote it or the user released the amount, and a later `done` over a `failed`, for which it reads the claims of `failed` entries again. An entry without an outcome whose claim is gone counts as spent.
- **Compaction:** when the record nears its size limit, the executor drops entries whose outcome is final and which are older than the longest period limit plus 60 s. It adds the paid amounts and counts of `done` ones to the running totals, and keeps each `failed` one's `attempt_id`, obligation and value in the totals, so that a later `done` still counts, once. A ledger with an entry in another asset than the Allowance's, an attempt listed twice, or a value or time that doesn't parse is invalid: usage and compaction refuse it rather than count it as less.
- **Local usage:** only a cache, rebuilt on start, restore and handoff from the ledger record and, for entries without an outcome, the request claims.
- **Last ledger version:** the executor keeps the last ledger write it wrote or read with an authenticated version, from the transition proof it got with the write or the verified stub of the feed item (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3), and the write's item hash. A version number alone proves nothing, since the service gives it. Before an automatic payment it reads the ledger record: the write shown must be that one, by item hash, or one signed as built on that version or a later one (its `expected_version`). Any other write, a missing ledger record, or one that fails the checks of Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3 is a rollback (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1). Two cases aren't. A write whose signature verifies with its writer's certificate, that is the kept write or is signed as built on it or later, but that fails a check by policy, such as its writer's standing, Who wrote it or the ledger rules, only pauses the Allowance: the executor refuses it as untrusted (Building on a version accepts it). And a record is absent after a delete the executor saw. A write whose signature doesn't verify with its writer's certificate, or whose item doesn't match its chain stub, can only come from the service altering what it serves: it is a rollback, whether or not the executor kept a version.
- **After a confirmation:** an Allowance whose ledger the executor didn't accept needs a repair, which the user confirms with its figures shown. The executor first shows the user what the repair would write: the usage per period limit, amount and count, the lifetime amount, the entries reserved, spent and released, and the amounts held reserved by a `failed` another app wrote. The user confirms those figures, and the confirmation writes exactly that ledger; if anything it was built from changed in between, the executor refuses and shows the new figures. The repair writes the ledger again, as a merge that never counts less: the last ledger version it accepted, which it keeps with its content, as it was; what the ledger shown adds to it (entries it lacks, a `done`, higher totals); every attempt its journal holds under the Allowance; and every attempt of an app named in the grant history that the claims of the counterparty's obligations show, unless the payment's verified proof names no Allowance or another one. Without an accepted version, the ledger shown is the only record of compacted totals, so the figures the user confirms are all there is to check them against.

**Paying under an Allowance:**

1. The executor reads the Allowance record, the ledger record and the request's claim.
2. It recomputes usage and checks the terms and limits at the current server time, and the transfer (Section 7.2 step 1): a destination that transfers another amount, or one the method can't tell, isn't paid automatically, nor is a payment priced by period details' rates (Section 8.2). It pays nothing automatically while its device clock and the service's differ by more than the clock tolerance (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1, Clock bound). Terms are checked whole against the table of `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.15; a time that doesn't parse fails closed, never as no limit.
3. It sends one batch:
   - the `attempt` on the request's claim;
   - the ledger record with the new entry, at its next version;
   - the activity record showing the payment as being handled;
   - when the activity record is a period record, a write of the request record that names the Allowance, at the request's first automatic payment (One Allowance per request, below), or adds the period to `attempted_periods` (Section 8.2), or both in one write, since a batch writes a record once;

   with the conditions `record_version(Allowance record) == r`, `server_time_in(at, at + 60 s)` and, on the attempt, the request's `deadline` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2), the version of the request record it read (Section 7.2) and, for a Billing Period, the period's start as its not-before (Section 8.2).
4. It follows Section 7.2. Proofs carry `allowance_id`.

- **Conflicts:** if any check fails, nothing is written and the executor recomputes. The ledger record's version serializes every executor batch for the Allowance; a change to the terms, status or grant changes the Allowance record's version; a late batch fails `server_time_in`. If another wallet is already attempting the request, the attempt fails and nothing is added to the ledger.
- **Doubtful obligations:** an executor never attempts an obligation with an `unknown` or orphaned attempt, or whose next attempt would be flagged `possibly_duplicate` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2).
- **One Allowance per request:** the first automatic payment of a request names its Allowance in the request record, in the attempt's batch, and every later automatic payment of the request or of its Billing Periods is made only under that one, as in v0.1, or under the one the user moves a later period to (below). An ended, handed-off or exhausted Allowance leaves the request to manual payment: the executor never picks another one for it. Only the user moves a recurring request's later Billing Periods to another accepted Allowance that covers it, as in v0.1 (`specs/allowances.md`, "Authorized recurring reassociation"): an app adds an entry to the request record's `reassociations` after its user confirms it, naming the Allowance and the first Billing Period it covers, one that hasn't started and is later than every period in `attempted_periods`. A period's Allowance is the one of the last entry starting at or before it, else the named one. Usage already counted stays in the earlier Allowance's ledger. An app that has seen the name or an entry ignores and flags a write that changes or drops it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2; `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3).
- **Limits:** the executor enforces the Allowance limits. Paykit coordinates the reservation (one ledger version, one terms version, server time) and never enforces spending limits at the protocol level, so a faulty or malicious executor can still exceed them.

**Handoff:** outstanding attempts keep counting as reserved until their outcomes arrive. The previous executor may still record the outcomes of its own attempts on their claims, without touching the ledger, which only the current executor writes; the new executor copies those outcomes into the ledger. Any other batch of the previous executor fails the Allowance record condition. A handoff doesn't cancel payments already started. When an executor stops being current, apps holding `allowance` prompt the user to choose another.

**Ending:** an app with `allowance` marks the Allowance record ended, at its expected version. The records stay as history.

**After provider loss** (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6): the Allowance record holds `confirmed_generation`, the generation in which its user last confirmed it. While that is lower than `recovery_from + 1` in the current `identity.json`, the executor makes no automatic payment under it. After the user confirms again, the executor writes a new grant with the current generation; no other app can end the pause. Earlier doubtful obligations stay blocked from automatic payment, and delayed outcomes of earlier attempts still count at their entries' times.

**Integrity:** every ledger write is a writer-signed operation, sealed and bound to its record key, so the service can't forge, alter or move amounts between Allowances. Rollback and incomplete batches are detected as in Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1.

### 8.4 Paying without a request

- **Destinations:** the payee's latest Private Payment List from the `payment_lists` record, or its public endpoints. Private and public payment are separate modes, as in v0.1: the app or the user chooses one explicitly, and a payer never changes from one to the other on its own. The app that published the destination must be current (Section 7.2).
- **Record first:** before paying, the payer writes, in one batch, the `attempt` on the payment's own claim (its obligation is derived from the attempt ID alone, Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3, and its `deadline` is 10 minutes after the latest service time the app has seen, so its tombstone goes 30 days after that, at the end of its reconciliation window, rather than 400 days after its last entry: there is no re-sent copy for it to stop), a `payer_activity` record for the payment, keyed by the attempt ID, and, for a single-use endpoint, the `payment_lists` record marking it used, at its next version. Two own apps therefore can't both pay one single-use endpoint, and a crash after paying leaves an attempt that shows "may be paid" and is resolved like any other (Section 7.3).
- **Without the switch:** a payment without a request to a public endpoint that isn't single-use may go ahead while the payer's own switch doesn't answer. Its obligation is its own, derived from the attempt ID, so no other app can attempt it, and a reusable endpoint has nothing to reserve. The app records the payment in its journal, still checks the destination and its own `identity.json` (fresh within 60 s and naming the service the app uses, so a switch the identity moved away from is followed first, Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3), and pays. Once the switch answers again it writes the payment's `attempt` and `payer_activity` record, then `done` and the record update, and sends the proof, so the identity's other apps and the payee see it then. That `attempt`'s `deadline` is 10 minutes after the service time of the switch's first answer once it answers again, never after a time seen before, which the service may already have passed. Until then it is in that app's journal only: if the app is restored from a backup taken before that write, the payment is lost from the shared history (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4), though not from the payment method's. Payments of requests, Billing Periods, under an Allowance or to a single-use endpoint still need the switch, since their claim or reservation is what stops a second payment.
- **After paying:** the payer writes `done` and updates the record, as in Section 7.2, and sends a `payment_proof` with no request reference. The proof is required after paying a single-use endpoint. Only the payee's receipt settles the claim by evidence: the payer matches a receipt to the payment through its record, whose counterparty must be the receipt's sender, since the obligation doesn't name the payee.
- **Unlabelled:** a payment without a request carries no label, so its proof reaches only the payee's apps holding `request`, which record and credit it (Section 3.4).
- **Credited once:** the payee records a payment without a request by its settlement: the Payment Endpoint Identifier paid and the settlement ID its payment method defines (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3). Every proof of one settlement names the same record, so a second proof with the same evidence, under a new `msg_id`, is a replay and isn't credited again. A proof whose method gives no settlement ID isn't credited. Each proof type has one spelling of its settlement ID, such as a `bitcoin-txid` proof's `txid` in lower-case hex, and a proof in another spelling isn't credited, so one settlement never names two records (Switch wire 1.5).
- **Only what it offered:** a proof that names a Private Payment List of the payee app checking it (`payment_app_id` and `list_version`, `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.10), for a request or without one, is credited by that app only for a payload it offered that payer at that version, while it still keeps the offer. It keeps what each version offered until 30 days after its `expires_at`, the time a payer keeps a proof sent before then to re-send it (Section 9); after that the record of the offer is gone. Within that time, a proof naming a payload or a version it didn't offer that payer isn't credited and gets no receipt; the proof stays in its record, and the payer gets no receipt, its claim showing what its own outcome wrote (Section 7.3). Another own app, which doesn't know what the publishing app offered, checks such a proof by its payment method alone, as any other proof (Section 8.1 and Credited once, above), and so does the publishing app after that time: a late proof of a real payment is still credited and gets its receipt (Section 7.2, What a payment method declares).
- **Reusable and single-use endpoints.** Paying to a reusable endpoint consumes nothing, so the payer can pay again at once. Paying to a single-use endpoint consumes the whole list version, as paying any endpoint did in v0.1 (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Resolve Private Payment"), until the publisher sends a newer one, even if the payment fails; the payer then asks for a newer list.
- **Publishing:** a publishing app sends its current list to the counterparties it chooses, for example each new contact, and a newer version whenever its list changes, when it receives a proof for one of its single-use endpoints, or when it receives a `private_payment_list_request` naming a used version, an expired one, or none (`held_version: null`).
- **Answering a list request:** a request naming the newest version the publishing app sent that counterparty counts as naming a used one, on the payer's word, and gets a newer version: a payer that reserved a single-use endpoint and then failed to pay has consumed that version without a proof. A request naming an older version that hasn't expired, used or not, gets the newest version re-sent, and nothing newly issued. A request naming an expired version, or none, gets a newer version, as above. So that a payer can't use up its single-use payloads, the app sends a counterparty at most one newer version for each version that counterparty names, and at most a small number of newer versions in any 24 hours in answer to its requests, which it chooses (the Paykit SDK: 4). Past the bound of one newer version per version named, a request naming a version already answered gets the newest version sent again, with nothing newly issued, so an answer lost on the way is answered; the app sends the newest version again to a counterparty at most once an hour. Past the daily bound it issues nothing newer.
- **Blocked counterparties:** while the deny list holds a counterparty (Section 8.1), a publishing app holding `contacts` sends it no list, renewal or answer, as in v0.1 (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Block A Peer"). Unblocking ends the pause.
- **No usable list:** if the list is missing, expired or used up, the payer sends `private_payment_list_request`, and sends it again at most once in any 24 hours (the Paykit SDK's interval) while it still has no usable list; a request that doesn't reach the payee's inbox counts as not sent, and goes again at the payer's next receive. A list has expired at its `expires_at` or 30 days after the payer's inbox accepted it, whichever comes first (Section 5). The payee can't be forced to answer, and doesn't answer past its bounds (Answering a list request, above), so the payer waits for the payee's next list, such as its renewal (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.13), and tells the user it is waiting for the payee's list. It may offer to pay to the payee's public endpoints instead, if there are any, but pays there only when the user chooses to.

### 8.5 Two payer apps on one identity

Both receive the request and are notified. The other app learns about the payment through the feed, from the request's claim, whether a wallet or an Allowance executor paid it, and shows:

- "Being handled by your other wallet" after `attempt`;
- "Paid" only after SETTLED.

Once the claim's entries are stubs in the feed (Switch wire 7.5), the other app learns SETTLED by reading the claim again (Switch wire 7.4.8), or from the payer's activity record, which carries the proof.

## 9. Delivery and re-sends

Paykit's messages are delivered as the Switch delivers every message (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11). Paykit adds a delivery state:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Paid | For the payer: the obligation is SETTLED. For the payee: it has verified the proof |

- **Sender retention:** the sender keeps the signed plaintext and `accepted_at` of each message it sent for at least 30 days from its first acceptance, to answer `resend_request`; it may keep them longer, but answers a `resend_request` only with messages first accepted in the last 30 days, the time recipients keep processed IDs, so every copy it re-sends is still deduplicated. A re-send doesn't restart this: the sender keeps one entry per `msg_id`, with its first acceptance time and the later ones, and a request naming any of those times selects it. A re-sent message is sealed to the current receive keys and keeps its `msg_id` and signed plaintext.
- **Deduplication of re-sent requests:**
  - A re-sent request, even after that window, has the same obligation ID, so its claim shows whether it was already handled; once a claim without a deadline is gone, 400 days after its last entry, the payer's record of the payment does (Sections 6 and 7.2).
- **Recovery request:** `resend_request` asks the counterparty to re-send messages; answering is best effort. The counterparty re-sends only kinds that the requesting app's certificate allows it to receive (Section 3.3), and a payer's replies only to an app that would take them under their label (Section 3.4). It limits what it re-sends: one message to the same requester identity at most once an hour, and at most a number of messages per requester identity a day, which it chooses (the prototype: 500). It leaves the rest out, and the requester may ask again later.
- **What an app asks for:** the service writes the unopenable, unreadable and `missed` items that lead an app to ask for re-sends, and the app can't check them, so it bounds what it asks on their word. For an entry it can't open, a `missed` item and an unreadable item, it never asks for a time before the time of its cursor, less the clock tolerance; for an unreadable item, also only after a verified departure, for an entry accepted at or before it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2); and never for a time that doesn't parse. A `resend_request` adds no conversation entry (Pubky Switch spec, Section 7).

## 11. Threat model

The adversaries, what they can and can't do, and the protections are those of the Pubky Switch spec (Pubky Switch spec, Section 14), whose table states what each can do to payments. Paykit adds:

- **Limits of the switch's copy:** a payer finds the payee's switch through the Homeserver's document, possibly an old one. After a cooperative move a stale copy names the old switch, which, frozen, answers with its last document and the freeze naming the new one, so the payer follows it to the newer document (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 4.3 and 12.5); it does so for the identity's record age limit, after which no record from before the move is payable. This doesn't help after a provider loss, where the old switch is gone or hostile. One that is gone doesn't answer, so the payer pays only on the user's explicit confirmation, and never automatically. A hostile one can answer with its last document and no freeze, which the payer can't tell from a current answer, so the old document's own limits apply (the record age limit, Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.2). Otherwise a switch that doesn't answer, or a malicious one, can only fail to help: it can't serve a newer document than the signer made, nor lower the one the payer uses. Someone flooding the read can't switch the lookup off silently either: reads are limited per network source, answers may be cached, and a payment the switch couldn't confirm needs the user (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3). Anchoring `identity.json` in PKARR would close the provider-loss case too, at the cost of PKARR space and of coupling every `identity.json` update to it; v0.2 doesn't anchor it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 19, decision 23).

## 12. Privacy

What each party learns is in the Pubky Switch spec (Pubky Switch spec, Section 15). Paykit adds one party:

| Party | Learns |
|---|---|
| A payee's switch, from payers | An anonymous lookup of the payee's `identity.json` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3) shortly before a payer without a fresh copy pays a public endpoint: the reader's network address and the time, so that someone is about to pay the identity, but not who. After a cooperative move the old service, frozen, sees the lookups of payers holding the old document for as long as it answers them (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.5). A cache in front of the service sees what the service would |

## 13. Guarantees

- **No duplicate payment for one obligation**, under these conditions:
  - *Holds for:* the same obligation, with compliant apps and a consistent switch: one that follows Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.5, so that every item it acknowledged is durable and it never serves a state behind what it signed.
  - *Does not cover:*
    - a user who confirms an outcome (`reconcile`, `unknown` or `failed`), or a manual payment marked "may already be paid";
    - a malicious or non-compliant switch, including one that shows different apps different histories after any shared prefix, or loses a write it acknowledged and serves on (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 6.5 and 10.1); on such a split a recurring obligation can be paid once per branch in every Billing Period;
    - a payer that carries out an authorized attempt more than once, for example on two devices of one app (rail-level idempotency is the wallet's responsibility);
    - a payment method that reports a final failure and then completes the payment anyway, or whose declared idempotency or history (Section 7.2) doesn't hold;
    - a payee that issues two obligations for one purpose, or misdates a re-sent request during provider loss;
    - an old provider that keeps authorizing attempts after a provider loss (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6, residual risk).
- **Allowance executors** are responsible for their Allowances' limits.
- **Confidentiality, forward secrecy, integrity, isolation, idempotent processing and availability** are the Switch's guarantees (Pubky Switch spec, Section 16), which Paykit inherits.

## 14. Performance targets

| Action | Target |
|---|---|
| Send any message | 1 round trip: the append to the recipient's inbox and the shared record write at the sender's own switch, in parallel; 0.3-0.7 s. A first message to a new counterparty adds one round for the directory read, made in parallel with delivering the sender's `identity.json` and writing its conversation entry |
| Receive a message | Delivered on the open long-poll; the shared record batch makes it durable in 1 round trip |
| Recipient sees it | Under 1 s in the foreground; 1-5 s through push |
| Pay a request | Critical path before money moves: 1 claim write (~0.2-0.3 s), with `identity.json` revalidated in parallel. The acceptance, `done`, the record update and the proof follow in the background, plus wallet time |
| Save a contact | 1 operation: allowlist entry |
| Directory lookup | 1 request when a counterparty is new; afterwards cached while the recipient's `identity.json` names the same switch and generation, and refreshed in the background when an append shows a newer `directory_version` |
| Receive key rotation | 1 request, about once per active day per app by default, after a feed read; never on a user-facing step. A sender whose cached directory predates the rotation pays one extra round (`stale_key`), unless it refreshed the directory in the background (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.1) |
| Add or revoke an app | One operation each for the service, `identity.json` and key bundles, whatever the contact count. A revocation also has own apps deliver the new `identity.json`, in the background, to the switches they wrote to in the last 30 days (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.2) |
| New app catch-up | 1 list call per collection, then the feed |
| Idle | Foreground: one long-poll every ~25 s. Background: none (push) |

**Homeserver calls outside the critical path,** allowed by the design rule: revalidating the app's own `identity.json` in parallel with an `attempt` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3); the event stream subscription that watches its own and its counterparties' `identity.json`, held while the app runs in the foreground; and fetching a counterparty's `identity.json` in the background when an event names it, or when a copy it doesn't watch is more than 10 minutes old, to route messages and to check the apps it hears from and pays to.

**Paying a public endpoint:** the lookup of the payee's `identity.json` at its switch runs in parallel with the Homeserver record read that follows the Homeserver's `identity.json`, and the payer waits for it at most a short grace after that read, so it adds no request in sequence. If it hasn't answered when money is about to move, for example because a lost request is noticed only after the client's timeout, the payment never waits longer: it needs the user's confirmation and is flagged (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3). The end-to-end latency test checks this on every sample: either the lookup answered before money moved or the payment was flagged, and with and without the lookup the requests in sequence before money moves are the same.

Targets are measured by counting requests per action, as the prototype does (Section 18). Latency is judged on a stated network profile for the user's own actions: sending a request, receiving one, tapping Pay until money can move, a first contact and a cold start. Background processing, such as the payer's follow-ups after paying, the payee's handling of an acceptance and a proof, and idle checks, and delivery on the open long-poll are reported separately.

## 16. Left to apps or later

What the Switch leaves to apps or decides later is in the Pubky Switch spec (Pubky Switch spec, Section 17). Paykit adds:

- **Up to each app:**
  - offline endpoint issuing;
  - deferring an occurrence for later reconsideration, kept in the executor's own state: it grants no authority to pay and binds no other app, as in v0.1 (`specs/allowances.md`, "Payment Request integration").
- **Decided later:**
  - a v0.2 ERC-20 Payment Proof profile (Section 15).
  - Allowances that pay requests priced in another asset, for example a subscription priced in usd paid from a btc Allowance. For v0.2 an Allowance pays only requests in its own asset, as in v0.1 (Section 8.3); a payee that wants automatic payment in another asset bills in that asset at its own rate. A later term can widen this without breaking older executors, which keep refusing.
- **Migration from v0.1,** planned in full after the freeze (Section 18). v0.1 and v0.2 apps can't pay each other, so the payer and the payee both update to v0.2. The plan carries across:
  - each identity's Pubky grants, revoked or narrowed before its first v0.2 `identity.json` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.4);
  - public Payment Endpoints, republished in each app's v0.2 record (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.2);
  - open Allowances, with their ledgers of what each has paid, so that no limit starts again from zero;
  - recurring requests in the middle of a Billing Period, so that the period is paid once, neither twice nor never;
  - receipts and the rest of the payment history;
  - sellers on paykit-server: each seller approves it as a v0.2 app with the `request` scope, and its open requests carry over.

## 17. Open questions for the team

Questions 1 to 12 are deliberate trade-offs; 1 to 6, 8 and 9 are the switch's, given in Pubky Switch spec, Section 19. Each has a default, which this spec describes and the prototype implements and tests. Questions 1 to 12 are now decided: 1 to 9, 11 and 12 as their defaults, with the metadata of 3 and 6 disclosed to users and push optional for each app, and question 10 is closed: shared contact details aren't part of Paykit. The team accepts each one explicitly, as a product choice; until then they are accepted risks. The team's decision list explains each one. The switch also has three open questions, given in Pubky Switch spec, Section 19: 25, an authenticated sign of erasure, 26, bounding `renewed` in `identity.json`, and 29, uploads from a dishonest app after a provider loss; until they are decided, their defaults apply, for 25 the 24-hour rule of Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.7. Paykit has three open questions too, 27, 28 and 30, below; the switch and Paykit share one set of numbers, which identify questions and don't order them, and 29 is the switch's; until they are decided, the rules this spec describes apply. Allowances across assets are deferred (Section 16).

7. **Limits.** 64 KiB messages, 16 KiB for the kinds activity records store, 256 KiB shared records, about 1,500 live ledger entries per Allowance (a 256 KiB ledger record), 32 apps per identity by default. Decided: kept.
10. **Shared contact details.** Closed: not part of Paykit. Contacts in Paykit are only the switch's allowlist and deny list (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2). Names, pictures and notes that all of a person's apps share belong in an address book for every Pubky app, not in Paykit's records.
11. **Who may end an Allowance.** Any own app with `allowance` may end one, and an `executor_notice` makes sure the executor stops even if it was offline and the Allowance record was rewritten afterwards (Section 8.3). This lets the user cancel from any device, including when the executor's device is lost or off. The alternative is that only the executor may end it, which removes the notice but means cancelling elsewhere requires revoking the executor or handing it off first. Decided: any own app with `allowance` may end it.
12. **Paying while the Homeserver is unreachable.** Before each attempt, an app revalidates its own `identity.json` unless it did so in the last 60 s (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3), so payments stop while the identity's Homeserver can't be reached. This keeps an app from paying through a switch the identity has moved away from, which matters most right after a provider change (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6). The alternative is a grace period on a cached copy, which keeps payments working through a Homeserver outage but widens that window. Decided: payments stop.

**Open questions.** Three questions are open. Each has a default, the rule this spec describes.

27. **Spam protection for apps without `contacts`.** The spam rule binds only an app holding `contacts`, the scope that gives `lists.read` (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1). Every app holding `pay` holds it (Section 3.1), so only an app that doesn't pay, such as a payee app, can lack it: such an app holds no lists, so in it a sender the service has flagged, or a message accepted before a block, can create an activity record, or have a reply kept for a request record the app doesn't hold yet, within the bounds of Section 8.1. Nor can such an app pause the Private Payment Lists it sends a blocked counterparty (Section 8.4). Protecting those apps needs one of two changes, and neither is taken here: (a) `contacts` required with `request` and its labelled selling scopes too, which lets every selling app, a hosted one such as paykit-server included, read the identity's contacts and deny list; or (b) a Switch change that shows a receiving app its sender's state with each entry. Two notes concern apps that hold `contacts`. A dedicated outcome for a message deferred until the lists are known would be cleaner than the client's store-retry hold; it is a later client addition, and until then the store-retry hold (`Outcome::Retry`) stands in for it, so a host sees `StoreFailing` for this cause too. And an app whose lists are too large to fit one commit of its store can't refresh its copy, so it defers all new activity until they fit: at the default limits they fit, so this residual comes with raised limits. Open: until it is decided, the rule of `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1 applies as written.

28. **Lifting the hold after a restore automatically.** After any restore from a backup, an app holds every open request and Allowance until the record shows a cancellation or end or the user confirms (Section 7.2, What a restore loses), since the re-sends a `resend_request` brings can't show that nothing more is coming. Lifting the hold without the user would need a `paykit.resend_answer` from each app that answers, saying that its selection was complete, with nothing left out by its limits or its retention; a MUST to answer; and a way for the requester to know which of the counterparty's apps must answer, since each app keeps only the messages it sent. Even then, a sender re-sends only messages first accepted in the last 30 days (Section 9), so a gap older than that stays uncovered. Skipping the hold for a backup that is fresh, and detecting a loss of state the app wasn't told of, both need something outside the app's state that moves with every entry it passes. One candidate is the Switch telling an app the cursor it holds for it, for example in the feed answer: a held cursor past the app's own proves lost progress, and one equal to the backup's proves none. That is a change to the Pubky Switch spec, and open there. The other is a marker the app keeps elsewhere, such as in its private Homeserver folder, which has a failure path of its own at every stop. The question also covers a request first seen after the restore, which may be a re-send of one the backup missed. Candidates for ending its hold: a mark by its sender that it is a re-send, which a sender can't be relied on to set; the end of the 30-day re-send window after the restore (Section 9); or the user's confirmation of each request. Open: until it is decided, the hold follows every restore, covers requests first seen after it, and ends only by the record or the user's confirmation of each request.

30. **Growth of `used` in a `payment_lists` record.** A payer's `payment_lists` record keeps every `used` entry it has held, and readers ignore a write that drops one (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.4), so the record only grows, within the 256 KiB limit for a shared record value (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2), and paying one payee app's single-use endpoints for long enough fills it. A pruning rule would let an entry go once no own app may still pay from the list version it belongs to, for example once that version has expired, at its `expires_at` or 30 days after the inbox accepted it (Section 8.4); the question also covers what a payer does when the record is full before then. Open: until it is decided, entries are never dropped, and when the record can't take another entry, the `attempt` batch that carries the mark is refused whole (Section 7.2 step 1, Reserve a single-use endpoint), so no single-use payment happens unmarked: the payment fails closed, and the payee then publishes its lists from a new app, whose record starts empty.

**Further team decisions.** These aren't protocol fixes, and they are left to the team. They are numbered as in the team's decision list, after its closed decisions 13 and 14; that list gives each in full. All are decided or closed. Decisions 15, 23 and 24 are the switch's, given in Pubky Switch spec, Section 19. Two of the decisions add protocol: the labelled selling scopes of 22 (Sections 3.1, 3.3, 3.4, 3.5, 6 and 8.1) and the hand-over of 24 (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.8).

16. **Personal data: GDPR, retention and erasure.** Closed: the spec gives account erasure (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.7). The signer erases the identity's account at a service, which deletes everything it holds for the identity, so what Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3 keeps for good is kept for the account's life, and a user can delete their account. The rest, such as a privacy policy, records of processing and a data protection impact assessment, is each operator's own compliance work, not a protocol decision.
17. **Background payment on iOS.** Closed: whether an app pays while it is closed is each app's choice, within the key rules of Pubky Switch spec, Section 9, which describes the default in which the push extension only shows messages.
18. **Per-device or seed-derived app keys** (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.1). Random keys per device keep devices apart, and every restore needs an approval; seed-derived keys restore without one, but make the devices one app, unprotected from each other. Decided: seed-derived keys. Keeping the devices of one app from carrying out one attempt twice is the wallet's job (Section 13).
19. **The signer at launch** (Pubky Switch spec, Section 18). Decided: Bitkit is the signer for now; whether Ring becomes one too is decided later. One Rust signer core serves both Bitkit platforms, and Ring if it does.
20. **The SDK timeline.** Decided: the v0.2 SDK's surface is drafted and Bitkit's calls mapped onto it before the freeze, then a phased plan with owners, dates and the kill criteria of Section 18.
21. **The name of the service.** Decided: it is the Pubky Switch, a name that isn't Paykit-specific, since the switch isn't payment-specific. Its mailbox part, where senders' messages land and apps read them, keeps the name "inbox", as in `POST /v1/{id}/inbox` and "send to their inbox". Pubky already has the `pubky/inbox` ping service and the relay inbox of Pubky auth. For now the switch doesn't merge with `pubky/inbox`, which sends wake-up pings and leaves message bodies on the sender's Homeserver.
22. **How finely history is shared.** A scope's keys open all of that scope's records, so every app holding a scope sees its whole history: a hosted app holding `request`, such as paykit-server, reads all of the identity's payee history, including requests the person's own wallets sent (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2). Should history be shared by scope, or more finely, for example per app or per label? Options: keep it by scope; finer sharing, such as a labelled request scope with its own keys, so that a hosted app reads only what is written under its label. Decided: labelled selling scopes, so that a hosted app holding a label reads only that label's history and the identity's own wallets, holding `request`, still read all of it. They change the scope and kind tables (Sections 3.1 and 3.3), and Sections 3.1, 3.3, 3.4, 3.5, 6 and 8.1 specify them: payers see the label, plain `request` covers every label, and a certificate holds at most 8 labels.

## 18. Prototype and process

The reference prototype is shared separately, with its history (phase 1, the mechanisms the spec has since dropped: tickets, acks, leases and the claim lease; and the full lists of what phase 2 built and tested) and its full measurements.

**What the prototype covers**

- The reference prototype implements this spec, but the rules listed below under "Where the reference prototype differs", on a local Pubky testnet: the switch with its feed and chain, shared records, conditions, batches, conversation entries, app and receive key registration, key bundles and `identity.json` delivery; apps with shared activity records, and Allowances and recurring requests built only on claims and shared records; moving to another provider and provider loss; an in-process push relay; and a full payment over HTTP against a local Pubky testnet Homeserver.
- The same reference prototype implements the Pubky Switch spec: the switch, its signer and its apps are the code that Paykit runs on.

Their public vector tests, which anyone can run, rebuild and check every vector of `specs/v0.2/vectors/` and of the Switch's set; the generator of the cases `paykit-lib` added is in those tests (`paykit-lib/tests/vectors/`). **Conformance cases.** Rules of an app's run that no vector can check are checked by these cases, which an implementation runs against its own app and a switch:

- **`restore_stale_backup_cancellation_stop`** (Sections 7.2, What a restore loses, and 8.1 to 8.3).
  - *Setup:* payer identity A with one app P holding `pay` and `contacts` (a variant has two apps, both of whose record writes fail); payee identity B with an app S holding `request`. S sends R1, a one-time request without a deadline, R2, a recurring request that P accepts (a variant pays R2 under an Allowance with P as its executor), and R3, another one-time request without a deadline. P records all three. P takes a layered backup K0 at cursor c0.
  - *Steps:* S sends a `payment_request_cancellation` C1 for R1 and C2 for R2. P verifies both, keeps the stops and passes the entries in that commit, at cursor c1 after c0. Then either (F) P's request record writes are refused for good, such as with an injected `403 permission_missing`, or (P) P stops before it sends them. Every target app's cursor has passed the entries, so the service deletes them. P is restored from K0.
  - *Expected:* P was restored, so it holds every open request and Allowance. Nothing in the chain shows that P passed anything after c0, since the entries were passed with cursor moves alone, so an implementation that relies on the chain to decide whether to hold fails this case. P makes no automatic attempt on R1, R3 or any Billing Period of R2, in the Allowance variant even after the user confirms the Allowance again (Section 8.3); a manual attempt on R1 or R3 needs the user's confirmation that the payee may have cancelled it; and P sends S a `resend_request` with `accepted_from` at the `accepted_at` of the entry at c0, less 5 minutes.
  - *Branches:* (i) S re-sends C1 and C2: P keeps both stops, makes no attempt on R1 or R2 even with the user's confirmation, and writes the request records; R3 stays held until the user confirms it. (ii) S never answers: the hold stays; once the user confirms a request, an attempt on it is allowed. (iii) P is restored instead from a backup K1 taken at c1: the kept stops come back and stop R1 and R2 for good, and no confirmation overrides them; the hold still covers every other open request and Allowance, so R3 is held until the user confirms it. (iv) Another own app's record write succeeded: the record shows the cancellations, which stop R1 and R2 for good.

- **`dispatch_window`** (Section 7.2 step 1).
  - *Setup:* payer identity A with one app P holding `pay` and `contacts`, paying through a method that declares its longest time L and isn't idempotent; payee identity B with an app S holding `request`. S sends R, a one-time request.
  - *Steps:* P writes the `attempt` for R. (a) The dispatch is delayed, after the `attempt`'s answer, until P's allowance before the dispatch has passed. (b) P is configured with an `orphan_after` below L plus that allowance.
  - *Expected:* (a) P submits nothing and writes `failed`, and S sees no acceptance. (b) P refuses the configuration before it writes any `attempt`.
- **`restored_certificate`** (Section 7.2, The restored certificate).
  - *Setup:* identity A with app P holding `pay` and `contacts`; its certificate passes Paykit's rules. P takes a layered backup K0. A's signer then renews P's certificate with one switch permission that P's Paykit scopes don't derive, which the Switch takes and Paykit refuses, and lists it in `identity.json`.
  - *Steps:* (a) A backup K1 carrying the refused certificate, made by hand since P takes no backup under a certificate it refuses, is restored. (b) P is restored from K0, then reads its feed, and a counterparty sends it a `payment_request`.
  - *Expected:* (a) The restore is refused and writes nothing. (b) The restore succeeds; the first read takes the renewed certificate; P then sends nothing, pays nothing and holds the request unprocessed. Once A renews the certificate again with one that passes, P processes the request.

**Not covered yet:** a real phone with APNs or FCM, the Pubky staging Homeserver, a real network (the profiles below are simulated), persistent storage on disk, per-app Pubky grants (apps write through their identity's session), and histories of days or weeks.

**Results**

- **Request counts (measured):** requests in sequence per action, in a release build: send 1, tap Pay 1, receive 2, first contact 5 (the prototype runs first contact's steps one after another; Section 14 needs two rounds). The counts are the same under every network profile, and they are what Section 14's targets count.
- **Timings on localhost (measured):** every action's warm p95 at most 25 ms.
- **Timings on a simulated network (with retries):** in release, three seeds per profile (2026-11-04), given as the median of the three runs' p95 with the highest in brackets: tap Pay 29 ms (30 ms) on home Wi-Fi (20 ms round trip, no loss) and 106 ms (107 ms) on mobile 4G (80 ms, 1% loss); 331 ms on poor mobile (250 ms, 3% loss), from one earlier run. On mobile 4G, over 20 warm cycles and 30 samples for first contact and cold start, the user's own actions' p95 were: first contact 1,377 ms (1,397 ms), receive 192 ms (1,158 ms), send 114 ms (1,075 ms), tap Pay 106 ms (107 ms) and cold start 204 ms (255 ms). A run's p95 is its second slowest sample, so two samples that hit a loss put it near 1.1 s, hence the higher brackets. The background steps after paying took 306 ms (1,274 ms) and the payee's processing of acceptance and proof 412 ms (1,358 ms). Paying a public endpoint without a fresh `identity.json` took 853 ms (1,329 ms) until money could move; 1 of the 60 such payments on 4G went ahead before the payee's switch answered, flagged as unconfirmed (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3). Every action succeeded, through retries of the identical request.
- **Delivery to an open long-poll, simulated mobile 4G:** p95 109 ms over 30 samples, against Section 14's target of under 1 s in the foreground. An earlier run reported 2,990 ms, the slowest of only 10 samples, in which the simulation noticed a lost request only 1 s after the long-poll's wait, later than TCP would retransmit it. It is reported apart from the actions above.
- **Scale (one identity for 10 minutes):** 10,000 contacts; 1,000 inbound messages per minute; no refusals. After the performance hardening, the service's CPU stays flat at about 6% of a core and the end-to-end p95 at 9 ms.
- **Soak (one switch for 75 minutes):** 1,000 identities with 2,500 apps, about 18,400 payments, including requests paid from two wallets at once, Allowance payments and payments without a request, with receive key rotations and apps going offline throughout. Every invariant held: no obligation paid twice, every outbox empty, every journal entry closed, every paid request SETTLED, and no inbox entry, open attempt or live claim left.
- **Correctness:** no duplicate payment outside Section 13's exclusions, and no stuck state without a shown way to resolve it.
- **What it changed:** building the prototype produced 175 changes, listed with the prototype: the design ones are made in this spec, and the wire details are settled in `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md`. The independent implementation found 129 gaps and 28 mismatches in the documents, all settled.

**Limits of these results**

- The service keeps its state in memory, and the apps save into an in-memory `Storage`, so no durable commit or fsync cost is included. Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.5's durability rule is therefore stated, not exercised; the prototype models only the key's mark: a restore fails stop, or models a non-compliant operator that serves on.
- The network is simulated: delay, jitter and loss per request, without bandwidth, TCP retransmission or connection setup.
- There is no real phone, APNs or FCM, and no Pubky staging Homeserver.
- The end-to-end latency test enforces the latency criterion below on the Wi-Fi and 4G profiles, with 30 samples for first contact, cold start and long-poll delivery, and checks that every action completes and that payments are made once; the poor-mobile profile is reported only. It needs PostgreSQL.
- These are prototype timings, not production measurements. Section 14 gives the targets.
- The scale run and the soak ran on an earlier version of the prototype and weren't run again. The end-to-end latency test ran again in release on 2026-11-04, on the Wi-Fi and 4G profiles with three seeds each: the request counts are the same; the poor-mobile timings are older. Since then it ran again in a debug build: the request counts are the same, and the timings above weren't measured again.

**Kill criteria**

- **Fail:**
  - warm p95 latency above 2 s for a user's own actions, on a mobile 4G profile (80 ms round trip, 1% loss);
  - any duplicate payment outside the exclusions of Section 13;
  - any stuck state that isn't shown to the user with a way to resolve it;
  - iOS push p95 above 10 s.
- **Pass:** a paused "may be paid" state that is shown, with a way to resolve it.
- **Network profile:** latency criteria are judged on a stated network profile. The user's own actions are first contact, send, receive, tap Pay and a cold start (Section 14). Background processing, such as the steps after paying and a payee handling an acceptance and a proof, is reported separately, and so is delivery to an open long-poll, which Section 14's "Recipient sees it" target covers. Under the prototype's poor-mobile profile the user's own actions stayed under 1.5 s at p95 except a cold start at 2.3 s, and the payee's background processing exceeded 2 s; the criterion is judged on mobile 4G.
- **Results:** on the simulated mobile 4G profile the latency criterion passes in every run, with the user's own actions under 1.5 s at p95 (first contact the slowest, at 1.40 s at most over three seeds); the duplicate payment and stuck state criteria pass. These hold for the prototype, within the limits above. iOS push p95 isn't measured yet; it needs a real phone (Before freeze).

**Production plan.** Work for production, not for the prototype. Each item needs an owner before the SDK and the switch ship.

- **Secrets:** secret types that aren't `Copy`, zeroize on drop, print nothing in `Debug` (already so in the prototype) and derive no `Serialize`; keys in platform keystores (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.1); deletion by crypto-shredding; and a test that a deleted key's bytes are gone from every file the app persists.
- **Supply chain:** a required cargo-deny job (advisories with expiring ignores, bans on duplicate crypto crates, sources, licenses) with scheduled runs and a Dependabot entry for cargo; the TLS crates kept current; the Android build's linker settings reviewed; one version of each direct crypto crate, one entry point for randomness and a getrandom backend per target; no patched or vendored pubky in any shipped crate, with a `cargo package` check in CI and a scoped conditional-write API asked of Pubky upstream.
- **A client and server crate split:** the mobile bindings must not link a web server, and the client gets TLS through an explicit feature, not through feature unification with pubky's dependencies.
- **A formal model:** state the at-most-once invariant and its assumptions in Section 13 (deadlines derived exactly, a restored journal never read as "not started", transitions durable before they are signed, service time that never goes back, `failed` only on authoritative evidence); model claims, the journal and recovery in Quint or TLA+; and export the model's traces as conformance tests.
- **Conformance:** freeze the vectors, so that a regenerated diff counts as a wire change; add boundary vectors for rules pinned only by text; build a second implementation on a crypto stack other than Rust's; run Wycheproof and the Ed25519 speccheck cases; fuzz the prototype against the independent implementation; and build a rejection corpus by mutating valid vectors, first the security bindings, the UUID form and the integer bound, then the rest, negative `stale_key` cases included. Releases are gated on it.
- **Fail-closed code:** no `unwrap`, `expect`, indexing or unchecked arithmetic in the protocol crates, enforced by Clippy, and fuzz targets for the parsers, times and Billing Period arithmetic.
- **Faults against real parts:** crash and failover tests against real storage, an approval end to end through Pubky auth, per-app grants enforced by a testnet Homeserver, and an on-device test of the iOS notification extension, with its choice of entry to show, a feed view of entries only and a fixed generic relay payload.
- **Bindings:** only the Rust core parses and verifies signed bodies; Swift and Kotlin never parse them again. CI builds every binding (`build.sh all`) and checks the bindings' diff.
- **The external crypto audit's scope:** the subset of hpke 0.14 that Paykit uses, strict Ed25519 verification with ed25519-dalek 3 and curve25519-dalek 5, and the approval's companion-claim channel.
- **CI:** the test job runs a PostgreSQL 16 service and sets `PAYKIT_E2E_REQUIRED=1` (2026-11-03), so the end-to-end tests run there and fail rather than skip if the database is missing.
