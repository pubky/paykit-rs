# Paykit v0.2 specification

Draft. The conditions for freezing and releasing this specification are in [Section 18](#18-conformance-and-release). The [implementation table](implementation.md) identifies the checks and remaining limits of the published wallet.

**Release requirements.** A released wallet must enforce the record rules in Section 6 and wire Section 5, including least-permissive states after deletion, continuity of a user confirmation and a service cursor acknowledgment held until its protective write applies. A new usable record requires authenticated evidence at application that its key holds neither a value nor a retained delete. Later readers must be able to establish which creation the current record descends from. Section 6 specifies the available path for a previously issued one-time payee receipt. Other positive paths requiring unavailable evidence stay withheld. Verify each supported path with positive and rejection cases before release.

**Built on the Pubky Switch.** Paykit v0.2 is a protocol on the Pubky Switch, an inbox and coordination service for Pubky identities specified on its own in [`spec/spec.md`](https://github.com/pubky/pubky-switch/blob/5f3209aae28d1b538a31c8ce43506b39df828b61/spec/spec.md) ("the Pubky Switch spec") and [`spec/wire-format.md`](https://github.com/pubky/pubky-switch/blob/5f3209aae28d1b538a31c8ce43506b39df828b61/spec/wire-format.md) ("the Switch wire format") of the pubky-switch repository, at commit `5f3209aae28d1b538a31c8ce43506b39df828b61`. The Switch gives Paykit its identities and `identity.json`, app certificates and records, receive keys, Shared Data Keys and key bundles, the sealed and signed message, admission, claims and shared records, push, export and import, and identity management. This spec defines what Paykit adds on top: its scopes and the switch permissions each needs, its message kinds, its collections and their policies, the payment flows, Allowances, recurring requests, labelled selling scopes and the retention of payment evidence. Where Paykit relies on a rule of the Switch, it cites it as "Pubky Switch spec, Section N" or "Switch wire N". Switch citations use this commit unless they explicitly name another revision. Throughout this specification and its accompanying documents, the generic protected-origin, lineage and retention contracts, including their proof formats in Switch wire 6.9, refer to Switch commit `f0bbddd15c7ecb1ec670f263f36dd4f2373dba8f`. The current Switch has no such contracts. Paykit paths that require them remain unavailable pending a mapping on the current Switch and its implementation. A later Switch change applies only once a Paykit specification revision names it here. Sections 6 and 8.4.1 identify the positive paths that remain withheld.

**Invitation delivery.** All invitation-specific rules and citations in this specification and its wire format use [Pubky Switch spec, Section 7](https://github.com/pubky/pubky-switch/blob/6ef258d1ada96db7637394b57307335cb802b461/spec/spec.md) and the invitation-specific definitions in [Switch wire 6.2, 6.5, 7.1, 7.3, 7.4.3, 7.4.6, 7.5 and 10.3](https://github.com/pubky/pubky-switch/blob/6ef258d1ada96db7637394b57307335cb802b461/spec/wire-format.md), at commit `6ef258d1ada96db7637394b57307335cb802b461`. This scoped reference leaves the other Switch rules at the revisions named above. A sender may present an invite code as Switch transport metadata when delivering a Paykit message. The code and the authenticated hash reported for its delivery are outside the signed Paykit body. Retry by content keeps the same sealed envelope and signed Paykit message. The retained entry identifies the invitation redeemed even if a later signed HTTP request presents a different code. They grant no selling scope, payment approval or authority over a referenced request. The receiving app still checks the signed message and any business reference under Sections 3.3, 3.4 and 7.2. The SDK accepts an optional invitation when sending a payment request and reports the authenticated delivery reference through its ordinary receive report. The [implementation table](implementation.md) links the enrolled wallet cases. This adds no Paykit format or generated vector.

## Terms

Paykit uses the terms of the Pubky Switch spec (its Terms): identity, signer, app, `identity.json`, scope, Shared Data Key, inbox, feed, claim, shared record, operation and the rest. Paykit's own:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Paykit App** (app) | An app of the Switch acting for an identity in Paykit: one application with its own keys, certificate and Paykit scopes, at least one. Its certificate may also hold other protocols' scopes (Section 3.2). An app may run on several devices; Paykit treats it as one app (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.1). |
| **Version** | Besides the versions of the Switch, Private Payment Lists have versions of their own (Section 5). |
| **Billing Period** | One period of a recurring request, computed as in Section 8.2. |
| **Executor** | The one app that an Allowance record names, with the app's own signed grant, to pay automatically under that Allowance (Section 8.3). |

## 1. Goals and product restrictions

- **Goals:**
  - Payments between Pubky identities; Pubky is the only identity layer required.
  - About one round trip per action, independent of contact count or history size.
  - Several apps per identity, isolated by scope, sharing only what their scopes allow.
  - Payment-method agnostic: the protocol never interprets rails, addresses, amounts' meaning, or proofs.
  - Feature agnostic: the switch offers generic primitives (claims and shared records). Features such as Allowances are built in apps on those primitives.
  - An identity can move to another provider without the old operator's cooperation.
- **Product restriction:**
  - v0.2 has **no unsolicited first contact by Pubky ID alone**.
  - A sender must be on the recipient's allowlist: a contact, or a conversation the recipient started or invited, for example at a checkout.
  - Strangers can't message without an invite. An identity can hand out invite codes, for example a shop's QR code, and, where the user allows it, strangers can ask to connect (Pubky Switch spec, Section 7). Messaging without either is deferred.

## 2. Components

Paykit runs on the components of the Pubky Switch (Pubky Switch spec, Section 2): the signer, the identity's apps, the Homeserver, the Pubky Switch and the push relay.

- **A Paykit App** keeps its own keys, scopes, local data, outbox and cursor, as every app does, and its payment journal. Each app manages its own contacts. Bitkit uses Pubky.app profiles for display.
- **The Homeserver** also holds the records of Paykit apps with public payment endpoints (Section 4).

**Protocol ownership and lifecycle.** The reference client's `ProtocolSet` owns scope roots, message kinds, collections and local state by protocol. `App::join`, `App::load` and `App::restore` require the registered owners of every own-certificate scope root. Section 3.2 gives the requirements for a host that registers more than one protocol (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 3.2, 3.3 and 8.3).

**Callbacks and shared observations.** Messages, notices and collection records follow each protocol's durable order. A shared deny-list change or gap waits for every protocol to reach it and changes the shared copy only when every owner has taken it. Failed callbacks remain owed. Claims and records are current authenticated views, not snapshots proving another protocol handled them; writes use their conditions. Current certificate authority, keys, service time and reconcile/provider-loss restrictions apply immediately (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.3). Paykit must not infer cross-protocol order or regain withdrawn authority from an older message.

**Duties and host events.** The client exposes inspection of pending protocol calls and duty events, durable event ownership and per-protocol acknowledgment. Inspection consumes no duty. Paykit keeps the local consequence before acknowledging its duty, tolerates repeated delivery, and cannot acknowledge another protocol's work. When one event has several owners it remains owed to each until that owner has kept its consequence. An app-wide host acknowledgment is valid only after every affected owner has done so (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 8.2, 8.3, 11 and 12.4).

The wallet app supplies the signer at launch. Its platforms share a Rust signer core. An integrating app chooses how to keep and share contact names, pictures and notes. Paykit uses the Switch allowlist and deny list for payment messaging. The Switch inbox carries the sealed messages (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 2 and 7).

## 3. Scopes and authorization

### 3.1 Scopes

Paykit's scopes are names under the Switch's scope rule (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.3): a name, or a name and a sub-scope. Each Paykit App's certificate lists the Paykit scopes it holds:

| Scope | Allows | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| `paykit_pay` | Act as payer. Send acceptance, rejection, payer cancellation, proof, Private Payment List requests and resend requests. Receive requests, period details, payee cancellations, receipts, Private Payment Lists and resend requests. Read and write claims. Read and write `payer_activity`, `payment_lists` and `block_floor` | `paykit_contacts` |
| `paykit_request` | Act as payee. Send requests, period details, payee cancellations, receipts, Private Payment Lists and resend requests. Receive acceptance, rejection, payer cancellations, proofs, Private Payment List requests and resend requests. Read and write `payee_activity` and `private_list_publications`. Publish, renew and withdraw Private Payment Lists | `paykit_settlements`, `paykit_contacts` |
| `paykit_request:<label>` | Act as payee for requests under this label. Send requests, period details, payee cancellations and receipts for those sales. Receive the payer's replies and read and write `payee_activity` only for this label. Send and receive resend requests under Section 9 | `paykit_settlements`, `paykit_contacts` |
| `paykit_settlements` | Read and write `settlements`, the marks of the settlements the identity's payee apps have credited (Section 6, Settlement marks) | - |
| `paykit_recovery` | Read and publish the original apps’ recovery reports (wire 5.10) | - |
| `paykit_allowance` | Receive `allowance_*` messages and resend requests for them; send `allowance_end`; send `executor_notice` to an own executor; with `paykit_contacts`, also send proposals, acceptances, rejections and resend requests (Outbound authorization, below); read and write `allowance_proposals` and `allowances`; read `allowance_ledgers` and claims | - |
| `paykit_allowance_execute` | Act as an Allowance executor; receive `executor_notice`; create the Allower's original zero ledger at acceptance and update it under Section 8.3 | `paykit_allowance`, `paykit_pay`, `paykit_contacts` |
| `paykit_contacts` | Read the contacts, conversation entries and deny list at the switch | - |
| `paykit_manage_contacts` | Change the contacts and the deny list | `paykit_contacts` |
| `paykit_ext:<prefix>` | Receive the extension kinds `<prefix>.<name>`; with `paykit_contacts`, also send them (Section 3.3; Outbound authorization, below) | - |

- A certificate lists every scope explicitly, including required ones. The signer never issues a scope without the scopes it requires. Paykit's readers, apps and payers, also refuse a certificate that lacks a scope another requires, or holds more than 8 extension scopes or a malformed one (such as `paykit_ext:paykit` or `paykit_ext:cap`, whose prefixes don't start with `paykit-`), or more than 8 labelled selling scopes; they ignore other names they don't know, which a later version may add, and other protocols' scopes (Section 3.2). The signer refuses an app request naming a scope it doesn't know (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 3.1 and Switch wire 3.9).
- **`paykit_pay` and `paykit_allowance_execute` require `paykit_contacts`,** so that every app that pays reads the deny list and pauses payment to a counterparty on it (Section 8.1). A payer app that can't read the deny list would pay a counterparty its user blocked without telling the user. The cost is that every paying app can read the identity's contacts, conversation entries and deny list, as every v0.1 app could.
- **`paykit_request` and every labelled selling scope require `paykit_settlements`,** so that every payee app, a labelled one included, reads and writes the one mark of each settlement the identity credits, and a settlement one own app has credited is credited by no other, as in v0.1, where the apps of an identity shared one state ("A single payment must not satisfy multiple requests or periods", `specs/payment-conversion.md`, "Payment Conversion Quote"; Section 8.1 step 4). A bucket of marks has its record key derived from its policy hash (Section 6), so one key for every label needs one policy for every label, and only a scope that every payee app holds can seal it: a label key opens only its own label's records (Section 3.5). The cost is that a labelled app reads every bucket: it learns how many settlements the identity has credited, when, and by which own app, whose certificate names its label, and, for a settlement it can name, whether it was credited; never a counterparty, an amount, or a settlement it can't name (Section 12). And a faulty or compromised labelled app can drop or plant any label's marks, or make buckets unreadable, so that the other payee apps credit only by hand (Section 13).
- **`paykit_request` and every labelled selling scope require `paykit_contacts` too,** so every app that receives as payee holds the identity's lists and keeps a block as a payer app does: a sender the service has flagged, or a message accepted before a block, creates no state in it, and an app holding plain `paykit_request` pauses its lists to a blocked counterparty. The cost: a selling app, a hosted one such as paykit-server included, reads the identity's contacts, conversation entries, deny list and the senders the service flags (Section 12); and a payee app that can't read the lists takes no new activity until it can, a payment without a request at a hosted app included (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1).
- **Outbound authorization:** sending an extension message, an `allowance_proposal`, `allowance_acceptance`, `allowance_rejection` or any `resend_request` requires explicit `paykit_contacts` as well as the kind's other sender scopes (Section 3.3). The sender then applies the deny-copy rules of Section 8.1: missing scope, an unknown copy and a known block each prevent signing or queueing the send. Missing scope is reported to the host as `MissingScope`, naming `paykit_contacts`, so the host can offer an explicit app-authorization request to the signer; the app never grants or requests the scope on its own. A valid certificate with only an extension or Allowance scope still receives its authorized kinds and is not rejected for lacking contacts. Its receipt permission does not authorize these sends. `lists.read` obtained through another protocol does not substitute for `paykit_contacts` (Section 3.2). The same requirement applies to queued delivery and to re-sends, including answers to recovery requests: loss of the scope holds existing queued messages, reports the missing scope and preserves their normal expiry; it does not release them without a deny check. The user's initial `allowance_end` remains authorized by `paykit_allowance` alone and follows Section 8.3's precise stop rules; an `executor_notice` between own apps likewise needs no contacts scope. An end generated to reflect a crossing response is still recorded at once, with its own-app notice, but its message waits for contacts authorization and a permitting deny copy (Section 8.3, Ending). These exceptions grant no permission for a re-send to a counterparty without contacts. Recovery marks awaiting authorization follow the ordinary bounds in Section 9.
- **Extension scopes:** one scope `paykit_ext:<prefix>`, for example `paykit_ext:paykit-cap`, covers every message kind whose name starts with that prefix. It is Paykit's own: it has no keys, and its bound is Paykit's. An app narrows it to the kinds it handles with its record's `accepts` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.4), which it signs itself, so a new extension kind needs no new certificate and no prompt at the signer. A certificate holds at most 8 extension scopes. A prefix always starts with `paykit-`, as `paykit-cap` does, so its kinds are Paykit's under the Switch's rule: a kind's prefix is its protocol's name, or that name followed by `-` and more characters (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.3). `paykit_ext:paykit` and `paykit_ext:cap` are malformed (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Sections 3.1 and 4.3).
- **Labelled selling scopes:** `paykit_request:<label>`, for example `paykit_request:shop1`, lets a hosted selling app, such as paykit-server, act as payee for its own sales only. The label is 1 to 32 characters of `a-z`, `0-9` and `-`, starting with a letter or digit, the syntax of a sub-scope (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.3), and, unlike an extension scope's prefix, needs no `-`; a certificate holds at most 8. Payers and the switch see the label, so it should be opaque, like `shop1`, never a name (Pubky Switch spec, Section 15). Labelled selling scopes are one use of the scope rule above: plain `paykit_request` covers every label, so an app holding it reads and writes every label's records and gets the payers' replies about every label's requests, and the identity's own wallets show all its sales. So a certificate holds `paykit_request` or labels, never both: the signer never issues one with both, it refuses an app request naming both, and every reader refuses such a certificate. A labelled app reads and writes only its label's `payee_activity` records (Section 6), under its label's keys (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.5), and gets the payers' replies only about its label's requests (Section 3.4). It has the Switch permissions of `paykit_request`. Only the plain selling scope authorizes an app to publish a Private Payment List or answer a Private Payment List request.

### 3.2 Switch permissions

- **Switch permissions** that each Paykit scope needs, for the certificate's `permissions` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2). This table is Paykit's: the switch enforces the signed list and never derives it. What each permission lets an app do is the Switch's (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2).

  | Switch permission | Given with |
  |---|---|
  | `claims.read` | `paykit_pay`, `paykit_allowance` |
  | `claims.write` | `paykit_pay` |
  | `lists.read` | `paykit_contacts` |
  | `lists.manage` | `paykit_manage_contacts` |
  | `conversation.add` | `paykit_pay`, `paykit_request`, any labelled selling scope, `paykit_allowance`, any extension scope |

- **Paykit's verifiers check the list.** The signer sets `permissions` from the scopes, using the table above for Paykit's and each other protocol's table for its own (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2). Paykit's verifiers, at apps and at payers, derive again from Paykit's scopes the list they need, and require every permission of it. When the certificate holds no other protocol's scope, they also require `permissions` to be exactly that list, and refuse a certificate whose `permissions` are any other list (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 3.1). A change to that table therefore needs a new certificate version (`v`).
- **Several protocols in one certificate.** A certificate may hold the scopes of other protocols beside Paykit's, so one app, such as a wallet that also chats, takes part in each of them under one App ID (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2). A scope's root is its name, the part before any `:`, and each root belongs to one protocol: a protocol's roots are its name, or its name followed by `_` or `-` and more characters (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.3). Paykit's roots are therefore `paykit` and every root that is `paykit_` or `paykit-` followed by at least one more character, the names of Section 3.1 among them. A scope with one of these roots is Paykit's, also one Paykit gives no meaning, such as `paykit_pay:x`, a plain `paykit_ext`, a plain `paykit` or a root such as `paykit_future`, which it ignores and which needs no permission. A scope with any other root is another protocol's, such as `chat`, and so are `pay` and `request`, which Paykit gives no meaning. So:
  - Paykit's verifiers require every permission Paykit's scopes need, whatever the other protocols' scopes beside them; the list may then hold more, the other protocols' permissions.
  - Paykit's scope rules (Section 3.1) apply in full to its own scopes, and its bounds, 8 extension scopes and 8 labelled selling scopes, count only them.
  - Another protocol's scopes, and the switch permissions they bring, authorize nothing in Paykit: each Paykit message still needs the Paykit scope Section 3.3 names for it, and an app whose certificate holds no Paykit scope isn't a Paykit App and sends and receives no Paykit kind.
- **Own-app lifecycle.** Public Paykit join, load and restore paths must support a valid own certificate containing Paykit and other protocols. The host supplies all registered owners through a protocol set or a constructed Switch app; the SDK retains the Paykit owner and every other owner and validates Paykit authority without requiring foreign roots to belong to Paykit. Missing owners are refused before installing partial state. Registration does not grant a foreign protocol authority over Paykit kinds, collections, local state or duties. Several devices sharing this App ID still obey the Switch's shared-state and cursor synchronization rule (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 3.1, 3.2 and 8.3).

### 3.3 Message kind to scope

| Kind | Sender needs | Recipient app needs |
|---|---|---|
| `payment_request`, `payment_period_details`, `payment_receipt` | `paykit_request` or the signed request's labelled selling scope under Section 3.4 | `paykit_pay` |
| `payment_request_acceptance`, `payment_request_rejection`, `payment_proof` | `paykit_pay` | `paykit_request` or the request's labelled selling scope under Section 3.4 |
| `private_payment_list` (including a withdrawal) | plain `paykit_request` | `paykit_pay` |
| `private_payment_list_request` | `paykit_pay` | plain `paykit_request` |
| `payment_request_cancellation` | the payer's `paykit_pay`, or the payee's selling scope for the request under Section 3.4 | the other one |
| `allowance_proposal`, `allowance_acceptance`, `allowance_rejection` | `paykit_allowance` and `paykit_contacts` | `paykit_allowance` |
| `allowance_end` | `paykit_allowance` | `paykit_allowance` |
| `executor_notice` (own apps only) | `paykit_allowance` | `paykit_allowance_execute` |
| `resend_request` | `paykit_contacts`: every current Paykit App of the counterparty holding it may send the request, whatever other kinds it handles | Any Paykit App; the answer re-sends only kinds the requesting app's certificate receives, and a payer's replies only under the label rule of Section 3.4 (Section 9) |
| An extension kind `<prefix>.<name>` | `paykit_ext:<prefix>` and `paykit_contacts` | `paykit_ext:<prefix>` |

- **Labelled selling scopes** send and receive the payee's request-related messages under Section 3.4. A `payment_request` carries the signed `label` of the scope it is sent under, and recipients drop one whose sender holds neither `paykit_request` nor `paykit_request:<label>` for its label, and one without a label whose sender doesn't hold `paykit_request`. So an app holding only labelled scopes sends every request under one of its labels. The payer's acceptance, rejection, cancellation and proof copy the request's label (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.3).
- **Extension kinds** carry no Paykit payment semantics: no payment relies on one, they carry no destination, and no Paykit collection records one. An app that handles one passes it to its extension as it arrives, and durably keeps it until the extension takes it or the atomic eviction rule below drops it; an app that doesn't know it ignores it, like any kind it doesn't know (Switch wire 5.1, check 10). An extension message gives its sender no more than a Paykit message would: at an app holding `paykit_contacts`, one from a sender the user blocked is dropped as one never processed and asked for again once the sender is unblocked, as a Paykit message is (Section 8.1), one from a sender the service flags is counted and not passed on, and while the app's copy of the deny list isn't known it waits, as a message that creates state does; an app without `paykit_contacts` takes it as such an app takes any message (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1; Section 17, question 27). An app keeps a bounded number of extension messages not yet taken, per sender and in all (the reference wallet uses 64 per sender and 128 total). Receiving one is a local transaction: the eviction order below includes the incoming message as well as those already kept. If a message already kept goes, forgetting its processed pair, counting its drop in the sender's mark, removing its payload and keeping the incoming message commit together. If the incoming message goes, its drop is counted in that same transaction without marking it processed or keeping its payload; its re-sent copy can be taken. Copies of a message whose local receive already committed, including copies stored later, are duplicates of that same `(sender, msg_id)` and must not prevent that transaction; this requires the atomic receive outcomes in Section 8.1. The eviction does not discard a message whose effect is still unknown. If the required earlier victim is protected, the incoming message is not admitted: the app requests the client admission wait of Section 8.1, Waiting for admission, before any receive effects commit. It does not choose a different ordinary victim, keep the incoming extension above either bound, or move an already kept extension into client waiting custody. A local storage failure changes none of this state: the incoming entry remains unprocessed for the client's retry, with later feed entries still handled. Thus no committed extension pool exceeds either bound, and backup and restore validate those same bounds. They are a family of Section 9, Messages dropped for a bound; for them its recovery rule and the atomic local transaction above mean: past a sender's bound the message of that sender its inbox accepted earliest is dropped, and past the bound on all the earliest of the sender holding the most, the lower identity on a tie; the dropped message counts in its sender's mark of the replies kept before their record (Section 8.1), which holds no record to wait for, so it asks as soon as sends to the sender aren't held; no extension message is a receipt, so none is kept past the bound as a counterparty's first receipt of an attempt is. No extension payload is dropped unless its mark and the pair changes needed to take a re-sent copy commit with it. Thus a bound does not hold up the app's feed, and every eviction atomically records its recovery obligation with the payload and pair changes. Recovery is best effort under Section 9: the ordinary bounded mark may expire or be evicted before an ask is delivered, including while the receiver lacks `paykit_contacts`. The app tells its host when such an undelivered obligation is retired; that notice contains no payload and cannot recover it. The evicted payload may therefore be lost permanently. Even a delivered ask does not establish recovery: the sender's retention and re-send limits still apply. Its body follows the JSON rules of every message, the integer rule included, so an extension writes a decimal such as a price as a string, outside the body's opaque `metadata` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.1; `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.3 and Switch wire 1.1).

### 3.4 Authorization rules

Paykit follows the Switch's authorization rules (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.4), checks every kind's sender and recipient scopes in Section 3.3 separately, and narrows the senders and recipients of a payer's replies by label. A recipient drops a message whose sender lacks a required sender scope, even when that sender's certificate is valid for receiving the kind:

- **Senders.** A payer sends its acceptance, rejection, cancellation and proof for a labelled request only to the payee's apps holding `paykit_request` or that label, and for an unlabelled request, or a payment without one, only to those holding `paykit_request`. So a hosted app never receives the payer's replies about the identity's own requests. A payee's cancellation carries no label and goes only to the payer's apps holding `paykit_pay`: a cancellation's recipient holds the other role than its sender (Section 3.3).
- **Recipients** also drop a payer's reply that the rule for senders above wouldn't have sent them: an acceptance, rejection, cancellation or proof with a label, unless they hold `paykit_request` or that label, and one without a label unless they hold `paykit_request`. A cancellation without a label from an app holding `paykit_request` or a labelled selling scope may be the payee's, and passes at an app holding `paykit_pay` instead; `paykit_pay` to `paykit_pay` and `paykit_request` to `paykit_request` are dropped.
- **Payee messages about a request.** For a payee cancellation, Period Details or a receipt, match its signed request reference and payee identity to the original signed request. The original sender's verified certificate must hold plain `paykit_request` or `paykit_request:<label>` for the request's signed label. An unlabelled request and a receipt for a payment without a request require plain `paykit_request`. Another app of the same payee may act under the same label or the plain scope. The three messages carry no label of their own. The same rule applies on first receipt, when placing a held message, and when reopening a message from shared activity. Redelivery uses the original sender's certificate. Details outside that authority cannot supply destinations or rates, a receipt outside it cannot settle an attempt or establish credit, and a cancellation outside it cannot end the request.
- **Before the request can be read.** Apply the existing custody, admission and bounds of Section 8.1. When the parent is unavailable, a signer with labelled selling scopes and no plain `paykit_request` cannot yet establish authority for that request. A retained cancellation still pauses any new attempt on an unreadable request. Once the authenticated request is available, apply only a cancellation whose original certificate covers its label. Preserve potentially applicable stops from different sending apps until each can be judged, so one app's stop cannot displace another's. For competing applicable stops, place the earliest in inbox acceptance order. The request's existing cancellation ends the need to retain those copies.
- **Private Payment Lists.** A payer wraps a list request only to eligible apps holding plain `paykit_request`. When the request names an app, only that app may answer. A selling app with only labelled scopes does not advertise, accept or answer list requests. A payer drops a list, including an empty withdrawal, whose signing certificate lacks plain `paykit_request`. It preserves the installed list and its version. An app with `paykit_pay` may ask for and receive lists as payer whatever its selling scopes.
- **Homeserver grants.** Paykit uses the Switch grant for the app's record and, if the app keeps its backup on the Homeserver, its backup. Paykit adds no paths of its own. Other protocols may request paths under the Switch's authorization rules (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.4).
- **Existing Homeserver grants.** Before the first v0.2 `identity.json` is published, an app must have no live grant that lets it write a v0.2 path outside its authority, such as the signer's identity document or another app's record. If such a grant exists, the account owner or its authorized host revokes or narrows it at the Homeserver. A v0.1 grant limited to `/pub/paykit/` does not cover the Switch identity, app record or backup paths.

### 3.5 Keys of Paykit's scopes

- **Label keys.** A labelled selling scope `paykit_request:<label>` is a sub-scope of `paykit_request` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.5): its keys, the label keys, derive from the `paykit_request` key. Which scopes have keys is Paykit's: its scopes of Section 3.1 and their labels do, extension scopes don't, and nor does any other sub-scope of a Paykit scope (such as `paykit_pay:x`), which Paykit ignores (wire 3.1).
- **`paykit_settlements`** has keys of its own, as every scope of Section 3.1 does. It isn't a sub-scope of `paykit_request`, so a labelled app holds its keys beside its label keys, and never the `paykit_request` key (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.5).
- **`paykit_recovery`** has its own scope key. The signer grants it on enrollment or ordinary certificate renewal. It adds no Switch permission. A contacts-only app can sign its own historical report and give the signed report to an authorized publisher. Reading the reports and publishing a report both require the recovery grant.
- Extension scopes have no scope keys: no shared record holds an extension kind, so key bundles and the approval carry keys for the other scopes only (Switch wire 3.5 and 3.10).

## 4. App records

A Paykit App's record is the Switch's app record (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.2). Its `accepts` lists Paykit's kinds, and its Paykit member, `protocols.paykit`, holds its `capabilities` and `public_endpoints` (wire 3.2). These rules are Paykit's, and the switch checks none of them:

- **Optional public deadline.** The publishing app may choose a deadline for its public endpoints or publish them without a Paykit deadline. This uses the existing signed app-record field `expires_at`. The record carries the earliest deadline requested by any of the app’s protocols, or `null` when none requests one. The signed deadline applies to the whole record. Renewing the record cannot extend a fixed public payment cutoff chosen by the publisher (wire 3.2). At dispatch the wallet also checks that the endpoint remains usable under its payment method. Private Payment Lists retain their required expiry under Section 8.4 and wire 4.13.
- **Record age limit.** The identity signer sets the maximum age of an app record in `identity.json` (`record_max_age` in seconds, 90 days when absent). A payer may use a signed public record only while its device clock and the highest service time it has verified are both strictly before `updated_at + record_max_age` and, for a finite `expires_at`, strictly before that time too (Section 7.2). It refuses a record with `updated_at` more than 5 minutes ahead of its device clock (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.2). On its next run past half the effective lifetime, the publisher renews the record (wire 3.2). For example, with no deadline and the default age limit, an otherwise valid endpoint can remain payable after day 30, but the unrenewed record is unusable at day 90. A publisher that stays offline beyond its trust window must publish a fresh record before its public destinations can be used again.
- **`capabilities.endpoint_identifiers`:** the Payment Endpoint Identifiers the app can pay to, so payees can offer matching destinations.
- **Public payments are optional.** An app may leave `public_endpoints` empty. Only an app holding `paykit_request` itself publishes public endpoints: a payment to one carries no label (Section 8.4, Unlabelled), so its proof reaches only the payee's apps holding `paykit_request`, and an app holding only labelled selling scopes would never see it. A payer reads at most 256 endpoints of an identity (below), so an app publishes no more than that. The identity's public Payment List is the `public_endpoints` of every current app, each listed under its app; payers may use any of them. An identity whose records list none has no public Payment List.
- **Public endpoints are read from the Homeserver.** They are public, non-interactive data: anyone reads them without authentication or the switch, and they stay readable while the switch is down or the identity is moving to another provider. A payer also compares the Homeserver's `identity.json` with the switch's copy, in parallel and waiting on it at most a short grace, and pays without the switch's answer only as Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3 allows.
- **Reading a payee's public endpoints:** the payer reads the record of each app current in the payee's `identity.json`, in that document's order, and keeps the usable endpoints of each. One app's failure, a record it can't read, or one it refuses or that has expired (Section 7.2), is reported as that app's, and the other apps' usable endpoints stay available, as in v0.1 (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Resolve Public Payment"); an app with no record has no public endpoints. The read takes at most 256 endpoints and 4 MiB of payloads over all the apps, as in v0.1: an app whose endpoints don't fit in what is left is reported as over the bound, never cut to fit. A request with `required_app_id` reads only that app's record, so that app's failure leaves the request with no public destination (Section 8.1).
- **Payers check every destination's app** before paying (Section 7.2): the app that published a public endpoint, or sent the request, period details or Private Payment List, must be current in its identity's `identity.json` with the certificate it presented (listed, or renewed and still counting, Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2); that certificate must hold plain `paykit_request` for public endpoints and Private Payment Lists, or the selling scope required for the request or period details (Section 3.3). A listed certificate must not have expired. A public record must still be within its effective trust window and a Private Payment List within its signed lifetime (Sections 5 and 7.2). A removed app's destinations are ignored, even if its record is still on the Homeserver.
- **Withdrawing a destination:** publishing a withdrawal removes the destination from the current record. A cached public copy can outlive the withdrawal within its trust window. A stale Private Payment List can likewise survive until its expiry. A payee whose destination is compromised therefore removes it and also revokes the app, or replaces it with a new app (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.2), so that payers stop paying to it once their 10-minute copy of `identity.json` shows the app removed.
- **What is public:** an app publishes its record on the Homeserver when it has public endpoints, or when another protocol it takes part in needs public data (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.2). Anyone can read a published record whole: its accepted kinds, its certificate with its scopes and permissions, and its Paykit member with the app's capabilities, also when its `public_endpoints` is empty. An app that publishes no record on the Homeserver appears publicly only as an app ID and certificate hash in `identity.json`, so anyone can see how many apps an identity has, but not what they are. Admitted senders get the full records from the directory.
- **Fresh before paying:** an app fetches or revalidates its own identity's `identity.json` in parallel with each `attempt`, unless it did so in the last 60 s. It moves money only when it holds such a copy and that copy names the service whose key signed the attempt's transition proof (Section 7.2); a payment made while its own switch doesn't answer, which has no attempt yet, needs a copy naming the service the app uses (Section 8.4). If the Homeserver is unreachable for longer, the app makes no new attempts. At dispatch, the age of the current version, measured from the start of the Homeserver request that supplied it, must be at least zero and strictly less than 60 seconds. If the app observes its clock move backward during or after that read, it discards the observation. It must obtain a fresh Homeserver observation before dispatch, even if the clock returns to its earlier reading. An open event stream doesn't replace this check, because a stalled stream looks the same as a quiet one.
- **App storage.** Besides what every app keeps (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.1), a Paykit App keeps Paykit's state: derived state such as Allowance budgets, its payment journal and the Private Payment Lists it publishes.

For the outer app-record body used in Section 4 and wire 3.2, Paykit adopts only the host-local display-name change in Pubky Switch revision `250cd60e98eecb2a0357e55257d5b0dad2d583a4` ([Switch spec 4.2](https://github.com/pubky/pubky-switch/blob/250cd60e98eecb2a0357e55257d5b0dad2d583a4/spec/spec.md#42-app-record), [Switch wire 3.2](https://github.com/pubky/pubky-switch/blob/250cd60e98eecb2a0357e55257d5b0dad2d583a4/spec/wire-format.md#32-app-record)). The host keeps an app's display name in local state; the signed outer record has no display-name field. This scoped adoption does not replace the Switch baseline for other rules.

For the identity checks of Sections 4 and 7.2, Paykit adopts the source and timing rules for reading an identity document in Pubky Switch revision `63e8a9232ba6ff8d3e4e6508e892a64073a137da`. They apply to the payer's own Homeserver revalidation, the source of the payee's document and the payee's public service lookup, including a lookup across a service move. See [Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3](https://github.com/pubky/pubky-switch/blob/63e8a9232ba6ff8d3e4e6508e892a64073a137da/spec/spec.md#43-identityjson) and [Switch wire, Sections 7.1 and 7.4.15](https://github.com/pubky/pubky-switch/blob/63e8a9232ba6ff8d3e4e6508e892a64073a137da/spec/wire-format.md#71-requests-and-responses).

**App-record lifetime.** The client can set a protocol member or renew a record with a lifetime measured from the exact `updated_at` it signs. The earliest protocol expiry and the identity's record-age cap apply; changing Paykit's member preserves other protocols' members and expiry policies. The policy and signed bytes persist before delivery, and an exact retry extends no lifetime. A clock rollback requires verified service time before a new signing (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.2; Switch wire 3.2). Section 4 and Paykit wire 3.2 still govern when Paykit publishes, renews or removes its endpoints.

**Renewing a certificate.** Before the app signs its record under a renewed certificate, each protocol gives its member and its expiry ask for that certificate, and the record is signed once with what each gave, the certificate, members and asks committed together; a member that fails validation is removed and the host told (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2). Paykit gives its member without public endpoints when the new certificate lacks `paykit_request`, and without capabilities when it lacks `paykit_pay`, with no expiry ask when no public endpoints remain (Section 4; wire 3.2).

A payer that observes two different valid identity documents at the same version stops acting on that identity, alerts the user and makes no payment to it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3).

## 5. Message kinds

Kind strings are `paykit.` followed by the names below, or extension kinds `<prefix>.<name>` under an extension scope, which carry no Paykit payment semantics (Section 3.3). Historical v0.1 references throughout this specification use [revision 53b8c8cb](https://github.com/pubky/paykit-rs/tree/53b8c8cb2735c0845232fbdf34bc83052bf88404/specs). Message bodies use the v0.1 field definitions in `specs/payment-requests.md` for requests, recurrence, Billing Periods, acceptance, rejection, cancellation, proofs and receipts; `specs/payment-conversion.md` for conversion terms, quotes and deadlines; `specs/allowances.md` for Allowance terms and messages; and `specs/paykit-sdk.md` for Private Payment List versioning. Those files title their protocol "Payment Request Protocol v0.2"; here they are the v0.1 bodies. This spec defines transport, authorization, storage and lifecycle, and changes the bodies as listed below. The exact v0.2 bodies are in `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.

| Kind | Notes |
|---|---|
| `payment_request` | Carries its amount and `payment_reference`, and usually its destination (`payment_endpoints`), which the payer must then use; without one, the payer pays to any of the payee's current endpoints, or only those of the payee app named in `required_app_id` (Section 8.1). A request carries at most one of `payment_endpoints` and `required_app_id`. Optional: proposal expiry, `recurrence`, conversion terms, payment deadline, and `per_period_endpoints` for a recurring request (Section 8.2). |
| `payment_request_acceptance` | One-time request: sent when Section 7.2 step 2 makes it sendable; not awaited. Recurring request: sent once, when the user, or an executor under an Allowance, accepts the subscription, before any payment, and recorded first (Section 8.2). The proposal expiry bounds the acceptance. |
| `payment_request_rejection` | Recorded before it is sent (Section 8.1). |
| `payment_request_cancellation` | From either side. A payer records its cancellation before sending it (Section 8.1). |
| `payment_period_details` | New in v0.2; replaces v0.1's conversion quote. The payee's details for one Billing Period of a recurring request: its `payment_endpoints`, its conversion rates (cross-asset only, as v0.1 quotes), or both, with an `expires_at`. Its rates apply to a payment whose verified payment time is from its `created_at`, cut to whole seconds, through `expires_at`, as a v0.1 quote's do from its `valid_from`. Each stays valid until its own `expires_at`, and the proof references the one it used (Section 8.2). |
| `payment_proof` | References the request and Billing Period, or none for a payment without a request, and the period details or Private Payment List version it paid to. Carries the `attempt_id` it reports and an opaque proof. Required after paying a single-use endpoint (Section 8.4). |
| `payment_receipt` | Signed by the payee; sending it is optional, but a payee app that credits a proof stores its receipt in that record, in the batch that writes the settlement's mark (Section 8.1). References the proof it confirms, and so the attempt. May carry the payee's `metadata`, and, for a payment without a request, a `payment_reference`, as in v0.1. |
| `resend_request` | Asks the counterparty to re-send messages by `msg_id`, request, Billing Period, or a range of acceptance times. Best effort. |
| `private_payment_list` | Latest state per publishing app and recipient identity. Its version is the ordered pair of the publisher's verified identity generation and a per-recipient reservation counter (Section 8.4.1, wire 4.13). Every new publication reserves a greater pair, including after a restore. It carries a required `expires_at`, which the publishing app computes from its current time, not from the version. Payers use the list only while the earlier of `expires_at` and 30 days after their inbox accepted it is later than both of their clocks (Section 7.2), so a stolen app key can't sign a list payable for long. Endpoints are reusable unless marked `single_use` (Section 8.4). |
| `private_payment_list_request` | Asks for the current list and names the version the sender holds. Best effort. |
| `allowance_proposal`, `allowance_acceptance`, `allowance_rejection`, `allowance_end` | The acceptance names the executor app at acceptance, for information; a later handoff doesn't change it. |
| `executor_notice` | New in v0.2, between apps of one identity only. Tells an executor that an Allowance ended (`reason: ended`) or was handed to another app (`reason: handed_off`), naming the Allowance's record key. Its only effect is on the recipient's consent list (Section 8.3). |

- **Differences from the v0.1 bodies and rules:**
  - `required_app_id` is optional and only allowed without `payment_endpoints`, whose owner is always the sending app (`from.app_id`).
  - Dropped: `event_id` and `payment_request_id`, replaced by `msg_id` and `refs`; Encrypted Links, Receipt Access and Delivery Confirmations; the 1000-byte limit; and FIFO message order, which Section 8.1 replaces.
  - `per_period_endpoints`, `payment_period_details` (which replaces the conversion quote and can also carry a period's destination) and `executor_notice` are new. So is `label`: a request sent under a labelled selling scope carries it, and the payer's acceptance, rejection, cancellation and proof copy it (Section 3.3). Private Payment List endpoints may carry `single_use: true`, and a list carries `expires_at`.
  - A payee app tells whether a payment was already credited to another occurrence from the settlement's mark, which a credit writes in the batch that stores its receipt, and only while no mark is there, where v0.1's one shared state held that across an identity's apps (Sections 6, Settlement marks, and 8.1 step 4).
  - Period details may come from any payee app that holds the request's record, a labelled one included, where v0.1 took a quote only from the app that proposed the request (`specs/payment-conversion.md`, "Payment Conversion Quote"): the sending app writes them into the payee's shared period record before it sends them, so every own payee app can check a proof against them (Section 8.2).
  - Billing Periods follow one rule, the anchored period rule of `specs/allowances.md` (Section 8.2), where v0.1 left expansion open.
  - Readers ignore unknown fields in message bodies (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.1), where v0.1 rejected them.
  - A Private Payment List carries a `version` member, the publisher's generation and counter pair (Section 8.4.1), where v0.1's `version` was the format version; and an Allowance proposal carries `allower_generation`, the Allower's verified identity generation (wire 4.15).
  - A second, different acceptance or rejection under one `allowance_id` makes the Allowance conflicted (Section 8.3). v0.1 marks it invalid history instead, which blocks automatic payment until an explicit review (`specs/allowances.md`, "Transport and common rules" and "Acceptance and rejection"). v0.2 has no review call, so the Allowance stays conflicted, and the two identities set up a new one.
- **Size:** the JSON of each kind stored in activity records (Section 6) is at most 16 KiB.
- There is no refund message; a refund is a normal Payment Request in the other direction.

- **Fixed policies:** each collection's records use the policy above. `settlements` uses the Switch's permanent record policy (`"permanent": {"generation": null}`), under which the service refuses a delete (wire 5.1 and 5.7). A request sent under a label has its `payee_activity` records, its request record and for a recurring request its period records, under that label's policy: `read_scope = paykit_request:<label>`, `write_scopes = ["paykit_request:<label>"]`. Apps ignore any record in these collections whose policy differs, and a labelled one whose request, or payer's message, names another label or none, and a fixed one whose request names a label, so no app can plant a record readable under another scope or another label.
- **Different writes at the same activity version.** After a recovery upload, the Switch client can hold one signed write for a `payer_activity` or `payee_activity` record while its next write would build on a different signed operation at that same record version (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 8.2 and 12.6). While the client reports this difference, the app holds any payment, credit or record update that depends on the disputed activity, including through its related request or Billing Period. It retains its local stops and the outcome of a payment already made. It keeps incoming verified messages for retry with their original message IDs under the ordinary intake rules. Unrelated activity can continue.
  The app checks the effective write again before resuming. When the client selects the original signed operation again or accepts a successor, the ordinary activity checks apply to that write. A version alone or a reconstruction from the app's journal cannot settle which signed write is current. A reload retains the comparison. After a backup restore the client reads the records again. If it has no copy of the original write, it has no same-version comparison from that copy.
- **One message per role:** an activity record stores the messages that make up its state, one per role. A request record holds the request, the payer's response (acceptance or rejection) and a cancellation from either side; a period record holds the period details, the proof and the receipt; a one-time request's record holds the request's roles and its proof and receipt; a record of a payment without a request holds its proof and receipt. A later message for a role already held is still processed when it arrives, for example a second proof, which shows a duplicate payment, but isn't stored. A payee record of a payment without a request is keyed by its endpoint and settlement ID (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3). A second proof there replays that occurrence, whose valid prior receipt still records its credit (Section 8.4). The one exception is period details: a period record keeps every details it has held for its period, at most 8 (Section 8.2). The permanent Allowance record holds the matched proposal and acceptance and any later end or conflicting message. Section 8.3.1 governs each pending proposal before acceptance. The kinds activity records store are at most 16 KiB (Section 5), and a record lists only a bounded number of `msg_id`s (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11), so an activity record stays within the 256 KiB limit for a shared record value without any message count, however many messages repeat a role it holds.
- **Deleting finished activity:** an app may delete an activity record (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3) once the user no longer wants it, with these exceptions: a payer never deletes its record of a paid obligation without a deadline (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2), one that carries the proof of its payment, and a payee app never deletes a record that holds a settlement it credited, one that carries its receipt, so that no own app is shown that settlement's proof again over a delete and credits it twice (Section 8.1, Records not listed yet; Section 8.4, Credited once; Section 17, question 35); a payer deletes a record holding `manual_only` only once its request's deadline has passed, by the latest service time it has verified, and never one of a request without a deadline; and it deletes a request record only once its `attempted_periods` lists no period, dropping finished periods first as any write may (Section 8.2, Attempted periods). An own app puts back a record of these deleted all the same (Putting back a deleted record). The user may archive the payer's record, which hides it in the app, but the record stays: the claim is gone 400 days after its last entry (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3), and from then on the record, not the claim, stops a second payment of a re-sent copy (Section 7.2). It stays whole, since a reader ignores a write that drops a message it has seen (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2). The payer's cost is one record per payment without a deadline, and the payee's one record per credited settlement, each kept for the identity's life. A record of an obligation with a deadline may go: its claim, or its tombstone, still shows the payment as done until 30 days after the deadline, after which a later copy can't be paid anyway (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3). A period record's deadline comes from its request, so the payer keeps a recurring request's record while it keeps any of the request's period records; a period record whose request record is missing counts as one without a deadline.
- **Settlement marks:** a settlement's mark records that the identity has credited it, and names the occurrence it was credited to. Marks live in `settlements`, in at most 1,024 bucket records: a settlement's bucket and the tag its mark carries derive from its settlement ID alone, as its payment method names it, whatever the counterparty, the endpoint or the occurrence (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Sections 1.5 and 5.7). A credit adds the settlement's mark to its bucket in the batch that stores the receipt, so of two credits that write a mark for the same settlement, by any own payee apps, labelled or not, to any occurrences, only one applies, and the other fails and decides again (Section 8.1 step 4; wire 5.7, Writing); only a credit explicitly confirmed by hand after the warning below can store its receipt without a mark. Such unmarked credits do not share the one-credit guarantee (Section 13). No app removes or changes a mark, or deletes a bucket, and under the permanent policy the service refuses a bucket's delete (Fixed policies). So, since the most recent provider loss, a bucket's key is either never written or live. An absent bucket supplies no mark of a settlement and leaves the other checks in force: the rollback check below, the receipt check of Section 8.1 step 4 and its provider-loss rule, under which a settlement of a bucket lost with a provider loss is credited only once the app dates its payment after the declaration plus the residual window (Section 7.3) or on its user's confirmation. Under the permanent policy, a bucket an app held live that reads absent or deleted, in the history since the last provider loss, raises the rollback alarm (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1), and the app credits nothing on that absence. A reader that has seen a mark keeps it, and ignores and shows with its writer a later write of its bucket that drops or changes it; it writes the bucket again at once with every mark it has seen, as many as fit in 9,000 (below), over a write that is valid and opens. A mark outlives the activity record that credited it. A claim's tombstone goes 30 days after its deadline or 400 days after its last entry (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3), since a later copy of its obligation can't be paid after that; a settlement has no deadline, and its proof can be shown again at any time, so its mark never goes. A mark takes 21 bytes, 28 in the bucket's JSON, and the buckets take at most 1,024 live record keys. The `payee_activity` record each credit keeps for the identity's life reaches the storage quota and the 100,000 live record keys long before the marks do; 100,000 marks take about 3 MB. A bucket holds at most 9,000 marks, and no write, by hand or not, holds more: a rewrite whose marks don't all fit holds those of the current write it keeps and as many of the app's other seen marks as fit, in ascending byte order, so the bucket is full, and isn't made when it would hold just the current write's marks; the app keeps every mark it has seen in its own state, those that didn't fit too, and tells its host once per bucket (`SettlementBucketFull`); each app goes on taking every mark it has seen as a credit, whatever the bucket shows, and no app credits a settlement of a full bucket automatically, so the apps converge however their seen marks differ (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.7, Over capacity). A credit whose bucket is full, or whose bucket write the service refuses for the storage quota or the value limit, is made only by hand, once its user has been shown that the settlement can't be marked and so may be credited again, and stores its receipt without a mark; the app tells its host once per bucket (`SettlementBucketFull`). A credit, with its mark or by hand without one, is complete only once the batch that stores its receipt applies; when the service refuses that batch for the storage quota or the value limit, also without the bucket's write, nothing is credited and no receipt is sent: the proof stays pending under the stored-entry rules (Section 8.1, Stored entries), and the app tells its host of the refusal (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.7, Complete). While a bucket's current write doesn't open or read, or is invalid, or while its view shows it divergent, every settlement in it may already be credited: no app credits one automatically, and one by hand only once its user has been shown that it may be, and the app tells its host once per settlement (`CreditNeedsUser`). How a bucket is written again over a divergent view or a value that is invalid or doesn't open is `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.7, Seen marks, Repairing and Over capacity.
- **Divergent records:** after a provider loss, an upload of a record that conflicts with a write made at the new service, or two different uploaded values at one version, is kept and listed as divergent until a writer writes a newer version there, and the record's view says so (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6; Switch wire 7.4.9); what to do about a divergent record is the protocol's (Switch wire 7.4.14). An app takes the current write as any other, but the upload may hold what the current write lacks, such as a cancellation or a rejection in a request record, a receipt or period details in an activity record, an end, a rejection or a `conflicted` message in an `allowances` record, entries in an `allowance_ledgers` record, a `manual_only` mark, or a `used` entry or a newer list in a `payment_lists` record. So while the latest view of a request or period record shows it divergent, the app pays or accepts that occurrence automatically never, and by hand only after its user has seen that the payee may have cancelled it, and a payee app credits no proof against it until its user confirms (Section 8.1); a recurring request's request record whose latest view shows it divergent pauses each of its Billing Periods the same way, whatever the period record's view shows, since its upload may hold a cancellation, a `manual_only` mark or a reassociation the current write lacks (Section 8.3), so while it does, no period of the request is paid automatically, and one by hand only after its user has seen that the payee may have cancelled it; and while the latest view of an Allowance's `allowances` or `allowance_ledgers` record shows it divergent, its executor pays nothing automatically under it; and while the latest view of a `payment_lists` record shows it divergent, no app pays to any endpoint of that record's Private Payment List, single-use or reusable, automatically, and by hand only after its user has seen that the list may be stale, since the list is latest state and the upload may hold a higher version, in which the payee may have withdrawn or replaced a destination. A request, or a Billing Period, that no confirmation could let be paid isn't paused while its record, or its request's record, is: one past its deadline, for a Billing Period its own attempt deadline (Section 7.1), is refused as one past its deadline is; one whose destination, the request's `payment_endpoints` or, for a Billing Period paid by period details, those details' (Section 8.2), comes from an app that isn't current in the payee's `identity.json`, read fresh, is refused as the destination check refuses it (Section 7.2); and one with `required_app_id` naming an app that copy lists as removed, which is never listed again (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3), is refused for that; a read of that copy that fails refuses the payment, and none of them names the record. A request requiring an app that copy neither lists nor lists as removed still pauses. An app that has seen a record divergent keeps this pause for the occurrence, the Allowance or the list until its user confirms, also once a newer write has cleared the view. It takes a record as seen divergent whenever a view it read showed it so (Switch wire 7.4.9), also when a later write it read in the same pass of the feed cleared it. The reference client tells the app of each record that turns divergent by a duty event, delivered at least once, kept until the app acknowledges it and raised again, with a new ID, after a restore while the record is still divergent: the app sets or keeps its mark from each such event by the record's key, never by the event's ID, acknowledges it once the mark is kept, or with no mark for a record it no longer holds, on which it can pay, accept or credit nothing, and judges whether the record is divergent now from its view's flag (Switch wire 7.4.9). An app keeps a mark of each record it has seen divergent, with its collection and key, the write it showed, whether its user confirmed it and whether that confirmation showed that the record may hold a credit its current write lacks; a confirmed mark lifts the pause. A payee app keeps the mark of a counterparty's record, once its view has cleared, until its user has given such a confirmation, and until then credits no proof of that counterparty (Section 8.1 step 4). A user's confirmation is of the write the app showed: given for another write than the one the app holds then, it confirms nothing, and the app shows the record again. It holds for that write alone: a later view that shows another write divergent pauses the record again. A second divergent upload beside the same current write can't be told from the first, since the view says only whether the record is divergent (Switch wire 7.4.9), so one confirmation covers both. A confirmation of a record the app no longer holds ends its pause and drops its mark. A mark whose write is later than the write the app holds at that key, or whose collection isn't that key's, matches no write the app holds: the app keeps the record at that key paused until its user confirms the write the app then shows, lifts no other pause by it, and goes on with every other record. A backup carries the marks of divergent records: a restore installs each one unconfirmed, so the restored app pauses every record it had seen divergent until its user confirms again. A mark set after the backup is lost with what the restore loses (Section 7.2); a record still divergent pauses again at its first view after the listing (Section 8.1, Records not listed yet). While a record's view shows it divergent, an app writes over it only a write that adds a stop, a cancellation, a rejection, an end, a `conflicted` message or a `manual_only` mark, a write that puts the record back over a delete (Putting back a deleted record), or settlement marks (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.7), or one its user confirmed having seen what the upload may hold; a receive-step write that adds none of these is kept (Section 8.1, Stored entries; Replies before their record) and written after that confirmation, in the order the inbox accepted the kept messages, and an app's own write of another kind, such as period details or a receipt a payee app stores, waits for that confirmation too. A receipt whose payer record's view shows it divergent, or over whose record messages kept while it was divergent wait to be written, is kept with those messages, with the claim `done` it carries evidence for, whether or not the app can read the record, under the spam rule for a payment without a request when it can't (Section 8.1, Replies before their record), and both are written only once the user has confirmed the record and the record reads; the first receipt from the record's counterparty for each attempt its journal or the claim shows is never dropped for the bound on the messages kept over the record (Section 8.1, Stored entries). A receipt kept as a reply before its record counts, once the record reads paused, as a message kept over it, and waits for that confirmation too. What records a payment already dispatched, a one-time request's acceptance not yet sent, the outcome and the proof, waits while the payer record is paused, that is until its user confirms, and goes once the user has confirmed and the messages kept over the record are written (Section 8.1, Stored entries), also while the view still shows the record divergent (Section 7.2 steps 2 to 4): the rule that the app pays or accepts nothing automatically while the latest view shows a record divergent, confirmed or not, covers what starts or repeats a payment or an acceptance, not what records a payment already made.
- **Writing over a delete:** a write over a record whose current write is a delete, including a delete the app refused, names that delete's version. If the current write came from a recovery upload, the operation also names its exact application in `record_current`. If the service has since forgotten the delete, it refuses the write with a version conflict and the app decides again under the protected-family and no-copy holds below (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 8.2 and 10.3; Switch wire 6.2 and 6.3).
- **Observing a deleted payee activity record.** When an own app learns that another own app deleted a `payee_activity` record, it retains the local evidence and credit restrictions described in wire Section 5. The payee can show an already issued receipt whose signatures and occurrence it can verify. A known credit still counts under Section 8.1 step 4, including after a later write at the same key or an in-place restore from an older backup. Observing the delete does not put the record back or authorize a new credit.
- **Publishing an issued receipt.** An own app that can write the record and has authenticated the complete predecessor of a delete in the same service generation can publish a receipt the payee already issued for a one-time request. It checks the original signed request, proof and receipt and keeps every applicable restriction. The app holds its service cursor below the delete until its protective write applies or it verifies a complete shared successor. A complete successor contains the required signed roles, restrictions and opaque members of both records, including within retained objects. The app publishes any missing compatible data over the exact live application. Conflicting opaque values keep the publication pending. A writer or reader may replace an eager loss marker only with the proof of the same deletion and its complete repair specified in wire Section 5. Devices that share an App ID must hand over its outstanding duties and their evidence before another device uses its cursor. Wire Section 5 specifies the checks. Publishing the existing receipt gives no authority to credit a payment.
- **Putting back a deleted record:** apart from publishing an issued receipt as defined above, the positive write and authorization paths described here are withheld under the incomplete-contract rule in wire Section 5 until their specific guarantees are implemented; the holds and retained restrictions apply meanwhile. A record whose content withholds or bounds a payment, or the credit of one, is one no Paykit App deletes: the families are listed below, each with its least permissive state. Every older copy of a `payee_activity` or `payment_lists` record belongs to its protected family, even if that copy holds no receipt or `used` entry: the deleted write may have added one. An own app that holds such a record, by its own copy, and reads a delete of it that another app wrote never acts on the delete as an end of what the record held. It doesn't take the delete, building on it as on a refused write (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2, Writing over a refused write), and writes the record back at once, over the delete, naming its version (Writing over a delete), before or in the batch that acknowledges the delete to the service. The service-held cursor never reaches or passes that delete until the protective write has applied, including when quota or the value limit refuses the write; a queued write is insufficient. The app keeps the publication obligation durably before moving its local receive position, and may continue local reads only without advancing the service-held cursor past that hold.
  - **Publication before acknowledgment:** the service uses its per-app cursor, not the app's local receive position, to forget deletions (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3; Switch wire 9.1). An app required to put a record back never sends `cursor.set` at or beyond the delete's sequence until a shared write containing every restriction it must preserve has applied: either `record.put` followed by `cursor.set` in one atomic batch, or an applied and verified protective write first (Switch wire 6.4). A write-back by another app qualifies only when it preserves those restrictions (Two apps at once). A queued repair, a local duty acknowledgment or a local cursor move does not qualify. A quota, value-limit or other refusal keeps the service-cursor hold; the app tells its host, keeps the family's least permissive state and retries when it can publish safely. Receive acknowledgment and operations that need it may remain unavailable while publication is blocked; safety does not assume room eventually becomes available. Retry, reload, backup and restore preserve the obligation, and every device sharing the app's service cursor obeys it.
  - **What it writes:** a write-back never restores a state more permissive than the one deleted. The app writes its copy as it holds it only when that copy is the write the delete was built on, the write at the delete's signed `expected_version`, matched by item hash through authenticated feed or chain evidence, not version alone; without that proof, including an upload whose predecessor cannot be authenticated, the copy is treated as older. An app holding the exact predecessor names the deleting app (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2). When its copy is older, after a gap in its feed, a `missed` item or a restore that hasn't caught up (Section 7.2, What a `missed` item loses; What a restore loses), it can't tell what was deleted: it writes the family's least permissive state, built on its copy, saying that the record's content was lost to a delete and naming that delete by its item hash (`lost_to_delete`), names no app, since it can't tell that the delete broke a rule, and tells its user.
  - **Held as deleted:** until a write over the delete lands, an app reads a record it holds as deleted as the family's least permissive state, whatever its copy.
  - **Two apps at once:** two own apps that put one record back at once both build on the same current delete, and one write lands. The other reads the write that landed and takes it when it withholds everything its own write would; otherwise it writes over it, at its version, the two merged, which withholds what either does (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5, Put back over a delete). A reader that has seen more of the record than a write-back carrying `lost_to_delete` holds merges over it the same way, naming nobody, rather than ignoring it for what it lacks. A merge only adds what withholds, so no write-back answers another more than once.
  - **Again and again:** each delete is put back. An app names a deleting app once, and its user is told once per deleting app, whatever the number of deletes, and once per delete a write-back from an older copy answers, which that write-back names. Apart from that, an app asks its user to confirm the record again whenever its confirmation ends, which a missed write ends too. A user's confirmation binds the item hash and version of the write shown, and advances only when authenticated feed or chain evidence proves that exact write was the successor's predecessor. It never covers the loss of a later delete: it ends at the first delete, missed write, unproven predecessor or other value the app reads, even if a version is reused, and at a restore (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5, Confirming a loss).
  - **Visible deletion without a copy:** an app that reads an authenticated delete but holds no copy cannot tell what the deleted value withheld. It therefore treats every deleted payer activity key (request or period), payee activity key and `payment_lists` key as potentially protected, regardless of a replay's content. The `block_floor` family retains its delete protection. The service refuses deletion of permanent Allowance records and ledgers (Section 8.3.1). The app durably keeps the delete's identity and the hold before advancing its local read position; listing, restart, backup and restore do not turn that known deletion into absence. It writes no replacement from a replay or new message at that key, including `{0, 0}` creates, and makes no payment, credit, acceptance, executor grant or endpoint use that depends on the missing state. A deleted recurring request holds every Billing Period; a new period key cannot bypass its request's hold. An app without a copy waits for an app able to make the protective put-back; quota or value-limit refusal may leave it waiting indefinitely. It takes a later write only under the family rules and never treats a concurrent unmarked recreation as proof that no content was lost. A put-back carrying `lost_to_delete` applies its family's restrictions, including the permanent payer and payee holds; a claimed exact-copy put-back must be proved as What it writes requires. A replay or a user's confirmation supplies no such proof. A listing or read that fails to establish whether a delete is current grants nothing.
  - **Creation and later writes:** for every potentially protected family above, an app may create new usable state only with authenticated evidence, enforced when the create applies, that the key has no current value and no retained delete. A prior read showing absence is insufficient: another app can create and delete the key before the create applies. The Switch's zero-version condition enforces absence at application, including for a key the service has forgotten. A later reader still needs evidence of that creation and its successors. A create over a retained delete instead needs the protective put-back and its evidence above, with authenticated evidence enforced when the put-back applies that the current delete is the exact item it answers. A put-back prepared for one delete cannot apply over another intervening delete and must read and decide again. Every later writer preserves the restrictions and authenticated provenance of that record incarnation, through updates, recreation and protective put-back. A reader with no seen state, including a new app or an old-backup restore, may authorize payment, credit, acceptance, a grant or endpoint use from a current value only after verifying this provenance: an authenticated absence at the incarnation's creation followed by exact-predecessor continuity with every required restriction preserved, or a verified protective put-back followed by that continuity. An unmarked current value, its valid signature, a version match, a replay and a user's confirmation do not prove the absence of an earlier protected delete; a later unmarked rewrite cannot conceal such a delete and grant authority. Missing, incomplete or unverifiable provenance keeps the key held. Retry, reload, backup, restore and provider recovery preserve or reauthenticate the retained restrictions and this provenance; none resets the incarnation or turns missing evidence into authority. Switch revision `f0bbddd1` defined bounded reader evidence in its spec Section 10.3 and wire 6.9. The current pin has no corresponding proof. The mapping for these families remains INCOMPLETE (wire Section 5); this paragraph defines safety requirements, not an executable positive path. Until the mapping, generic implementation, generated cases and Paykit integration are complete and the implementing Switch revision is adopted, an implementation cannot enable first creation, protective put-back or later authority that depends on that provenance. For families without a permanent origin slot, genuine absence after the service has legitimately forgotten all evidence remains subject to the all-witnesses-removed limitation below; a still-listed witness or retained delete cannot be treated as that case.
  - **Without the write scope:** an app whose certificate holds none of the record's `write_scopes` writes nothing: it names the deleting app as above, tells its user and holds the record as deleted until an own app that holds a write scope puts it back.
  - **When a record is lost:** the service may forget a deleted value only once every app the identity's `identity.json` lists has acknowledged it with its service-held cursor (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3, Forgotten deletions). An honest app required to put it back withholds that acknowledgment until the protective write applies, including while quota blocks publication, so its local progress cannot let the key be forgotten. An app that does not acknowledge delays the forgetting; a removed app no longer does. So the value can be lost when every honest app that could put it back is removed before publication, the removed-app case that section names. For a family without a permanent origin slot, absence with no copy or retained evidence can then leave an app unable to tell that the record existed.
  - **The families**, each with its least permissive state, which a write-back from an older copy writes and a record held as deleted reads as. A write-back from an older copy can't tell whether the deleted record held a payment made, a credit made or an end, so each state forbids what any of them would have forbidden. Where money or a credit could then be given twice, a user's confirmation doesn't lift it, since the user can't see what was deleted either: only independent durable evidence does, as each family says.
    - a payer's request or period record holding `manual_only`, while its request's deadline hasn't passed at the service time of the feed page that shows the delete, or of a request without a deadline: the mark kept, manual-only.
    - every payer's request or period record, when the copy is older: its occurrence, every Billing Period of the request for a recurring request's request record, is paid never, automatically or by hand, since the deleted record may have held its proof. No missing, present or recreated claim, whatever attempts it shows, and no user confirmation releases this hold (Payment history lost, below).
    - a payer's request record whose `attempted_periods` lists a period: every listed period kept listed, and, when the copy is older, every Billing Period of the request whose claim the app reads with an open or `unknown` attempt added, with `manual_only` (Section 8.2, Attempted periods).
    - a payer's record of a paid obligation without a deadline, one holding its proof: the proof kept, paid, and when the copy is older, paid never (above), `manual_only` too where the record takes it (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3) (Deleting finished activity).
    - every payee's activity record when the copy is older, regardless of whether it holds a receipt, and an exact copy holding a settlement it credited, one holding its receipt: every receipt held is kept, and when the copy is older, no proof credited to its occurrence, automatically or by hand, since the deleted record may have held that credit and a user's confirmation can't show it didn't (Deleting finished activity).
    - every `payment_lists` record when the copy is older, regardless of whether it holds a `used` entry, and an exact copy holding a `used` entry: every `used` entry held is kept, and when the copy is older, every single-use endpoint of its list's version or an earlier one counted as used, and no endpoint of the list paid automatically, and one by hand only once its user has seen that the list may be stale, until its user confirms that put-back, a confirmation of that loss alone (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5, Confirming a loss).
    - the `block_floor` record: the exact authenticated deleted floor when held, never below any higher floor already seen; otherwise the conservative bound in `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.6, Recovering a deleted floor. That bound includes the clock tolerance an unblock adds. An undated delete, lost clock continuity or unrepresentable bound withholds numeric put-back and every automatic one-time dispatch or repeat; a recent response from a new service is not proof of the missing floor. The same rule applies while the delete remains current. This can hold automatic payments indefinitely; ordinary payment by hand remains subject to every other payment check, and does not clear the hold.
    - outside: the deny list and the allowlist are the switch's own lists, not records (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2): a removal from the deny list is an unblock, which the switch's rules govern, and what a block withheld in Paykit's records the block floor keeps; an exact copy of an activity record outside the protected families may be deleted (Deleting finished activity); and what an app keeps for itself alone, such as its marks of divergent records, is in no record.
  - **Payment history lost:** a payer's record put back from an older copy with `lost_to_delete` cannot be paid through again. The rule does not depend on the request's date, claim retention, the current claim's state, a provider generation or an account incarnation. An absent claim or one showing only failed attempts cannot show what payment evidence the deleted record held; provider-loss, restore, missed-item and divergent-record confirmations do not release the hold either. Erasure, reenrollment or a cooperative move cannot reset it in a record that carries the marker. This deliberately loses availability: even an unpaid request whose payment history was lost cannot be retried through that record. If another payment is intended, it needs a distinct new request or obligation, with its own identity and ordinary payment checks; replaying the old request or clearing or changing its loss marker does not create one. A put-back from the proved exact predecessor remains the existing path above: it preserves the known deleted content, including any existing loss marker, and does not add `lost_to_delete` merely because a delete occurred.
- Collection IDs and record keys are derived as in Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3. Values are sealed under the read scope's key, a label key for a labelled policy (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 3.5 and 13.4).
- **Destinations come only from the payee.** An app pays only to endpoints in a verified message from the payee's current app, or to the payee's public endpoints (Section 7.2). No own app can redirect a payment by writing shared state; at most it can hide records, replay older messages from the same counterparty to an app that never saw newer ones, or act within its own scopes.

**Writing over a refused write.** Except for the different writes at the same activity version described above, when the switch shows as a record's current write one an own app refused, the reference client has the app build its next write on that write's version, from the content the app trusts, as it does for a write it doesn't take only for a missing date or for the certificate the switch shows with it; a write it ignores for another reason, such as a signature that doesn't verify or its own write, isn't built on, and the app builds on the version it holds (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2). Question 36 identifies the remaining risk.

**Record versions and permanent records.** A write naming version 0 holds only where the service holds nothing at the key; a write over a retained delete names that delete, and after a recovery upload a write names the record's current write (`record_current`), not a bare version (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3). A permanent policy with `generation: null` forbids deletion in every generation. A policy naming generation `g` allows creation at zero only in `g` and deletion only by an operation in a generation above `g`. A signed read or covering listing that shows a held live key absent to an authorized reader at its hosting service raises the rollback alarm, except when the newest own identity document's `recovery_from` covers the last generation in which the app saw the key live, or the account has passed the policy's named generation (Section 10.3.1). The publisher's per-recipient counter uses this generation-bound policy (Section 8.4.1). The other Paykit collections retain their policies.

**Record answers.** A record read returns the record with the newest signed head, and a write the switch shows older than the one the app holds is told apart from one an answer current before it overtook; a protocol's check of a newer write also runs when the app has seen nothing of the record yet (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 8.3 and 10.3). Paykit's checks of Sections 6 and 8 apply unchanged; with nothing seen there is nothing to go back on.

## 7. Paying

### 7.1 Claims of payments

- **Obligation IDs.** The payer's apps derive a request's obligation ID from the payee identity and the request hash, each blinded with the payer's naming key, and the Billing Period's start (0 for a one-time request). A payment without a request's obligation ID comes from its attempt ID, blinded the same way (wire 1.5). So every own app finds the same claim, and the service treats the claim key as opaque (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3). The app that made the payment takes the attempt ID from its payment journal; another own app takes it from the payment's `payer_activity` record (wire 5.3).
- **A second wallet** that writes `attempt` while another attempt is open gets `409 conflict` with the current state. It tells the user that another wallet is handling the payment.
- **Deadlines:** an attempt on a one-time request carries `deadline: d + 1 ms`, where `d` is the earlier of its proposal expiry and its payment deadline. An attempt on a Billing Period carries it with `d` the period's start plus the payment deadline's `seconds`. A request with neither carries no `deadline`. `d` is taken at millisecond precision, the precision of every time comparison (Switch wire 1.6). The service rejects an `attempt` once its clock reaches the `deadline`, so `d` is the last instant at which it takes one. The first `attempt` on a claim pins its deadline, including none: the service refuses a later `attempt` that names another (`409 conflict`), and the claim view shows the pinned deadline, so apps can compare it with their own. The service thus bounds, on its own clock, when an attempt may start; the payee still checks v0.1's inclusive deadline against the verified payment time, read as for the rates of period details (Section 8.2). The proposal expiry of a recurring request bounds only its acceptance (Section 8.2).
  - **Not before the request:** the payee also bounds the verified payment time from below, as in v0.1 (`specs/payment-requests.md`, "Payment time and attribution"). Evidence whose verified payment time is earlier than the time the payee recorded its own request, by its own clock, less a tolerance its payment method sets (`specs/payment-conversion.md`, "Payment deadlines"), doesn't prove the request: the payee holds or flags it and doesn't mark the request, or the Billing Period, paid on it alone. The funds still count as received. An own app that didn't record the request itself takes as that time the request's signed `created_at`.
  - An app makes no attempt once the deadline is at or before the highest service time it has verified (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1). A transition proof that stamps an attempt at or after its deadline is misbehaviour: the app pays nothing, writes `failed` and makes no new attempt through that service until the user resolves it, as for a rollback (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1).
  - Each app computes the deadline itself, from the request, and keeps evidence for an obligation, a SETTLED proof or the signed answer that stands for one (Section 7.2, Kept proofs), at least until the later of the pinned deadline and its own, plus the 30-day reconciliation window during which the service keeps the tombstone, and longer for a claim pinned to "none" (Section 7.2 and Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3), so it can still compare, upload and repair it. A pin earlier than the app's own deadline, or any pin where the app computes none, is an alert, and the obligation is doubtful: the tombstone may already be gone while the payment it records still counts. A later pin is a conformance error. Either way the service refuses this app's own `attempt`, which names its own deadline.
- **No automatic payment of doubtful claims:** a claim with an `unknown` or orphaned attempt, or whose next attempt would be flagged `possibly_duplicate` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2, claim transitions), is attempted again only by a user who has been warned that it may already have been paid, never automatically.

### 7.2 Paying order

Every payment follows these steps, whether it pays a one-time request, a Billing Period, a request under an Allowance or nothing requested (Section 8.4). The one exception is a payment without a request to a public endpoint while the payer's own switch doesn't answer, which writes step 1's `attempt` after paying (Section 8.4, Without the switch).

The writes to activity and `payment_lists` records in this order, and the payments that depend on them, remain subject to Section 6, Creation and later writes, and the Release requirements in the introduction. The steps below do not enable first creation, protective put-back or later authority that those requirements withhold.

1. **Write `attempt`**, conditioned on the version of the request record the app read (Section 6), so that a rejection or cancellation recorded since makes it fail, and on the deadline, if any (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2).
   - **Check the transfer first,** before any write. The payment method reports the asset, the amount and the largest fee it will transfer to the destination. The amount must equal the amount being paid, which the payer computes before paying, as in v0.1 (`specs/payment-conversion.md`, "Conversion terms"): to an endpoint of the requested asset that no rate prices, the request's amount, rounded up once to the endpoint's smallest unit (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 1.2); to an endpoint a rate prices, the request's fixed rates or the period details' rates (Section 8.2), the request's amount times that rate, the asset-rail rate if there is one, else the asset-wide one, rounded up once to the endpoint's smallest unit, which its payment method names; and, for a payment without a request, its own amount (Section 8.4). Vectors: `messages.json` `conversion_amounts`. The payee expects the same amount for the endpoint its proof names, by the same rule, with the smallest unit its own payment method names; a payment to an endpoint the terms leave unusable pays no occurrence, and its proof isn't credited and gets no receipt, though, as in v0.1, the payee may still show the funds received. The smallest unit belongs to the endpoint's Payment Endpoint Identifier: the payer's and the payee's payment methods name the same one for it, as both peers use the selected endpoint's precision in v0.1 (`specs/payment-conversion.md`), or a payment made by the rule pays no occurrence. A payer's payment method names the smallest unit of every endpoint it pays, and one whose unit it can't name is unusable for a payment of a request; a payee app whose payment method can't name the endpoint's smallest unit credits no proof to it. Under conversion terms, an endpoint of another asset that no rate prices is unusable for the payment, and so is one whose identifier isn't of the form `<asset>-<rail>-<format>`, whose asset can't be told, as v0.1 takes conversion only under that convention (`specs/payment-conversion.md`); a destination of the requested asset that no rate prices needs no confirmation of rates, also when the payment method orders a priced one first, and is paid automatically only where it otherwise may be; a request without conversion terms promises no rate, so the payer pays it to an endpoint of another asset only by hand, by its own calculation, which its user confirms, and the payee judges what it received by its own, as in v0.1; an app may decline such payments. A rate-priced payment outside an Allowance pays only the exact quote its host authorized. The host may ask its user to confirm each quote or apply an amount or rate policy the user expressly authorized for these payments. The decision binds the payee and signed request, the Billing Period when there is one, the destination app, endpoint and payload, the requested amount, the signed rate source, the rate and the computed amount in the endpoint's asset. If the selection changes, the host decides again. A standing policy can authorize successive in-bound quotes without another prompt. The payment itself still requires the user's instruction or, for an automatic Billing Period, the ordinary recurring-payment policy and current window of Section 8.2. Later period details never reprice an existing attempt. A destination without an amount, such as an address or an amount-less invoice or offer, is paid exactly that amount. A destination that fixes another amount or asset, or whose amount the method can't tell before paying, is unusable for automatic payment; a manual payment shows the amount it would transfer and pays it only on the user's explicit choice. Fees don't count against Allowance limits, which count the amount paid, as in v0.1. The fee is on top of the amount paid and never part of it, as in v0.1 (`specs/payment-conversion.md`), and it is left to the payment method, as in v0.1, where fees and route policy belong to the payment adapter (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`). When no fee cap is configured for the asset the payment method transfers as the attempt is planned, Paykit makes no check of the fee and asks no confirmation for it; whatever limit the payment method applies is its own, and Paykit neither knows nor checks it. A payer app may configure a fee cap per asset, a guard on the part of the fee the payee can influence through the destination it chose, which is the largest fee the method reports. With a cap, that fee must stay within it, judged when the transfer is checked and again immediately before dispatch, a repeat's included, against the cap the attempt was journaled with; above it, the destination is unusable for automatic payment, and a manual payment needs the user's choice, which binds the fee shown: before dispatch, a fee above the cap passes only when it is the one the user chose. With no cap, nothing bounds the fee between planning and dispatch: the fee is what the method reports at the call, no destination is made unusable for its fee, a confirmation given for another reason doesn't bind the fee, and the fee of an automatic payment, an Allowance executor's included, and any part of the fee the payee can influence through the destination it chose are bounded only by the payment method's own limit, if it has one. A payer app whose payment method enforces no fee limit of its own, or that wants a tighter bound for unattended payments, should configure a cap.
   - **Reserve a single-use endpoint** in the same batch: when the destination is a single-use endpoint of a Private Payment List, the batch also writes the `payment_lists` record marking it used, at its next version (Section 8.4), whatever obligation is paid. The list's generation is checked against the payee's fresh `identity.json` (Section 8.4, No usable list) before this batch is submitted, not in parallel with it, so a list the check refuses has nothing reserved and no version consumed. If that write fails its version check, nothing is written: the app re-reads the record, and pays again to the next usable endpoint or asks for a newer list. An executor's batch adds its ledger and activity writes (Section 8.3), a payment without a request its activity record (Section 8.4), an attempt on a Billing Period the request record doesn't list as attempted the write of the request record that adds it (Section 8.2), an attempt on a Billing Period paid without its user's confirmation the write of its period record at the version read (Section 8.2), and an attempt its user started by hand the write of `manual_only` on the occurrence's record (Section 8.3, Manual only).

   In parallel with the batch:
   - **check the destination:** the message or record carrying the destination verifies as in Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2, carries the destination, and hasn't expired: a public record at a finite `expires_at` or at its `updated_at` plus the payee identity's record age limit, including when `expires_at` is null, a Private Payment List at its `expires_at` or 30 days after the payer's inbox accepted it, and period details at their `expires_at` (Sections 5 and 8.2 and Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.2); a record also names the app whose path it was read from. A destination has expired once its expiry is at or before the later of the payer's device clock and the highest service time it has verified for its own account at its current service (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1, Server clock), compared at millisecond precision (Switch wire 1.6), so a device clock running behind a service time the payer has verified never keeps an expired destination usable. This adds to the device-clock check of Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1, Clock bound: Paykit refuses a destination that either clock shows expired, also by hand. When only the service time shows a destination expired, the payer tells the user that its device clock is behind the service's time (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1, Clock bound) and treats the destination as any expired one. This applies to manual and automatic payments alike, wherever a destination's expiry is judged, the last time immediately before dispatch. A payer that has verified no service time judges by its device clock alone, and a payment without the switch (Section 8.4, Without the switch) judges by its device clock and the highest service time it verified earlier, reading none afresh. A request or period details carrying it name the obligation being paid by their signed fields: the request is the one paid, and period details name it first in `refs` and the Billing Period paid; a Private Payment List isn't tied to one obligation. The app that sent the request, period details or Private Payment List, or published the public endpoint, is current in the payee's `identity.json`, cached for at most 10 minutes (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 4.2 and 4.3), and, for a public endpoint, the higher of the Homeserver copy and the payee's switch's copy when the payer had to fetch it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3). For a public endpoint, if the payee's switch lookup fails or is still pending after its grace period, a manual payment requires the user's explicit confirmation before any write. An automatic payment waits for confirmation from the switch and stops if the lookup fails. The payer uses an answer that arrives before money moves. For a Private Payment List, the payer must have a qualifying observation of the payee's document to check the list's app and generation (Section 8.4, No usable list). If it lacks that observation, it refuses the payment even when the user confirms;
   - revalidate the app's own `identity.json` if needed (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3).

   A destination carried in a signed message needs a Homeserver or verified public-service read of the very version of the payee's document used for that destination. Its age must be at least zero and less than 10 minutes when the payer uses it. A version known only from an app directory has no such observation.

   For a public endpoint, when the payee's switch lookup succeeds, each public app-record read used for discovery or payment must begin and finish while both the observation of the chosen identity version and the switch lookup are current. Their shared interval starts at the later of the two observation times and ends at the earlier time plus 10 minutes, with the end excluded. This also applies to app-record reads started in parallel with the lookup. When the payer uses the endpoint, including immediately before dispatch, both observations must still be current. If either has expired, the payer repeats the identity lookup and checks the app records under the resulting observations.

   If the lookup failed or is still pending after its grace period, a user who explicitly confirms may use the exact identity version observed at the Homeserver. In this case each app-record read must begin and finish within that Homeserver observation's ten minutes, and the observation must still be current when used. An automatic payment waits for the switch and stops if the lookup fails. On either path, a read outside the applicable interval or a change to the signed identity document requires new app-record reads under the eligible document. An answer received before dispatch takes effect even after the user has confirmed. The payer applies the successful-lookup rule if the answer succeeds.

   All three must succeed before any money moves. If any of them fails, the app writes `failed` instead of paying. An executor checks its Allowance again at this point (Section 8.3), and a payment priced by period details' rates checks that they are still usable (Section 8.2). An automatic payment isn't attempted at all while the device clock and the service's differ by more than the clock tolerance, or the highest service time the app has verified lies more than that ahead of the device clock, (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1, Clock bound).

   **Dispatch window.** The owner sets the attempt's `orphan_after` to at least the longest its payment method can take to complete or fail, plus the time it allows itself between the `attempt` and the dispatch (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2). It dispatches only while that allowance hasn't passed since the service accepted the `attempt` and its own certificate hasn't expired, judged with no wait between the judgment and the dispatch; past it, it submits nothing and writes `failed`. It repeats an idempotent dispatch only while the method's longest time still fits before the orphan age. The owner's own revocation inside the allowance isn't seen without a read; the user's confirmation that Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2 rows 5 and 6 require before another app resolves the attempt, and the `possibly_duplicate` flag (Section 7.3), cover it.
2. **Pay.** For a one-time request whose record holds no response yet (Section 8.1, Rejecting and cancelling), send the acceptance at the same time, without waiting for it. The acceptance becomes sendable only once the payment method's call is made, a resume finds the payment in the method's history, or the user marks as paid (`done`) a payment that may have been dispatched: never while a check before the dispatch can still stop it, and never for a payment the method reported failed, so a payee never sees an acceptance of a payment that never ran. The one exception is a payer record paused as divergent: the acceptance then waits with step 3's write (step 4).
3. **Write `done`** (basis `owner`) and update the activity record, in one batch. If the payment failed, write `failed` on the claim instead, leaving the activity record as it is.
4. **Send the proof** to the payee. While the payer record is paused as divergent, step 3's write, the claim's `done` with it, the proof and a one-time request's acceptance not yet sent wait until its user confirms, and go once the user has confirmed and the messages kept over the record are written, also while its view still shows it divergent (Section 6, Divergent records); a `failed` outcome, which writes nothing over the record, goes at once, alone. The attempt stays open meanwhile, so another own app frees it only once it is orphaned and its user confirms (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2 row 6), and then pays the obligation only by hand, the new attempt flagged `possibly_duplicate` (Sections 7.1 and 7.3), as for any open attempt.

- **Kept proofs:** before attempting, an app compares the claim with any SETTLED proof it keeps for that claim (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3). That proof is the transition proof of the entry that settled the claim, from the answer to its own write or a feed item that carried it (Switch wire 7.5). An app that got no such proof, such as one that first sees the claim once its entries are stubs, keeps instead the signed answer of its read showing the claim SETTLED (Switch wire 7.1 and 7.4.8), and keeps and compares that the same way; it uploads none, since the Switch's uploads take proofs, heads and operations and no answer of a read (Switch wire 7.4.14). A service that shows FREE, or nothing, for a claim the app holds a signed SETTLED proof for is treated as a rollback (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1): the app makes no attempt through it and alerts the user. The one exception is a tombstone the service may already have deleted (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3): from its pinned deadline plus the 30-day reconciliation window, or, for a claim pinned to "none", from 400 days after the proof's entry, the time the switch keeps that tombstone after its last entry, which is no earlier. The app keeps the proof until the later of the bounds that apply: the later of the pinned deadline and its own, where there is one, plus the window; and, for a pin to "none", the 400 days. A pinned deadline where the app computes none (its request sets no deadline) is an early pin, and the app keeps that proof for good, since it may still pay the obligation. While the service may have deleted the tombstone but the app still keeps the proof, the pin differs from the app's own deadline (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2), early or "none" beside a deadline, so the app alerts and still treats the obligation as paid. A bound has passed, and the app may drop the proof, only when it lies before the earlier of the highest verified service time and the device clock plus 24 hours, so one far-future signed time can't make the app drop its evidence. Until then the app keeps it and compares it with the claim, and uploads a proof in a move or after a provider loss (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 12.5 and 12.6), when a faulty old service may have left the tombstone out.
- **The record shows it paid:** an app never attempts an obligation whose activity record it holds already carries the proof of a payment, whatever the claim shows. A claim without a deadline is gone 400 days after its last entry (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3), and then the record, not the claim, stops a second payment, for example of a re-sent copy of the request. So the payer never deletes the record of a paid obligation without a deadline; the user may only archive it (Section 6).
- The **attempt ID** is random. It is recorded in the app's payment journal before the write, and used as the payment method's idempotency key where the method supports one.
- **Only a verified answer lets money move.** If the answer to the `attempt` is lost or its service signature doesn't verify, the app doesn't pay; it re-reads the claim and continues from what a verified answer shows. The same holds for an answer whose transition proofs don't bind the batch: one proof per operation, each naming its operation by its stub's item hash, and its object (Switch wire 6.5). Before paying, the attempt's proof must also show the app's own attempt: the claim ATTEMPTING, with this app as owner and the attempt ID it wrote current and open, at the step after the one it expected. Otherwise the app pays nothing and writes `failed`. A signed answer that fails these checks is misbehaviour, treated as a rollback (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1).
- **Starting to pay** is recorded in the journal before the payment begins. After a crash, an app whose journal shows no payment started writes `failed`; otherwise it resolves the attempt from the payment method's history (Section 7.3). This crash rule holds only for the app's current journal. A journal restored from a backup may predate a payment made after the backup, so a restored app treats its open attempts as Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4 says.
- **What a restore loses.** A restore from a backup brings back the app's own state as it was at the backup (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4): what the app kept only in its own state since is gone, and so are the entries it passed since. The Switch recovers every lasting effect from claims and shared records, but a lasting effect kept only in the app's own state is in neither, such as a stop kept from a verified `payment_request_cancellation` or `allowance_end` whose record write had failed or was still pending (Sections 8.1 to 8.3). Cursor moves aren't in the chain (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.3), but the service reports with every feed page the cursor it holds for the app, and the Switch says when that report shows a restore clean: no entry after the backup's position was passed, and none the app could still need is lost (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4, The held cursor). After a clean restore the app resumes: it holds no request and no Allowance for the restore, asks its user nothing, and sends no `resend_request` for it. After any other restore from a backup, one whose held cursor is ahead of the backup's position or unknown, the app treats every open request and every Allowance as possibly stopped: it makes no automatic attempt or acceptance on such a request or under such an Allowance, and a manual attempt only after its user has seen that the payee may have cancelled it; resuming an acceptance whose original delivery is proved still pending, as below, is no new acceptance. Once authorized under Section 3.1, Outbound authorization, it sends each counterparty of an open request or Allowance, but a blocked one (Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties), a `resend_request` with `accepted_from` at the `accepted_at` of the last entry its backup's cursor passed, less the clock tolerance, and keeps a cancellation or end that comes back as Sections 8.1 to 8.3 say. A blocked counterparty is asked once it is unblocked, which brings back only what that sender still keeps (Section 9). A restore that isn't clean also marks every counterparty its deny list holds at its next read of the lists as blocked, from the backup's cursor time less the clock tolerance, or the restore's bound on the times it restores where that is earlier, to that read (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1), and, in the commit that applies those marks, raises the block floor to that read's time (Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties). What such a counterparty's inbox accepted since is then asked for once it is unblocked. A sender blocked and unblocked again between the backup and that read is in no mark the backup carries and not in the list the restore reads, and the inbox entries the device the backup came from read are deleted (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2): what its inbox accepted while it was blocked is asked for only by the restore's other re-sends, which ask the counterparty of an open request or Allowance, and is otherwise lost when its sender stops keeping it, 30 days after its acceptance. Such a sender's one-time request that comes back opens paid only by hand, by the floor, so none of it is paid automatically. The Paykit SDK can't rebuild who was blocked in that stretch: the feed shows the deny list's changes to an app holding `lists.read` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.3), but the client hands them to no protocol. Before any restored flush or reconstruction, the app needs current evidence that it still owes the exact original delivery, including any disposition since the backup. For example, before an in-place restore the host can establish from the uninterrupted original store that it still owes that exact delivery, provided it has exclusive custody through the restore and no other copy could have disposed of it. A signed message in a shared record proves intent. Neither that record, an outbox entry copied from a backup, a clean feed cursor nor the absence of a terminal notice establishes that no later delivery or give-up occurred. With only those sources, even within the replay window, the app holds the message, makes no automatic delivery and does not call `App::queue_stored`. It keeps the outcome as possibly delivered and tells the host to reconcile it under Deliveries of unknown outcome. If current evidence establishes that the original delivery remains pending, the app may resume it with its original signed bytes and replay deadline, only after listing the relevant records, obtaining their scope keys and applying all current send rules. An answer or later stop suppresses the send. A receipt stored without sending is never sent this way. Period details additionally require a readable period record without a receipt and a readable request record without rejection or cancellation. If a required record cannot be read, the app waits and checks again on receive until the original replay deadline. A copy reconstructed from stored plaintext may go only until 30 days after its `created_at`, less the clock tolerance (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 5.3 and 11). The hold on a request or an Allowance ends when its record shows it cancelled or ended, which stops it for good and which no user confirmation overrides, or when the user confirms it. A counterparty that never answers leaves the hold in place until the user confirms; nothing releases it automatically. A stop the backup holds still applies, and no confirmation overrides it either: the hold is for stops that may have come after the backup. The rule binds a restore the app knows of, because the app made it; a store rolled back without the app knowing, such as a device image restored underneath it, shows, to an app whose devices don't share its cursor, as a held cursor ahead of its own position, which the app handles as a `missed` item (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.3, Ahead; What a `missed` item loses, below); for an app whose devices share its cursor nothing in the Pubky Switch detects it (Section 17, question 28). The backup also carries the app's marks of divergent records, which the restore installs unconfirmed (Section 6, Divergent records). A restored publishing app whose backup holds its list or a list it sent holds the list and the counterparties of its backup: a list change or a withdrawal made since is gone. So it sends no list but a withdrawal until its host sets its list again, which the host does once it has re-applied what the user changed, its withdrawals first (Section 8.4, Withdrawing a list), and it tells its host so once. Every Private Payment List the restored outbox still carries, empty as well as nonempty, is taken out of it before the host can send it. The app durably retains the obligation to withdraw from that counterparty before removing the carried delivery, also suppressing a carried copy that a kept batch could later queue. Once its current certificate holds plain `paykit_request` and its identity and restore first-read guards permit delivery, it sends a newly signed empty version reserved under Section 8.4.1, then sends a nonempty list only to recipients the host has chosen for sharing after reapplying its changes. An empty withdrawal or a counter discovered at restore never enrolls a recipient. A carried empty withdrawal cannot discharge this obligation: a later nonempty version may have reached the counterparty after the backup, even when the restore verdict is clean. If the app cannot establish or publish a valid newer version yet, the withdrawal remains owed and the carried list stays suppressed. This replaces only carried list deliveries; owed receipts, proofs and other messages remain subject to their own send rules, and every delivery is gated before the restored app can send it (Section 8.1, Messages already queued). Until then what is owed waits, and a payer whose list expires meanwhile has none from it until then.
- **What a `missed` item loses.** A `missed` item shows that the service deleted entries wrapped to the app before it read them (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2), and the app can't check it, so a payee's `payment_request_cancellation` or a counterparty's `allowance_end` may have been among them. In the commit that passes the item, the app holds every open request and every Allowance as after a restore that isn't clean (What a restore loses, above): it makes no automatic attempt or acceptance on such a request or under such an Allowance, makes no repeat at a resume of a dispatch made before the item, since a repeat is an automatic payment too (Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties), and makes a manual attempt only after its user has seen that the payee may have cancelled it. A confirmation its user gave before the item releases nothing; a request first seen after the item is held only if its signed `created_at` is earlier than the item's last acceptance time plus the clock tolerance, since a later request can't have had its cancellation among the missed entries. The item neither sets nor lifts a pause of a divergent record, nor undoes a confirmation of one: it loses inbox entries, not the records' views, which the listing after it reads again (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2; Section 8.1, Records not listed yet). The requests and Allowances the app holds stay held until the record shows a stop or the user confirms, as after a restore, by a confirmation given after the item: nothing releases them automatically (Section 17, question 28). The app asks each counterparty of an open request or Allowance, and of its unfinished activity records, to re-send as Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2 says, but a blocked one, which it asks once it is unblocked, bringing back only what that sender still keeps (Section 9; Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties), Paykit narrowing that duty, never for a time before its cursor's time less the clock tolerance (Section 9). In the commit that passes the item, the app also keeps the item's window, from its earliest to its last acceptance time, and marks every counterparty its deny list holds at its next read of the lists as blocked over that window, for the same ask, raising the block floor to the earlier of the item's last acceptance time and the service time of the read that passes it, in the commit that applies those marks, when it makes any (Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties).
- **The restored certificate.** A Paykit App checks every certificate it runs under by Paykit's own scope and permission rules (Sections 3.1 and 3.2), which are stricter than the Switch's. At a restore it checks its backup's certificate before the restore writes anything, and doesn't restore from a backup whose certificate fails them. A certificate it takes afterwards, such as the renewed one its `identity.json` lists (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4), it checks before it acts under it, and it takes no Paykit action under a certificate that fails them: it sends nothing, pays nothing, and holds the entries it receives, unprocessed, until a certificate that passes replaces it. It still keeps its marks of divergent records meanwhile, setting one for each record it sees divergent: they are its own state, which acts on nothing, and they hold once a certificate that passes replaces it (Section 6, Divergent records). At a join, it checks the approval's certificate before it keeps anything.
- **Failed means definitively not paid.** Once a payment may have been submitted to the payment method, the owner MUST NOT write `failed` without authoritative evidence that it did not and cannot settle. A timeout, a disconnection, a crash or an ambiguous result MUST keep the attempt open: it blocks any replacement payment, keeps its Allowance reservation, and is resolved only through Section 7.3. As in the v0.1 SDK, the owner reports one of three outcomes: definitive failure, verified success, or nothing yet.
- **No second execution.** An app never carries out an attempt a second time, after a crash or otherwise, unless the payment method's idempotency on the attempt ID makes a repeat harmless or the journal shows the payment never started. When the method's history shows any record of the attempt, the app resolves the attempt from that record and never carries it out again, idempotent or not.
- **What a payment method declares:** each integration states whether it is idempotent on the attempt ID, and for how long it remembers the key; whether its history can show the outcome of a dispatch, and for how long it keeps it; and whether that history is authoritative across a restore of the paying device, because it lives with the account and not only on the device. An empty history means the payment never started only within the history's retention, counted from when the attempt was journaled, and, after a restore, only for a history that is authoritative across one. A dispatch is repeated only on an idempotent method, within its retention, and never from a restored journal. Otherwise the dispatch stays open as potentially paid. Only a receipt or the user can resolve it. An owner that finds a payment it reported failed completed after all sends its proof, so the payee's receipt can correct the claim. The service keeps the claim for 30 days after its deadline (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3); a receipt after that can't be recorded on it, and an app that gets it and holds the failure shows the user that a payment marked failed was paid. An app holds the failure when it has it from a stable source: the attempt's owner from its own journal, or any own app, the owner included, from a view of the claim, kept or read, that shows the attempt `failed`. An owner restored from a backup that predates the failure has no journal entry for it, and uses such a view as any other own app does. This decides only the warning: how an Allowance ledger counts the attempt (Section 8.3) doesn't change. The record's `status` is informational (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3) and never decides it. An own app that never saw the failure, such as one that joined after the service deleted the claim, can't tell and shows nothing. Either way the app records the receipt in its activity record (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.11). It takes the claim as gone once the switch holds none, or once the claim's pinned deadline plus 30 days is at or before the highest service time it has verified.
- **Doubtful window:** only a crash between paying and writing `done`, an outcome the method can't report, or a restore whose payment history can't tell (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4), leaves doubt. The wallet tells the user the payment may already have gone through.

#### Installed wallet bindings

A platform wallet supplies the payment method that moves the money. The SDK owns endpoint selection validation, the payment journal, the checks before dispatch and the signed Paykit messages. Selecting or quoting a candidate cannot start a payment. The host returns candidate IDs from the batch it received and attributes each selection failure to its candidate.

When step 2 authorizes dispatch, the SDK calls the host method with the selected endpoint and payload, the transfer amount, the original attempt ID and the payment reference when present. The host passes that attempt ID as the method's idempotency key wherever the method supports one. It declares its idempotency and history retention, whether history survives device restore and its longest completion time. A lookup by the original attempt ID only reads history. Reopen, restore and an interrupted callback follow this section's rules for a second execution. The host must be able to finish its callbacks without acquiring the wallet handle that invoked them.

A method that confirms payment returns its method-specific proof object and, when known, its verified payment time. The SDK binds that evidence to the original attempt and occurrence, signs the Payment Proof and delivers it under step 4. The same rule applies when the SDK learns of success from method history. A timeout, an interrupted call or an outcome the method cannot determine leaves the attempt open. A refusal before submission means the method knows that call submitted nothing and can no longer do so. Where Allowance reconciliation requires an inventory, the host reports every possible dispatch within the required coverage (Section 8.3). It returns an empty inventory only when it can establish that none exists.

The payee's own method checks the proof against the expected endpoint and amount, returns a verified payment time on confirmation and supplies the method's settlement identifier where the credit rule requires one. The SDK applies the credit and receipt rules of Sections 8.1 and 8.4. The payer's method evidence alone cannot authorize a payee receipt. Generated bindings keep endpoint payloads, payment references, proof data and nested method errors out of ordinary diagnostic output.

### 7.3 Reconciliation

- **Orphaned attempts** (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2) stay open until reconciled.
- **After a provider loss** (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6), a payer treats a request as potentially paid at its old service if its inbox accepted it before the end of the residual window (`uncertain_until_ms`) or its signed `created_at` is earlier than that end plus the clock tolerance. This includes a Billing Period of an earlier subscription even when the period starts after the window, since the payer could pay that period early by hand (Section 8.2). A request first accepted at or after the end of the window, with a signed creation time at or after that end plus the tolerance, follows the ordinary payment rules. A payee that misdates a re-sent request falls outside this rule (Section 13). An app keeps the loss's declaration time and the end of its residual window (`declared_at_ms` and `uncertain_until_ms`, Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6) for this, and a backup carries them: an app restored from a backup taken before it learned of the loss learns them again when it follows the loss (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6). An obligation it can't place before or after them, such as one whose request it can't date, is in doubt. The permanent hold of a payer record put back from an older copy with `lost_to_delete` still applies (Section 6, Payment history lost).
- **Resolutions** (the rows are those of the claim table of Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2):

| Resolution | Who | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `done`, basis `evidence` (row 3) | Any own app with `claims.write` | The attempt the evidence names is done; claim SETTLED |
| `done`, basis `reconcile` (row 4) | Any own app with `claims.write`, user-confirmed, orphaned attempt | Attempt done; claim SETTLED |
| `unknown` (row 6) | Any own app with `claims.write`, user-confirmed, orphaned attempt | Attempt unknown; claim FREE; the next attempt is flagged `possibly_duplicate` |
| `failed` on an `unknown` attempt, or on an orphaned open attempt whose owner isn't current (row 5) | Its owner while current; otherwise any own app with `claims.write`, user-confirmed | Attempt failed; its Allowance amount stays counted, and is released only as Section 8.3 (`failed` isn't final) says; from a non-owner, the next attempt is flagged `possibly_duplicate` |
| The owner's later report (rows 2 and 5) | Owner of an `unknown` attempt, while current | Done or failed, as reported |

- **The evidence a resolution cites** (`evidence_ref`, Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2): for basis `evidence`, the hash of the payee's `payment_receipt` message JSON; for basis `owner` and `reconcile`, the hash of the payer's `payment_proof` message JSON. Before writing `done` by evidence, the app verifies the receipt: its issuer, its request and Billing Period, and that it confirms the proof of the attempt. A receipt carries no amount: the amount is the one that proof reports paid, the request's or the period's, or the proof's own for a payment without a request.
- **Only the payee's yes counts.** A receipt can settle an attempt, because the payee confirms it was paid. Nothing from the payee shows that an attempt was not paid: a payee gains from a second payment, and may not have seen the first one yet. A reminder, a re-sent request or a `resend_request` is no evidence either way.
- Features that account for payments, such as Allowance budgets (Section 8.3), derive their accounting from the outcome of each attempt.
- **An app that isn't current can write nothing,** not even outcomes. Its attempts are reconciled through receipts or by other apps.
- **Graceful removal:** the signer warns before revoking an app, or letting its certificate lapse (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.2), while it still owns open attempts, which `GET /v1/{id}/apps` lists. Apps with `paykit_allowance` warn when an Allowance's executor stops being current (Section 8.3).

An app with `paykit_recovery` can show which original apps are listed on the identity-signed opening, which have an explicit signed removal for that loss, and which have a verifiable report at the service (wire 5.10). A report commits to the original’s inventory cutoff, the subjects it found and its decision for each. A wallet given the disclosed evidence can recompute the three digests using wire 5.10 and independently check the basis of a decision. Roster completion, removal and republication do not establish that a particular obligation was unpaid or a settlement uncredited.

For an uncertain obligation, the wallet shows that the signed request or specific Billing Period may already have been paid. It holds automatic payment, manual payment and any repeat that could debit the payer while the wallet cannot establish whether the obligation was already paid or whether an earlier submission or authorization could still settle. The hold applies at reservation and again at dispatch. A SETTLED claim or a verified receipt for the same attempt can establish payment under this section.

Before treating an uncertain obligation as unpaid, the wallet must account for every original app through a verified report or an explicit signed removal for this loss, and for every funding account and submission that could have paid the obligation. A lookup by attempt ID accounts only for that attempt in the account serving the lookup. A refusal before submission accounts only for that call. A report from one installation, an empty claim at the new service, a payee reminder or a user's confirmation leaves the hold in place while any earlier debit remains unaccounted for. Consent to one manual attempt records no statement about payment before the loss. A failure or interruption leaves that earlier history in doubt. The app reconciles the attempt under Section 7.2. A newer loss requires assessment against its own boundary, including after restart or restore.

**Backups and local storage.** Layered backup and restore preserve each protocol's local state, the carried processed pairs and their kind ownership, bounded waiting custody and owed notices, and outbox holds and origins within the Switch's stated backup coverage. They do not create absent sent-message or unanswered-batch history (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 11 and 12.4). The reference store requires atomic, durable, ordered commits, exclusive namespace ownership and guarded commits across processes; an unknown commit requires resolution from durable state before further action. Paykit uses its qualified protocol namespace and commits its state with the client outcome, without direct writes that bypass ownership or a success result after storage failure.

**Service keys, responses and account lifetimes.** One service-wide key signs responses, with a rollback mark for each account creation. Responses bind the exact received request, including signed-header presence and values, and clients judge authenticated generation as well as key; unsigned public-read headers supply no authority (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 6.1, 6.5 and 12.6; Switch wire 2.1, 7.1, 7.3 and 7.6). Signer requests bind the selected account creation under `spec/account-binding.md`; a signed recovery notice or an unreachable service never authorizes recovery automatically.

To obtain verified service time without taking an inbox entry, read a signed feed page with `limit=0`. Verify the response from the current service under Switch wire Sections 7.1 and 7.4.4. The empty page changes no cursor.

## 8. Payment flows and features

### 8.1 One-time request

Creation and use of the `payee_activity` and `payer_activity` records below remain subject to Section 6, Creation and later writes, and the Release requirements in the introduction. The request exchange does not release a payment or credit held by those requirements.

1. The payee sends `payment_request`, usually with its destination, admitted by the payer's allowlist, for example after an invite, and writes its `payee_activity` request record in parallel.
2. The payer's apps with `paykit_pay` receive it, with a push. The first to process it writes the `payer_activity` request record.
3. One app pays, following Section 7.2.
4. The payee verifies the proof by its own method, updates its `payee_activity` record to paid, and stores a `payment_receipt` there, which it may send. Before crediting a payment to an occurrence, the payee app checks the payee records it can verify across all counterparties, including requests, Billing Periods and payments without a request (Section 8.4). It credits the proof only if none of those other occurrences holds a proof of the same settlement, as its payment method names it (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 1.5), with a receipt. A payee app that credits a proof, to a request, a Billing Period or as a payment without a request, stores its receipt in that record, also when it sends none, so the record shows the credit. For a marked credit (Section 6, Settlement marks), the batch that stores the receipt writes the settlement's bucket in `settlements` at the version the app read, `{0, 0}` for a bucket not yet written, with the mark added, naming the occurrence credited (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3; `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.7), so of two own payee apps writing a mark for the same settlement at once, to one occurrence or two, of one counterparty or two, labelled or not, only one batch applies. The credit is complete, and shown so, only once the batch that stores its receipt applies, with the mark or, by hand, without one; when the service refuses that batch for the storage quota or the value limit (`429 quota_exceeded` or `413 too_large`), also once the bucket's write is left out, nothing is credited and no receipt is sent: the proof stays pending, in its record without a receipt or as a stored entry (Stored entries, below), the app tells its host of the refusal, and once room frees it decides the credit again (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.7, Complete). When the method identifies the settlement, the app first reads its bucket at its switch, never deciding from its copy or a backup alone, and credits nothing, whatever its user confirms, while the bucket holds a mark of the settlement or the app has seen one; an absent bucket supplies no mark of it and leaves the other checks of this step in force (Section 6, Settlement marks); while the bucket doesn't open or read, is invalid or divergent, or the read fails, the settlement may already be credited: the app credits it never automatically, and by hand only once its user has been shown that it may be, and tells its host once per settlement (`CreditNeedsUser`). A proof of a request or a Billing Period whose payment method gives no settlement ID (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 1.5) has no mark, and is credited only by hand, once its user has been shown that it may already be credited, the host told (`CreditNeedsUser`); one of a payment without a request isn't credited (Section 8.4). When a bucket comparison fails (`409 conflict`), the app reads the current bucket and occurrence again and repeats the full credit decision, including verification by its payment method. A condition failure may return only the key and version. If the bucket now holds a mark of the settlement, the settlement is credited already: if the mark names this occurrence, another own app credited it, and the app takes that app's write; otherwise the app stores and sends no receipt for this occurrence, whose record keeps the proof without one, and shows its user that the settlement was credited to another occurrence. It retries a marked credit only if the current bucket opens, binds and can take the mark after merging the marks already seen. A newly full or divergent bucket follows the same rule as on the first read. After a provider loss, a settlement whose payment the app can't date after the declaration plus the residual window (Section 7.3) may have been credited at the lost service and its mark lost with it: the app credits it only once its user confirms, having been shown that it may already be credited, also when the new service holds no mark of it, the host told once per settlement (`CreditNeedsUser`); a mark the app holds from before the loss counts as there, and the app uploads its bucket (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6). One transaction is one settlement, so a payer pays two occurrences in two transactions, as `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 1.5 says for payments without a request. So an occurrence whose record holds the proof and no receipt, beside another record holding a proof of that settlement with a receipt, isn't credited, and every own payee app that holds both records reads it so. An app that holds a record of that counterparty it can't open, or one whose view shows it divergent (Section 6, Divergent records), or whose copy of the records may be incomplete (Records not listed yet, below), credits no such proof until it can tell, or until its user confirms crediting it, having been shown that it may already be credited, and a user's confirmation of a divergent record of the counterparty counts as one only if it showed the user that the record may hold a credit of a settlement its current write lacks; for this it checks every record of its `payee_activity` collection it holds, and one it can't read as a record of a counterparty, deleted, unopened or unbound, counts as one of that counterparty's while it is paused, divergent or not confirmed for a credit; an app holding only labelled selling scopes reads only its labels' records, so it checks a settlement against those alone, and the settlement's mark covers the others. A payer whose record holds that proof pays the occurrence again only after a new request. A mark derives from the settlement alone and prevents credit to another counterparty's occurrence as well. A hand-confirmed credit without a mark has the limits in Section 13.

- **Destination:** a request with `payment_endpoints` is paid only to those. One with `required_app_id` instead is paid only to that payee app's Private Payment List or public endpoints, and that app must be current; a request carrying both fields is invalid. One with neither, for example from an app that receives no payments itself, is paid to any of the payee's current Private Payment Lists or public endpoints, under the rules of Section 8.4, including single-use reservation and the explicit choice between private and public payment; the proof names the endpoint it used.
- **Rejecting and cancelling:** a payer app records its rejection or cancellation in the request record, in a batch conditioned on the step of the request's claim it read (`claim_step`), which it writes only while that claim shows no open, `unknown` or paid attempt, and sends it only after that batch succeeds. An attempt is conditioned on the request record (Section 7.2), so one own app can't reject a request while another pays it. A payer app sends at most one response, an acceptance or a rejection, to a request, as in v0.1 (`specs/payment-requests.md`, "State derivation"), and only while the request record holds neither a response nor a cancellation: after an acceptance it may cancel, after a rejection it neither accepts nor cancels, and after a cancellation it sends no response. It judges this from the request record, so a response another own app recorded counts, and sends none while its copy may lack that record or can't open it (Records not listed yet, below). A recurring request is answered and cancelled only as Section 8.2 says, conditioned on its Billing Periods' claims, never on a one-time obligation.
- **Blocked counterparties:** blocking pauses what is open with a counterparty and revokes nothing. While the deny list holds a counterparty (Pubky Switch spec, Section 7), a payer app, which always holds `paykit_contacts` (Section 3.1), makes no automatic payment or acceptance for it, and accepts a subscription from it, pays its open requests and Billing Periods, and makes a payment without a request to it, only after its user confirms, having shown the user that the counterparty is blocked. Where v0.1 ran nothing for a blocked counterparty ("No intake, send, or private automation then runs for that counterparty", `specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Block A Peer"), a v0.2 payer app may still accept and pay for it on its user's confirmation; an app sends a blocked counterparty no list, renewal, answer, re-send request, list request or extension message, and asks it again for what it still owes to ask once it is unblocked (Sections 8.4 and 9). An answer is also a rejection of a request and the payer's cancellation of one, and each is a stop: while its copy of the deny list holds the payee, or that copy isn't known after it tried to read the lists, the app still records it in the request record, on the same conditions as otherwise, so that no own app pays or accepts the request, and holds only its message, which it sends from that record once that copy is known and doesn't hold the payee, at its first receive at which its records are listed, none waits for a scope key and the request record reads; a message still held 30 days after its `created_at`, less the clock tolerance, is never sent, and the app tells the host that it gave it up (`MessageGivenUp`) at its first receive past that time at which that copy is known and doesn't hold the payee, its records are listed, none waits for a scope key and the request record reads, the record still holding it. While the request record is paused as divergent and its user hasn't confirmed it, a delete included, the app decides nothing on it about the held message; once the record reads unpaused and holds neither that message nor any stop, the app gives the message up, telling the host (`MessageGivenUp`), so the user may answer again. An Allowance's proposal, acceptance and rejection require contacts authorization and are refused when it is missing or sends are blocked, recording and sending nothing; its end follows its separate stop rules (Sections 3.1 and 8.3, Blocked counterparties). Nor does an app holding `paykit_contacts` send a new request, or period details for a subscription, to a counterparty its copy shows blocked, or while its copy isn't known. What stops or settles a payment still goes when the app first sends it, though not as a restore's re-send (Section 7.2, What a restore loses), but for a publishing app's withdrawal of its list, which Section 8.4 sends again: a payee's cancellation of a request it sent, a publishing app's withdrawal of its list, a user's `allowance_end` (Section 8.3, Blocked counterparties), a receipt, and the acceptance and proof of a payment dispatched before the block or on the user's confirmation, which report money sent or being sent. A resume's repeat of a dispatched payment (Section 7.2, What a payment method declares) is an automatic payment too: the user's confirmation was for the first dispatch, so the app makes no repeat while the deny list holds the payee, and the attempt stays open. Unblocking ends the pause. A message from a blocked counterparty that would create state is dropped as one never processed and counted in a recovery mark, from which the app asks again once the counterparty is unblocked (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1; Section 9). Recovery is best effort within the mark's lifetime and the sender's retention; the mark's retirement and host-notice rules do not guarantee that the message returns. A one-time request that comes back this way is paid only by hand, whichever payer app reads it first, but for the case below. The re-sent request is routed to every current payer app (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.1), one newly added or holding no mark of its sender among them, so the cutoff is the identity's: the **block floor**, one `block_floor` record under `paykit_pay` (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.6), which every app that pays holds (Section 3.1). The floor only rises and never expires; a request created after it is dated above it. The floor is raised only as a blocked window closes, by each app once, or as a widening applies, never per message and never at the ask. A Paykit App holding `paykit_pay` that removes a sender from the deny list first raises the floor to the service time of its latest verified read plus the clock tolerance, building on the version it holds (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.6), and writes the unblock only once that write has succeeded; if the raise fails, it doesn't unblock, which fails closed, since a floor a little high only holds more requests for payment by hand. An app without it writes the unblock as any other app does, and each app holding `paykit_pay` raises the floor in the commit that records it. An app without `paykit_pay` raises nothing and holds nothing for the floor. For an unblock it didn't write, an app holding `paykit_pay` raises the floor in the commit in which it records the unblock, its read of the lists showing the sender gone from the deny list that held it, to the service time of its latest verified read plus the clock tolerance, before it processes any further entry of that sender and before it queues or sends any ask; and a restore that isn't clean raises it to the time of its first read of the lists, and a `missed` item to the earlier of the item's last acceptance time and the service time of the read that passes it, each in the commit that applies its widening (Section 7.2, What a restore loses and What a `missed` item loses). A clean restore marks no blocked sender and raises nothing for the restore, since no entry after the backup's position was passed, so nothing a blocked sender sent since was read and lost. The floor holds every one-time request of every sender dated before it, so it isn't raised while a window is open: then every own app that pays holds `paykit_contacts` (Section 3.1), sees the sender on its deny list and pays nothing for it automatically, and a blocked sender can't move the user's other one-time requests to payment by hand by sending more. Each unblock does move them: every one-time request of any sender still unpaid and created before it is paid only by hand, even when the blocked sender sent nothing. A copy that comes back once the window has closed, asked for or not, re-sent by its sender's own choice or answering another app's ask, finds the floor raised in every own app holding `paykit_pay` that saw the block, since a Paykit App holding `paykit_pay` raises it before it writes an unblock, and each app holding `paykit_pay` raises it at the unblock its read of the lists shows before it takes that sender's entries, and the raise is in the identity's chain before any entry the app takes after it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.3, One order per generation). What remains uncovered: an unblock that no app holding `paykit_pay` raised the floor before writing, one written outside Paykit or by an app without `paykit_pay`, leaves an own app that joins after it, before any own app holding `paykit_pay` that saw the block has raised the floor, with neither a deny list entry nor a mark of that sender, and a copy that comes back to it then opens unmarked. A block mark that goes, for the bound or its own expiry, needs no raise of its own: the unblock that closes its window, or the widening that made it, raises the floor. Until its raise is applied, an app holding `paykit_pay` holds that sender's entries unprocessed and asks it nothing, building the raise again on the floor read again (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.6); a widening whose raise isn't applied isn't applied either; the app applies both together at its next receive. A payer app opens the record of a one-time request whose signed `created_at` is earlier than its sender's block mark's latest time or the floor, plus the clock tolerance, with `manual_only` (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3), also when that record is written later. A record a read opened without it, at an entry whose read took a raise the entry's step didn't see yet, it marks once the read is done. The mark is never lifted (Section 8.3, Manual only), and every own app sees it, a restore included. Whatever a record holds, no app pays a one-time request so dated automatically: no executor pays it (Section 8.3, Executor), and a resume makes no repeat of its payment, since the repeat would be an automatic payment, nor a repeat of an automatic attempt's payment of a request whose record holds `manual_only` (Section 8.3, Manual only); the attempt stays open until the payment method's history or a receipt settles it. This is judged at the dispatch, not at the attempt's commit: immediately before each automatic dispatch of a one-time request, an executor's payment and a resume's repeat alike, the app reads the `block_floor` record again from its switch, as it reads the deny list again (How fresh the deny list is, below), and judges the request against the higher of the floor that read shows and the one it holds; it waits for no raise. A read that fails, or a record that doesn't read with none seen before, never counts as a floor that doesn't hold the request: the app makes no automatic dispatch of it on that read. So a raise applied before the attempt's commit, which the attempt's batch doesn't condition on, and one applied after that commit but before the dispatch, are both caught by that read. A raise applied after the dispatch began is too late for that payment, which was made under the floor as the read before it showed. While an app can't read the floor (its value sealed under a key the app doesn't hold, its payer records not listed yet, or a value that doesn't read with none seen before), every one-time request counts as so dated, and an unblock or a widening that must raise the floor is held until the app can read it. The floor holds a one-time request of any sender dated before it, which is the conservative side: one still unpaid when the floor passes its date is paid only by hand. A recurring request that comes back is taken as new, with no mark. A proof, receipt, list or reply that comes back is taken as any other. The app tells its host, once per sender and window and with no content, when a mark's window expires before an ask for it was delivered, when a bound on marks drops a mark of any family before an ask covering its latest time was delivered (Section 9, Messages dropped for a bound), and, at a window's first ask, for the part of it older than its sender keeps: what the counterparty's inbox accepted in that window can no longer come back, and a payment among it shows only in the payment method.
- **Messages already queued:** the send rules for a blocked counterparty apply when each delivery or re-send is attempted, including a message queued before the block, a retry after a delivery failure, a message a kept batch will queue, and a message carried by reload or restore. Queuing or signing a message while sends were permitted does not authorize its later delivery. This rule applies wherever this specification requires the app to judge the deny list; it does not grant `lists.read` to an app whose scopes do not include it. The app uses the same deny decision, including its own pending block or unblock, as How fresh the deny list is, below. Before attempting delivery of a message governed by these counterparty holds, it refreshes a copy at least 10 minutes old or not known. A failed refresh leaves that send held; it does not turn an unknown list into an empty one or authorize delivery from a stale copy. A block the app wrote itself holds these messages at once, before another flush or resume can deliver them. A deny change or gap the app has learned takes effect before any subsequent delivery decision. When reload, restore or provider loss leaves the copy unknown, a successful current read is required before a queued message governed by these holds can go.
  - The app holds nonempty Private Payment Lists, renewals and list answers, list requests, re-send requests, new Payment Requests and Billing Period details, subscription acceptances that do not report a dispatched payment and do not carry the exact user confirmation described below, rejections and payer cancellations, Allowance proposals, acceptances and rejections, and an Allowance end the app generates when a proposer's withdrawal crosses its acceptance while sends to that counterparty are held. For that generated end, the durable end and own-app notice still apply at once (Section 8.3, Ending). The same rule applies to their already queued copies and re-sends. A message whose role or effect the app cannot establish is not treated as an exception: it waits until the app can decide under these rules. A user's confirmation to pay a blocked counterparty authorizes only the acceptance and proof reporting that payment, not unrelated queued messages.
  - A payee's cancellation of a request, an eligible `allowance_end` initiated by the user under Section 8.3, a receipt already owed, the acceptance and proof of a payment dispatched before the block or on its user's confirmation, and an empty newer Private Payment List that withdraws the list still go while the recipient is blocked or the deny copy is unknown. This includes continuing or retrying their owed delivery from a kept batch or outbox, including an outbox carried by a restore only when Section 7.2's current evidence establishes the delivery is still owed, subject to the selective replacement of every carried list delivery by the fresh withdrawal required after restore (Section 7.2). An own-app `executor_notice` is not a counterparty send. These exceptions bypass only the counterparty's send hold: the certificate, current identity, restore's first read and other ordinary delivery requirements still apply. They neither release another message to the same recipient nor authorize a new message or a copy reconstructed from shared records after restore where Section 7.2 withholds it; Section 9 still decides responses to re-send requests. The app determines the exception from the signed message and the durable state that establishes its role and effect, not its kind alone: a payer cancellation differs from a payee cancellation, a subscription acceptance from a payment's acceptance, a nonempty list from a withdrawal, and a user's Allowance end from one generated automatically for a crossing withdrawal. The last two ends may have the same message kind and reference form; the exception requires durable evidence that this end was initiated by the user. The record holding an end, its already queued status or its signature alone does not grant that exception. If the app cannot establish the origin, the counterparty message stays held without undoing the end or its own-app notice.
  - A manual subscription acceptance may also finish its original delivery to a blocked payee when the user confirmed that exact blocked subscription under Sections 8.1 and 8.2. This permission requires durable evidence of that confirmation, bound to the exact signed acceptance, payee and request and written with the accepted response and its queued message. It does not follow from the message kind, signature, shared record or queued state alone. Unlike the payment-reporting exceptions above, it requires a fresh known deny decision; a failed or unknown read keeps it held. It permits only that original acceptance and retries of its still-owed original delivery, not a later re-send or a copy reconstructed from a shared record. It never releases an unconfirmed acceptance or another message to that payee.
  - The classification and hold must govern every path that can transmit the message: direct delivery, generic outbox flushing, payment resume, re-send, kept-batch resolution, reload and restore. Before handing any such path work that can send, the app durably retains the required per-message holds or otherwise prevents the held messages from being transmitted. It does this when establishing its local block decision or learning that the deny copy is unknown, before a client call can flush queued work as part of that operation. A batch may still record a stop or another effect that is required while sends are held; its queued message remains held where required. A failed hold update, an unknown batch outcome or a crash between queueing and delivery cannot leave an unrestricted delivery path. Reload and restore apply the same rule before any delivery; a clean restore verdict does not make the backup's deny copy current or release a counterparty hold. Holding or dropping the whole outbox is not a substitute, because the exceptions above remain deliverable. The publishing app's separate duty to remove every carried list delivery, including an empty withdrawal, and retain the obligation for a safely newer withdrawal before returning from restore still applies (Sections 7.2 and 8.4). Suppression and the durable obligation must also survive a crash or a kept batch's later application; an unsuccessful update cannot expose the carried list through another delivery path.
  - Holds survive restart and backup/restore, and a later call decides them again from the current deny state and all other applicable restrictions. Once sends are permitted, the app releases only the messages still eligible to go; an unblock does not override a stop, a restore's list withdrawal, or another hold. Holding never extends the signed message's replay deadline. Past that deadline the message is given up under the Switch's normal rules, never sent or made eligible by a later unblock (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11). The app retains the message's required effect and reports an undelivered message as the applicable rule requires; it never erases a stop or an owed payment outcome merely to suppress a send.
- **How fresh the deny list is:** for a payment the user starts, with a request or without one, the app judges the deny list as it read it at most 10 minutes before, the age Section 7.2 allows its copy of the payee's `identity.json` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3). Only when its copy is older, or it holds none, does it read the lists first. For an automatic payment, a resume's repeat of a dispatched payment, an executor's payment under an Allowance (Section 8.3) or a Billing Period paid without its user's confirmation (Section 8.2), it reads the deny list again before each dispatch, and before an automatic acceptance (Section 8.2). It reads the `block_floor` record again before each such dispatch of a one-time request, with the same rule for a read that fails (Blocked counterparties, above). A block the app wrote itself counts at once, for its payments, its sends and the spam rule. An unblock it wrote counts at once for its payments and sends, while the batch that carries it may still apply, and in the spam rule only once its copy of the lists shows it (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1). After a restore from a backup, the backup's copy counts as never read: the app reads the deny list again before any payment decision that needs a fresh copy (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1). A read that fails never counts as one that doesn't hold the counterparty: the app makes no payment to it on that read.
- **Labelled requests:** a hosted app holding `paykit_request:<label>` sends every request under its label and writes its records under that label's policy; the period details, receipts and cancellations of the request come from it as from any payee app. The payer's replies go to it and to the identity's apps holding `paykit_request`, which see the sale too (Section 3.1 and Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.4).
- **Messages that cross:** a payee that receives a valid proof for an attempt treats it as payment, even if it cancelled the request meanwhile; it may refund with a new request in the other direction. A payer that has recorded a payee's cancellation makes no new attempt, and one already attempting finishes and sends its proof. A payer app that has verified a payee's `payment_request_cancellation` MUST keep it durably in its own state before it does anything else with it: for a message it receives, in the commit that passes the entry, with no service operation in that step. The kept cancellation stops new attempts under Section 3.4 even before it can be placed in the request record. While that stop applies the app MUST make no new attempt for the request, and a restart, or a record write that failed or is still pending, never lifts it. The app records the cancellation in the request record by a later write of its own as soon as it can; until then the identity's other apps may not know of it, the same exposure as a cancellation not yet delivered. It keeps the copy at once whenever it holds the request record, also one whose value doesn't read or is sealed under a scope key it doesn't hold yet, since keeping it needs only the record's key: the stop never waits for a scope key, and giving up the entry never lifts it (Stored entries, below). It drops the copy once the request record holds a cancellation. While its copy of the payer's records may lack records and holds no record of the request, the entry is held unprocessed (Records not listed yet, below). A cancellation for a request whose record the app doesn't hold, once its copy of the records is listed, a record its copy holds as deleted counting as not held, is gated by the spam rule as a message that creates an activity record (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1): the app keeps such stops for records it doesn't hold under the bounds of a reply before its record (below), counted apart from those replies, at most 64 per sender identity and 128 in all, and may drop one 30 days after the cancellation's acceptance, judged on the service time it verified, but never while its copy may lack records or it keeps a stored message of that request to write (Stored entries, below); a stop it keeps for such a message counts outside these bounds and is never evicted. By then its sender re-sends nothing, and any own app that held the request got the cancellation too. A stop dropped earlier, for the bounds of 64 and 128, is dropped as one never processed: the client marked the cancellation processed when the app kept it, so the app forgets its pair in the commit that drops it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11), and a later re-send of the same cancellation is taken and kept again; the stop counts in its sender's mark of dropped replies, which asks that sender at once (Replies before their record, below; Section 9, Messages dropped for a bound). If the client cannot yet forget the pair of the ordinary stop this order selects, the app retains that stop and defers admission of the incoming ordinary stop as Waiting for admission, below, specifies; no counted stop is admitted above either bound. This does not defer a verified cancellation that this paragraph requires to be kept immediately, or change the stops it explicitly protects outside those bounds.
- **Replies before their record:** a payer's acceptance, rejection, cancellation or proof can reach a payee app before it holds the record of the request the reply names, for example a request another own app sent whose record this app hasn't read yet. The app keeps such a reply in its own state, under the spam rule, as a message that creates an activity record (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1), and places it in the record by a later write of its own once it holds the record. The same holds for a payee's period details at a payer app before it holds the record of their request. An app also keeps an Allowance acceptance or rejection that arrives before it can match the proposal, for example when another own app sent it. It counts, drops and marks these messages under the same reply bounds. For an Allowance reply it reads the pending and permanent keys in order and applies Section 8.3.1 to the signed proposal and reply. An acceptance takes effect only through the original acceptance batch in that section. A reply from the counterparty of a request whose record the app holds, or period details from the payee of a request whose record it holds, that it can't place yet because the period record, or, at a payer, the request record, doesn't open for good, read or bind, or because a proof names period details the period record doesn't hold, is no new state: the app keeps it outside the spam rule, within the same bounds, places it by a later write of its own once the record can take it, and after 30 days gives it up, telling the host (`MessageGivenUp`). A value waiting for its scope key holds the entry instead (Stored entries). A payee app that holds a request record whose value it can't open, read or bind, other than for want of a scope key, can't tell whether a reply naming it comes from its counterparty, since the record's key names only the request: it keeps the reply as a reply before its record, under the spam rule, places it once the record reads naming the reply's sender, and gives it up at its 30 days otherwise. No received message is written over a held record's value that the app can't read. A request reaching a payer app that holds the payer's record of it but can't open, read or bind it, other than for want of a scope key, is held, its entry unprocessed, until the record reads; once 30 days have passed since its acceptance the app takes it with no effect, telling the host (`MessageGivenUp`), and it stays processed. A proof of a payment without a request whose payee record, keyed by the endpoint and the settlement, the app holds but can't open, read or bind is new state, since that key doesn't bind its sender: it takes the spam rule, is kept as a reply before its record within those bounds, is written once the record reads naming its sender, and goes, with no notice, once it reads naming another; dropped for those bounds, it counts in its sender's mark and is asked for again as any reply (Section 9, Messages dropped for a bound). A message naming a request from anyone but its counterparty is never placed: a proof naming a Billing Period is new state, under the spam rule, and a response or cancellation is invalid and dropped. A payee's cancellation or a counterparty's `allowance_end` naming a record the app doesn't hold is kept as a stop instead (Messages that cross, above; Section 8.3). A receipt reaching a payer app that holds the payer's record it names but can't open or read it, other than for want of a scope key, is kept as a reply before its record is, counted, dropped and marked with those replies, under the spam rule for a payment without a request, whose record alone names its counterparty, and once the record reads it is written there with the effects its first read would have had on the attempt it names (Stored entries, below). It keeps a reply at most 30 days from its acceptance, judged on the service time it verified, the time its sender keeps it (Section 9), and at most 64 such replies per sender identity, the identity its certificate names, and 128 in all, the counts by which Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2 bounds the entries of a sender whose standing couldn't be checked. Past a sender's 64, the app drops that sender's reply accepted earliest; past 128 in all, the reply accepted earliest of the sender identity that holds the most such replies, and among sender identities holding the same most, the lower sender identity. Earliest means by acceptance time and, within one millisecond, in the order the inbox accepted them, the order the app's feed gives its entries in, never by `msg_id`, which a sender chooses. The selection includes the incoming reply. Admission and any required eviction commit atomically, with no committed ordinary reply pool above either bound. If the selected earlier reply is protected, admission waits as Waiting for admission, below, specifies; the selected earlier reply stays where it is, with its pair and effect unchanged. The app processes kept replies to one record in that order. For Allowance answers, Section 8.3.1 gives the effect of each order while the proposal is pending and the conflict rule after acceptance. A reply dropped for these bounds is dropped as one never processed: the client marked it processed when the app kept it, so the app forgets its pair in the commit that drops it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11), and a later re-send of the same message is taken. For each sender identity whose replies it dropped for these bounds, the app keeps one mark holding two times: the earliest acceptance time among those replies, which a re-send asks from, and the latest, which the mark expires by. A re-sent copy dropped again counts with its new acceptance time; a sender re-sends only messages it first accepted in the last 30 days (Section 9), so such copies move the latest time to at most 30 days after their first acceptance, and a mark that only re-sent copies feed ends at most 60 days after the replies it covers were first accepted. The mark asks that sender to re-send what its inbox accepted from the mark's earliest time (`resend_request` with `accepted_from`, `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.12) as Section 9, Messages dropped for a bound, says: at once, whatever records the app holds, since the record a dropped message names may come at any time, from another own app or a re-send, and again at most once in any 24 hours by its own clock (the reference interval) while the mark lives: a sender may leave selected messages out for its own limits, and its requester may ask again later (Section 9). A payee's cancellation, or a counterparty's `allowance_end`, kept as a stop and dropped for the bounds on those stops (Messages that cross, above) counts in its sender's mark the same way; a sender whose sends are held is asked nothing until they aren't (Blocked counterparties, above). The retry and unknown-delivery rules of Section 9, Messages dropped for a bound, apply to the ask. Asking doesn't change the mark, and the re-send may leave out replies the sender no longer keeps, for which nothing is owed. A re-sent reply follows the same rules as the original. The app retries its effect against the current record or keeps or drops it under these bounds while it cannot match the record. An Allowance reply follows Section 8.3.1. An effect already applied is not applied again (Stored entries, below). A mark goes 30 days after its latest time (Section 9), or, past 128 marks, when it is, among the marks an ask covering whose latest time was delivered, the one whose latest time is earliest (on a tie, the one of the lower sender identity); with none such, the one whose latest time is earliest among those whose ask was refused or can't be sent yet, the host told (Section 9, Messages dropped for a bound). A backup carries the marks with their times, so a restore neither loses nor resets the marks its backup held; a mark made after the backup goes with what the restore loses (Section 7.2), whose own requests for re-sends ask for those replies again. So a sender sees only ordinary `resend_request`s from each payee app, at most one in any 24 hours, while the app keeps dropping its replies and for 30 days after: an admitted sender can make a payee app send it no more than that.
- **Waiting for admission:** when the ordinary family and its required eviction order select an earlier message whose payload or processed pair the client still protects, the app does not admit the incoming message to that family. It requests plain admission waiting unless it also owes the protected cancellation described below. Both outcomes use the adopted [Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2](https://github.com/pubky/pubky-switch/blob/f50dcc7603f2ae182b0ed45b916300a398cb9ffe/spec/spec.md#82-shared-state) and its bounded waiting pool. The app discards the ordinary admission attempt's staged operations, local writes and pair forgets. The incoming message gains no processed pair. The client applies waiting-pool selection to the unadmitted authenticated input with its original identity, acceptance evidence, sequence and generation before its cursor passes. No Paykit payload queue is added, no already retained ordinary payload moves there, and no ordinary bound or required victim changes. If the ordinary rule instead selects the incoming message itself, its drop and recovery mark use the existing atomic unprocessed-consumption rule; it is not deferred merely to avoid that rule. A real inability to commit custody or the required drop notice is a real storage failure, not successful intake or a capacity wait with no durable obligation.
  - This decision comes after the rules that require an immediate durable stop or protect an attempt's first receipt. A known mandatory stop is kept immediately, including an end or conflict of an Allowance, as Messages that cross, above, and Section 8.3 require. A protected first receipt is kept under Stored entries, below. Neither may be postponed, discarded or relabeled as an ordinary admission wait; their existing retention and counting rules are unchanged. Waiting does not settle, release or forget the prior client's pending or uncertain effect, even if Paykit's monetary state is already settled.
  - An unlabelled request cancellation can have both directions when the two apps' certified roles permit them. The signed Payment Request at the exact referenced hash identifies its payee and payer. The payer may already hold that original in a stored entry whose record write is owed. The payee's original may have been signed by another app of the same identity. A reply carrying the hash, a deleted record or a record whose original cannot be verified leaves the direction unresolved. Apply Section 3.4 to the established direction. While both remain possible, each side keeps its own custody and admission duties.
  - If the payer must keep the cancellation immediately and admission of the same input as an ordinary payer's reply must wait, the app designates the exact Paykit-local writes needed to retain the signed original, its signing certificate, acceptance time and feed position. One commit keeps that custody and applies waiting-pool selection. If the incoming reply itself is selected for an immediate overflow drop, the same commit stores its payload-free drop notice and the protected stop. The cursor may pass only after the commit. If storage rejects it, the custody, waiting pool and cursor stay as they were. If its result is unknown, the client reloads the committed state before retrying. On replay or reopening, an already retained stop keeps its original order and the app judges the ordinary reply again, even when custody needs no further write. A layered backup carries the protected stop in Paykit's state and any waiting input or owed notice under the client's own rules. Expiry or eviction of the waiting reply leaves the protected stop available for placement under Messages that cross.
  - Admission waits share the client's 21-day, 64-per-sender and 128-total waiting pool with sender-verification and key waits, on equal terms under the client's custody order, not Paykit's ordinary eviction order. The client applies those bounds atomically on custody admission, so the incoming copy may itself be selected for a custody drop. A retry keeps the same stored-input identity, original acceptance and age. It rechecks the current records, authorization, deny state and required ordinary victim through the client's bounded fair retry schedule, without same-read spinning or a global intake hold. Admission does not restart the ordinary family's retention period. Reload and backup preserve custody or an owed drop notice and the original evidence. Successful processing retires eligible stored duplicates under the generic completion rule; independent uncertainty remains protected. Admission is not guaranteed while the prior victim remains protected.
  - If the client expires or selects an admission-waiting copy for its custody bound, its durable notice distinguishes that reason and carries the established sender and message identity, original acceptance evidence, sequence and generation, without a payment body. The app counts this loss in the existing dropped-reply recovery mark of that sender (Replies before their record; Section 9), never the unverified or key-wait mark and never a new family. It acknowledges the client notice only in the local transaction that durably records the mark changes and any host notice those changes require; a repeated notice does not count a second loss or reset its times. It creates no incoming processed pair and forgets no independently protected pair, copy or effect merely because custody of this copy was lost. The notice is not a resolution or release of an uncertain effect. The mark's ordinary authorization, deny checks, bounds, ask cadence, retention and host-notice rules apply; no extra request, payment detail or contact information is sent to the sender. An ask and a notice guarantee neither delivery nor recovery after custody expiry or overflow.
- **Stored entries:** an app stores an entry locally, moves its cursor past it and tells Paykit when the service refuses its effect for good, or the effect doesn't fit one batch or the app's store, or the effect's fate couldn't be known, and when it drops an entry whose sender's standing couldn't be checked (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2). An entry whose record write hits a per-kind limit or the storage quota (`429 quota_exceeded`), or would take the record over the 256 KiB limit for a shared record value (`413 too_large`), is stored too, and its record is written once room frees (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2), or its message is given up as below. Paykit keeps the message of an entry whose effect was refused, whose record write waits for room, or whose fate couldn't be known, and of one whose record's view shows it divergent (Section 6, Divergent records), whatever its kind, and retries its effect as soon as the records it needs can take it (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.3, Recorded). The app writes a request, period details and a receipt into the payer's record of the request, the Billing Period or the payment; a payer's acceptance, rejection, cancellation or proof into the payee's record of the request or the Billing Period; a proof of a payment without a request into the payee's record of its settlement, which the write may open (Section 8.4); and a Private Payment List into the `payment_lists` record, held until it is written, a newer list from its publishing app replacing an older one. When an Allowance proposal, acceptance or rejection waits for a record's scope key, the app follows the key-wait rule below. If a local read succeeds but the app cannot read the current value or bind it to the counterparty and Allowance ID for another reason, it immediately takes durable custody before its cursor passes the entry. This applies to a proposal, acceptance or rejection over a held permanent `allowances` record, and to a proposal over a held pending `allowance_proposals` record. An acceptance or rejection that the app cannot match to a pending proposal when it holds no permanent record follows Replies before their record, above. After 30 days from the original inbox acceptance, measured using the highest service time it has verified, the app may end ordinary placement. Before passing or releasing the entry, it must still retain its authenticated evidence or a conservative conflict stop whenever the message could establish a conflict. It bounds the evidence it keeps for each affected record. If another distinct input exceeds that bound, the stop must still cover every unresolved input. The host can distinguish expired placement with a retained conflict hold from a message given up without such a hold. Acknowledging the report does not release the hold. The app resolves the evidence against a qualified record before allowing the affected action. An established permanent conflict is never cleared (Section 8.3). Only an exact discard or a verified deletion covering the affected pending lifetime can retire its conflict stop (Section 8.3.1). An entry waiting only for sender lists or an incomplete record listing uses the bounded feed wait (Section 17, question 27). For a stored Allowance proposal, acceptance or rejection it reads the pending key and then the permanent key and applies Section 8.3.1, including the original order of replies. Over a record whose view shows it divergent, it keeps, of each role of the record, the earliest message its inbox accepted and, of each sender, the 16 latest after it, at most 34 in all, and for a `payment_lists` record the list of the highest version; past 34, the role's earliest aside, over a record that reads the earliest message of a sender other than its counterparty goes, and over one that doesn't, the earliest of the sender keeping the most, the lower sender identity among those keeping as many, receipts aside, as below; once its user confirms, it writes them in the order its inbox accepted them, each placed as the receive step places it (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3): into its role when the record holds none for that role, period details among the period's details (Section 8.2), and every other one reflected by its `msg_id`. Receipts over a payer record are bounded by attempt, not by sender or role: the first receipt from the record's counterparty its inbox accepted for each attempt ID that the app's own journal holds, or that the claim of the obligation the receipt names shows, is kept whatever the bound, never dropped for it and counted outside the 34; only a further receipt for an attempt, or one naming an attempt neither shows, counts under the bound, and one it drops is asked for again (Section 9, Messages dropped for a bound). A receipt is the payee's evidence that settles an attempt whose fate the payer doesn't know (Section 7.3): dropped, the first receipt of an attempt would leave it failed or unknown, and a payment without a request, which nothing but its record and its receipt shows paid, could then be paid again by hand. Every other write over that record that adds no stop, a new message the receive step takes, a stored entry, a kept reply or a held list, waits behind the messages kept over it that its inbox accepted before it, also after the confirmation and once the view has cleared, so none takes the role of one accepted earlier, and a new message the receive step takes meanwhile is kept beside them. A kept message that doesn't point to its record any more, one kept over a later write than the one the app holds at that key, or a proof of a payment without a request whose settlement, as its payment method reads it now, isn't the one the record is keyed by, isn't written, and is given up at its 30 days as any kept message; what waits for the kept messages waits only for those that still point to the record. It writes them before anything is paid, accepted, credited or marked done on that record. The bound drops the messages of a role between the earliest and the 16 latest, proofs among them, each as one never processed: the app forgets its pair in the commit that drops it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11), so a later re-send of it is taken, and counts it in its sender's mark of dropped replies (Replies before their record), which asks for it again (Section 9, Messages dropped for a bound), so a proof of a payment made twice while the record was divergent comes back while its sender keeps it. The selection includes the message the receive step is taking. If it selects that incoming message, the step commits its drop and recovery mark without keeping its payload or marking it processed. If it selects an earlier ordinary message whose pair or effect the client still protects, the app keeps that earlier message and defers the incoming admission as Waiting for admission, above, specifies; it neither chooses another victim nor commits an ordinary counted pool above its bound. The role's earliest message, protected first receipts and immediate stops retain their stated protection and counting rules. The rule holds as well for any kind recorded later. A payee's cancellation and a counterparty's `allowance_end` are kept as stops at once instead, and placed as Messages that cross, above, and Section 8.3 say. The app never writes over a record value it can't read or open, which may hold what another own app stored. It tries for 30 days from the message's acceptance, judged on the service time it verified, the time its sender keeps it (Section 9), and may then give the message up, telling the host (`MessageGivenUp`, the notice it also gives for a message the client drops while it waits for a scope key, below); either way the message stays processed. The 30 days run also for a message kept over a divergent record whose user hasn't confirmed it, since its sender keeps it no longer. A message waiting behind messages kept over its record keeps its own 30 days, and may be given up at them, never while the app's copy may lack its record; a held list stays held. What a given-up message loses depends on its kind: a request is never shown; period details are never paid by; a payer's acceptance, rejection, cancellation or proof never shows in the payee's record; a proof of a payment without a request opens no record of its settlement; a receipt never shows in the payer's record and settles no attempt; a given-up Allowance proposal or reply no longer waits for its outstanding effect under Section 8.3.1; and a Private Payment List is never paid to. The app retains the stop from a payee's cancellation, an Allowance end or a later rejection of a pending proposal under Section 8.3.1. A message whose record the app's copy may lack (Records not listed yet, below) is neither written nor given up by the app meanwhile; only the client's drop of one waiting for a scope key, below, gives it up then. Giving a message up ends only its record write, and never lifts a stop. The same later write compensates for an effect whose fate couldn't be known, since a record that already holds the message isn't written again, and once the app has written it, it declines a released copy of the entry (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2), so the effect isn't applied twice. For entries dropped unverified, it keeps a mark of their own per sender identity, with the same two times, bounds, expiry, backup and cadence as a dropped reply's (Replies before their record): it asks that sender at once to re-send what its inbox accepted from the mark's earliest time to its latest (`accepted_from` and `accepted_until`, `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.12), and again at most once in any 24 hours by its own clock while the mark lives (Section 9, Messages dropped for a bound), and a message it already holds isn't applied twice. A message whose effect needs a scope key the app doesn't hold yet, a payee's cancellation and a counterparty's `allowance_end` aside, which never wait for one (Messages that cross, above, and Section 8.3), waits for that key in the client's local store, past the cursor, under the bounds that Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2 gives for stored entries: 21 days from its acceptance, judged on the service time the app verified, 64 per sender identity and 128 in all, counted together with entries whose sender's standing couldn't be checked and entries waiting for admission, above. Past them the client drops first every such entry kept past the 21 days, then, past a sender's 64, that sender's entries accepted earliest, then, past 128 in all, one at a time, the entry accepted earliest of the sender identity that holds the most such entries, the three waiting reasons counted and ordered alike (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 8.2 and 19, question 32). The client asks for the key by one full-bundle request for all such entries, again at most hourly while a key is still missing, and tries the message again once a key bundle brings that key (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 3.5 and 8.2), and only after it has opened the records the key opens, so the message never runs on a record still held unopened. Once the key comes, the message is processed, or held as Records not listed yet, below, says while the copy may lack its record. If the client drops it first, the message was never processed: the app tells the host once that it is given up (`MessageGivenUp`), even while its copy may lack the message's record, and keeps a mark of its own for such messages per sender identity, with the same two times, bounds, expiry and backup as a dropped reply's (Replies before their record), from which it asks that sender to re-send, at the same cadence, only once it holds every key those messages waited for (Section 9, Messages dropped for a bound). A copy sent again is processed as a new message.
- **Undelivered messages:** when the client gives up a message this app sent that its store never saw the counterparty's inbox accept (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11), and an activity record of this app holds it in one of its roles, the app tells its host once, naming the record's counterparty, the message and the record's request, and keeps the record as it is. The host may conclude only that this store never saw it accepted, not that the counterparty never got it: after a restore, the device the backup came from may have delivered it, so a new request in its place may be paid as well as the first. This host notice requires an activity record. The unknown-delivery duty above applies even without one, including to a `resend_request`, a list request or a list.
- **Records not listed yet:** an app's copy of the shared records lacks records until it has listed them: from the moment it joins, after a restore, after a `missed` item until the app has listed them again (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2), and after it takes a certificate that reads collections, or members of one, that the certificate it last listed under, or any certificate it held since, didn't, until it holds that certificate's scope keys and has listed them (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 3.5 and 8.3). After a `missed` item, the client itself lists again, in the read that passes the item and, past its page budget, in the reads after it, before it hands the protocol any entry, a stored one included, and a read whose listing fails ends there, failed, the next read listing first (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2; Switch wire 7.4.4), so this rule covers what the app decides between the item and that listing. Until then a record's absence from the copy decides nothing about a collection not yet listed. An authenticated visible delete is not absence, also after listing and at an app with no seen state, such as a new app or one restored from an old backup. For any potentially protected key, it imposes Section 6's Visible deletion without a copy rule: an incoming request, period message, proof or list cannot open an unmarked replacement, and no claim attempt, payment or credit is authorized by replaying that message. An app with seen state writes nothing over the delete but the protective write-back Section 6 requires, and a replay supplies no new authority. A record is treated as genuinely absent only when the app has no retained evidence of it and the service shows neither a live value nor a delete; a key legitimately forgotten after all honest witnesses were removed remains the explicit loss limit (Section 6, When a record is lost). A settlement mark still forbids credit independently (Section 8.1 step 4). A reply is kept before its record (Replies before their record), and a payee's cancellation is kept as a stop for a request not held. The service refuses deletion of the permanent Allowance record. A current read that loses a previously observed permanent record in the same account history raises the rollback alarm (Section 8.3.1). A received message of a kind that is recorded (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.3), whose effect is decided from whether the app holds a record of such a collection, is held unprocessed until the records are listed, as are period details the app would take with no effect from what such a record holds. The app reports the missing listing as a protocol deferral (Section 17, question 27) and checks the original message again after listing. A message about a record the copy holds takes its effect from that record: its write builds on the version the app holds, or, where the switch's current write is one the app refused, on that write's version, under Section 6, Writing over a refused write; and a newer write at the switch fails it, so the effect is built again on that write, at the latest at the next read. One whose effect needs a scope key of a certificate the app took and doesn't hold yet waits for that key as Stored entries, above, says, and once the key comes is held this way until the records are listed. Today these are a payment request, period details, a payer's acceptance, rejection or cancellation, a payee's cancellation, a proof, a receipt, a Private Payment List, and an Allowance proposal, acceptance, rejection or end. The app checks `allowance_proposals` and then `allowances` for that counterparty and ID (Section 8.3.1). The rule holds as well for any kind recorded later.

For an authenticated input whose ordinary admission must wait, both waiting outcomes of [Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2 at revision f50dcc76](https://github.com/pubky/pubky-switch/blob/f50dcc7603f2ae182b0ed45b916300a398cb9ffe/spec/spec.md#82-shared-state) apply.

When a message would create new activity and the app's lists do not establish the sender's standing, the app defers that delivery with a short reason naming only the missing prerequisite. A receipt that updates an existing record may still apply (wire Section 4.1). The host's read report identifies the protocol and the waiting delivery. The client retries the original delivery with current data and continues with other eligible deliveries in their normal order. An entry held on the feed keeps its bounded wait. When Section 8.1 calls for immediate local custody, the app stores the original entry before its cursor passes it. A local store failure while checking the prerequisite or retaining the delivery follows the client's storage failure rule. An app whose lists are too large to fit one store commit cannot refresh its copy and defers new activity until they fit. The default limits fit.

**Atomic receive outcomes.** Completion retires eligible stored copies with the processed pair and local effect. Forgetting an absent pair cannot erase an uncertain effect. Unprocessed consumption commits the local writes, pair forgets, input removal and retry-state removal together, with no remote operation or input processed pair. Plain admission waiting discards the callback's staged effects. Under the adopted Section 8.2 waiting rules, a protocol that owes immediate local custody can designate its repeatable local writes. The client discards the ordinary staged work in both cases, including remote operations and pair forgets. It commits the designated writes, if any, together with waiting-pool selection and any resulting payload-free drop notice before passing the input. Neither outcome adds a processed pair. Both use the same 21-day, 64-per-sender and 128-total bounds and fair retry order. Paykit's ordinary pools, eviction victims, mandatory stops, first receipts and drop marks keep their own rules (Sections 3.3, 8.1, 8.3 and 9).

**Held inbox entries.** When the Switch client holds an entry or processes it ahead of its cursor, it saves the entry ID, acceptance time, verifying service key and signed page time from that first observation. It keeps them until the cursor passes the entry. A later signed page in the same sequence that shows a different entry, a different acceptance time or a different kind of item the client reads at that position raises a rollback alarm (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 8.2 and 10.1, and Switch wire 7.4.4).

### 8.2 Recurring request

- **Billing Periods** follow one rule, the anchored period rule of `specs/allowances.md` applied to the request's `recurrence`. Boundary `k` is `anchor` plus `k * every` units: for minutes, hours, days and weeks a fixed number of seconds; for months and years a calendar step computed from the original anchor, clamped to the target month's last day when the anchor's day doesn't exist. Period `k` is `[boundary(k), boundary(k+1))`.
  - The first Billing Period is the one containing `starts_at`; the last is the one containing the instant just before `ends_at`, when set.
  - Obligation IDs name a period by its start boundary in Unix seconds (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3). Messages name it by `{starts_at, ends_at}`, which must equal a computed period; anything else is rejected.
- **Accepting:** when the user accepts the subscription, or an executor accepts it under an Allowance, the payer records the acceptance in the request record, in a batch conditioned on the version of the request record it read and, when the request has a proposal expiry, on `server_time_in(null, proposal_expires_at + 1 ms)`, and sends it after that batch succeeds. The expiry is inclusive: its own millisecond is the last at which the acceptance is recorded. A rejection is recorded the same way, so an acceptance and a rejection can't both go out. An executor accepts a subscription under an Allowance only while the conditions of Section 8.3, Executor, hold and the Allowance's terms are valid and in force at the current server time, capacity being checked only when each period is paid, as in v0.1, and the request matches those terms: the request's asset is the Allowance's, its amount is within `per_payment_amount` where set, and, when the terms list allowed identifiers, the request can be paid to at least one of them, as in v0.1 (`specs/allowances.md`, "Payment Request integration"). Its acceptance's batch also carries `record_version(Allowance record) == r`, as an executor batch does (Section 8.3), so it can't pin an Allowance another own app has just ended, and writes `allowance_id`, naming that Allowance, into the request record, so the Allowance is pinned at acceptance, as v0.1 pins it (`specs/allowances.md`, "One-time and recurring requests"). Accepting takes the guards of paying a request: while the request is held after a restore or a `missed` item (Section 7.2), no app accepts it automatically, and one accepts it by hand only after its user has seen that the payee may have cancelled it; and while the deny list holds the payee, an app accepts it only after its user confirms, having been told that the payee is blocked (Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties). Each period then has its own obligation, attempts, proof, optional period details, optional receipt and period record. A period record of a request the app holds and the payer has accepted is an update of that request's activity, not a new activity record under the spam rule (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1).
- **Paying periods:** accepting a subscription pays nothing by itself, as in v0.1 (`specs/payment-requests.md`, "Recurring Payment Requests And Subscriptions"). A payer pays a Billing Period, by hand or automatically, only of a subscription its request record shows accepted, as in v0.1, where acceptance "makes the request active" and only an active one is paid (`specs/payment-requests.md`, "Recurring Payment Requests And Subscriptions"). Under the Allowance its request record names, which for a recurring request only an executor's acceptance under that Allowance writes (Accepting, above), or under the one the user moved the period to (Section 8.3, One Allowance per request), its executor pays a Billing Period automatically, and that payment's proof carries its `allowance_id` (Section 8.3). An executor, or an app paying a period by its own policy without its user's confirmation (below), pays automatically only from one Billing Period on: the request's first Billing Period when the payer's acceptance of the subscription was recorded before that period starts, else the period that contains the time that acceptance was recorded, the service time of that write (Accepting, above), and no period when that time is after the request's last Billing Period has ended; the periods keep the boundaries the request's recurrence gives them (Billing Periods, above). An app reads that recorded time as the service time of the request record's write it holds, which is the acceptance's or a later write's; both give the same periods paid automatically, since every period that ended before a later write's time has an automatic window that ended too (below); while the write it holds has no service time that a proof or a verified stub dates (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3), it pays no period automatically. It pays each of these periods automatically only within the period's automatic window, which ends at the earlier of its attempt deadline, when the request has a payment deadline (Section 7.1: the period's start plus the deadline's `seconds`), and the start of the next Billing Period, for every request, with a payment deadline or without one, judged on the highest service time it has verified. The window is judged again immediately before the attempt's first dispatch (Section 7.2 step 2): an attempt whose period's window has ended by then is written `failed`, never dispatched, and its period is left to payment by hand; a repeat that completes a payment already dispatched isn't judged, since money may already have moved. A period that ended before that acceptance, as for a subscription whose `starts_at` lies in the past, is paid only by hand, after its user's confirmation for that period, and so is any period whose automatic window ended before it was paid; paid by hand, a period still has its own payment deadline (Section 7.1). So an executor, or an app paying by its own policy, that accepts late or comes back after being offline never pays a run of earlier periods at once. For example, with monthly periods anchored at 2027-01-01, a `starts_at` of 2027-01-15 and an acceptance recorded at 2026-12-15, no period contains the acceptance, and the first period paid automatically is the request's first, `[2027-01-01, 2027-02-01)`, the one containing `starts_at`; and with daily periods and a 7-day payment deadline, each period's automatic window ends when the next period starts, so an executor offline for three days pays none of the three periods that ended meanwhile automatically, and each of them is paid by hand, within its own 7-day deadline. A period moved to another Allowance later (One Allowance per request, Section 8.3) is paid under it on the same terms. A subscription whose acceptance named no Allowance, one its user accepted by hand among them, has no Billing Period paid under an Allowance, and no period's payment names one, as in v0.1, where an Allowance is a candidate only for a request that remains proposed and a manual response excludes automatic handling (`specs/allowances.md`, "Payment Request integration"; `specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Durable Allowance payment accounting"). Without an Allowance, for a request whose record names none (Section 8.3, One Allowance per request), whether the paying app asks its user before each period is its own policy, as in v0.1. A period it pays without its user's confirmation for that period is an automatic payment for every rule of this spec that names one (Sections 6, 7.1, 7.2, 8.1 and 8.2, and Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6 step 5), though made under no Allowance. That includes the `manual_only` mark: no such payment is made of a Billing Period whose period record holds it, or whose request's record does (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3), with or without an Allowance, and its attempt's batch writes that period record at the version it read, as an executor's does (Section 8.3), so a mark written since makes the batch fail. Its proof, like that of a payment the user confirmed, carries no `allowance_id` (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.10).
- **Period details:** once the payer has accepted the subscription, as v0.1 issues quotes only then (`specs/payment-conversion.md`, "Payment Conversion Quote"), the payee may send `payment_period_details` for a Billing Period the payer stores them for (below), with that period's conversion rates, its destination, or both. Rates come only for a request whose conversion terms are `per_period`, as v0.1 takes a quote only on such a request (`specs/payment-conversion.md`, "Payment Conversion Quote"): a request's fixed rates price every period, and one without conversion terms promises none, so period details with rates are invalid against either, and the payer takes them with no effect (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.9). The payee app that sends them holds the request's record and writes them into its `payee_activity` period record first, conditioned on the version it read, and sends them only after that write succeeds, so the identity's other payee apps can check a proof that names them; it sends none once that record holds 8 for the period. Each carries `expires_at` and stays valid until then: a later one replaces none, as in v0.1. The payer may pay by any valid one its period record holds, by default the latest that carries what the payment needs, a destination when the request has `per_period_endpoints` and the period isn't the first, and rates when the payment is priced by them, ordered by `created_at` and then `msg_id`; it pays by one, never a destination from one and rates from another, and waits while none carries both when it needs both; its proof references the one it used. A period has at most 8 period details: the payee sends no more, the payer's period record holds them all (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3), and the payer pays by none its period record doesn't hold. A payer stores period details only for a request it has accepted, as v0.1 issues quotes only after acceptance, and only for a Billing Period whose record doesn't show it paid and that is, on the highest service time it has verified, the next to start, or one that has started and, with a payment deadline, whose deadline hasn't passed, or, without one, is one of the 16 latest to have started; it takes any other details, and those of a request it has rejected or that is cancelled, with no effect. Each app judges this on the service time it has itself verified, never on the other's: a payee that has verified none yet can't tell which periods have started and sends no period details, whatever the payer has verified, and a payer that has verified none yet holds the details it would store until it has verified one, whatever the payee has verified; details of a request it hasn't accepted, has rejected, that is cancelled or whose payee's cancellation it keeps, and for a period whose record shows it paid, the payer takes with no effect all the same. So a payee can make a payer hold period records only for periods it may still pay.
- **Rates and payment time:** as with v0.1's conversion quotes, the rates of period details apply to a payment whose independently verified payment time is from their start through their `expires_at`, compared at millisecond precision. Their start is their signed `created_at`, cut to whole seconds, which is v0.1's `valid_from`; the payee sets `created_at` to the time it signs them and never backdates them, as in v0.1. That time comes from the payment method's evidence (`specs/payment-conversion.md`), never from when the proof arrived. Both sides apply it. The payer pays by the rates only while its device clock and the highest service time it has verified are both at or after their start and before `expires_at`, checked again just before money moves (Section 7.2); otherwise it waits for valid period details, or pays to an endpoint of the requested asset; after the host authorized a quote, either choice needs a new decision under Section 7.2. The payee credits a payment at those rates only if its verified payment time is from their start through `expires_at`, and a payee app does so only once its period record holds the details the proof names: it never credits at rates it can't read. A payment verified outside that interval doesn't pay the period at those rates. As in v0.1, late evidence isn't malformed: the payee still credits the funds received and may label them underpaid, late, or both, but they pay no occurrence and the proof isn't credited: it stores and sends no receipt for the period, whose record keeps the proof and stays unpaid, so the rule of Section 8.1 doesn't count them as a credit; Paykit adds no refunds, disputes or resolution. A payment to an endpoint of the requested asset is paid 1:1 without rates, so this bound doesn't apply to it, and the destination of period details is used while they haven't expired (Section 7.2). An Allowance counts the amount the payment transfers in the requested asset, never less than the requested amount, for its reservation, its spending and every limit, so a transfer above that amount counts whole and doesn't fit at a limit's edge where the requested amount would; an executor cannot use it to pay at a rate from the request or period details (Section 8.3). Authorizing a quote under the payer's own policy confers no Allowance authority and does not extend a Billing Period's automatic window.
- **Destinations per period:** without `per_period_endpoints`, the request's destination serves every period. With it, the request's `payment_endpoints` serve only the first period: the payer never attempts a later period before receiving period details with its destination, and a payee credits a later period only for a payment to the destination of period details its proof names. A payee app credits a Billing Period's proof only once it can read the period's request record, against whose amount and destination it checks the proof: while it holds that record but can't open it, it credits nothing until it can, and no confirmation of its user stands in for it.
- **One period per payment:** a Billing Period's credit writes its settlement's mark as a credit does (Section 8.1 step 4), naming the request and the period, so one payment pays one Billing Period of one subscription at most, as in v0.1 ("A single payment must not satisfy multiple requests or periods", `specs/payment-conversion.md`, "Payment Conversion Quote"). Late evidence, which pays no occurrence (Rates and payment time, above), stores no receipt and writes no mark.
- **Not before its start:** no app pays a Billing Period automatically before it starts, and the service enforces it: every attempt on a period carries `server_time_in(s, null)`, so the service applies it only from `s` on its own clock, whatever the payer's clock says. `s` is the period's start. A payer pays a period before its start only by hand, after its user confirms paying that period early; that attempt carries as `s` the latest service time the app has verified when it builds the attempt, after that confirmation, so the service still never applies it before the user agreed. For a subscription that could have been paid under the payer's lost provider, the start of the selected period and confirmation to pay early or resume the subscription leave the historical payment check in Section 7.3 in force.
- **Tombstones:** each paid period leaves a small tombstone, kept 400 days after its last entry when the request has no payment deadline (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3) and counted against the paying app's share of the claim limit. Without a payment deadline the payer also keeps the period's record for the identity's life (Section 6). So payees of frequent subscriptions should set one.
- **Attempted periods:** a payer's request record lists, in `attempted_periods`, the start of every Billing Period that may have an open or `unknown` attempt: at most 16, in ascending order, each once. An attempt on a period not listed writes the request record in the attempt's batch, adding the period, at the record's next version; the attempt is already conditioned on the version it read (Section 7.2 step 1). An attempt on a listed period doesn't write it. A write of the request record may drop a listed period whose claim has no open or `unknown` attempt, conditioned on that claim's step as the writer read it (`claim_step`), so a new attempt on that period meanwhile makes the write fail. Apps drop finished periods whenever they write the record anyway, for example when adding one. With 16 listed and none to drop, an app makes no attempt on a period not listed until one of them finishes. So every period with an open attempt is listed in the version of the request record its attempt's batch left, the one it was conditioned on or the one it wrote, and stays listed until its claim has no open attempt. A period whose claim holds an `unknown` attempt (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2) stays listed as one with an open attempt does, and refuses the cancellation the same way, until its owner reports it, a receipt settles it, or, once its owner isn't current, another own app fails it after its user confirms (Section 7.3; Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2 rows 2, 3 and 5). This covers every period an attempt may pay: the current one, one paid early by hand (Not before its start, above), and a past one whose payment deadline, which can be longer than the period, hasn't passed.
- **Ending:** either side's cancellation ends the subscription. A payer records its cancellation in the request record, in a batch that writes the record at its next version, so conditioned on the version it read, and conditioned on the claim of every listed period being at the step read with no open or `unknown` attempt (`claim_step`), as in Section 8.1, and sends it after that batch succeeds. An attempt on a period not listed writes the request record, and one on a listed period moves its claim's step, so an attempt and a cancellation can't both succeed. A listed period with an open or `unknown` attempt refuses the cancellation: the app shows that the payment is in progress or, for an `unknown` attempt, that it may already be paid, as for a one-time request. A payee's cancellation of a recurring request stops the payer app as Section 8.1 says (Messages that cross): the app MUST keep a verified `payment_request_cancellation` durably in its own state before it does anything else with it, and from then on MUST make no new attempt on any of the request's Billing Periods, whatever its request record shows; a restart, or a record write that failed or is still pending, never lifts the stop. It records the cancellation in the request record by a later write of its own as soon as it can.
- **Ended:** a recurring request whose recurrence sets `ends_at` has ended once its last Billing Period has ended, at that period's end, which may lie after `ends_at`, since the last period is whole (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.4), judged on the highest service time the app has verified, where v0.1 used the wallet's trusted local time. As in v0.1 this is a view, not a message: nobody sends a cancellation for it, and a proof of an earlier period may still arrive (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Recurring Scheduling"). v0.1 counted it from `ends_at` itself, which in v0.2 can fall inside a period still to be paid. Reaching its end closes nothing: a request that has ended may still be accepted, and then shows as ended, as in v0.1, where reaching `ends_at` "is not a lifecycle transition" (`specs/payment-requests.md`, "Recurring Payment Requests And Subscriptions").

### 8.3 Allowances

Allowances are built entirely in apps on claims and shared records. The switch has no Allowance endpoints, objects or rules.

| Allowance concept | Primitive |
|---|---|
| An unanswered proposal | Ordinary record in `allowance_proposals` |
| Accepted proposal and terms, subsequent conflict, end and executor grant | Permanent record in `allowances` |
| Ledger | One shared record per Allowance in `allowance_ledgers`: its initial ledger (Section 8.3.1), then an entry per attempt and running totals |
| Payment | The request's claim (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2) |

Record keys are derived from the counterparty and the `allowance_id` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3).

**Setting up.** In the first release the same Allower app obtains the user's approval, creates the initial ledger and grants itself execution authority (Section 8.3.1). Another app must leave execution held. The handoff and change-of-executor rules below are requirements for a later release. The wallet shows the counterparty, terms and its own role as executor in one user decision. Merely sending or accepting a proposal supplies no consent to automatic payment. Before paying, it checks the accepted terms against the signed proposal and acceptance stored in the Allowance record, and writes its **executor grant** there: its app ID and the terms hash (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3), signed with its own signing key over the identity, the counterparty, the `allowance_id` and its `confirmed_generation`, the current generation (Pubky Switch spec, Terms and Section 4.3). It must hold `paykit_allowance_execute`. The grant authorizes payment only once its executor has established a complete current anchor under it (A grant implies a ledger, below).

**Executor.** An app pays automatically under an Allowance only while all of these hold, besides the checks of Paying under an Allowance (below): the Allowance record holds its own grant for the current terms and counterparty, no end or rejection, and isn't conflicted (Conflicted, below), and its current write isn't one the executor ignores, unless its user has confirmed the Allowance since (Ending, below); its **consent list**, kept durably in its own state, holds the Allowance; the request comes from that counterparty and is priced in the Allowance's own asset, as in v0.1 (Section 16); the request record names this Allowance for the occurrence, or, for a one-time request, names none yet (One Allowance per request, below); its copy of the deny list, read again before the dispatch (Section 8.1, How fresh the deny list is), is complete and doesn't hold that counterparty (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1); the Allowance record, read again from the service immediately before the first dispatch, once the attempt is journaled and its batch has succeeded (Section 7.2 step 1, where an executor checks its Allowance again), as the deny list and the `block_floor` record are read again before it, still holds all of the above at the version the attempt's batch was conditioned on: another own app may have ended, rejected or handed off the Allowance after that batch, and the executor's copy doesn't show it yet; a read that fails, or a record that doesn't read, pays nothing, and a resume's repeat is held as Attempts already open (below) says; neither the occurrence's record nor, for a Billing Period, its request's record is marked manual-only (below), and its request, if a one-time one, isn't dated before the block floor as a read of the `block_floor` record made immediately before the dispatch shows it, nor does that read fail or show a floor the executor can't read (Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties): the executor's batch doesn't condition on the floor's version, so that read, not the batch, is what a raise meets; it keeps no stop for the request (Sections 8.1 and 8.2) or for the Allowance (Ends reach the executor, below); the Allowance record's response is settled: the executor keeps no answer to the Allowance's proposal that it hasn't written into the record yet, kept before its record or stored (Section 8.1, Replies before their record; Stored entries), the reference client stores no entry from that counterparty that may be one (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2), and that counterparty owes it no re-send of replies dropped for the bounds on kept replies (Section 8.1), since an outstanding distinct answer can conflict with the recorded acceptance (Conflicted, below), and Section 8.3.1 governs a reply to a proposal still pending; a kept answer or a mark it can't read pays nothing either; neither is held after a restore or a `missed` item (Section 7.2); the Allowance isn't paused after a provider loss (After provider loss, below), and none of its records, nor the occurrence's, a Billing Period's request record among them, is divergent (Section 6); its user has confirmed the Allowance again since any restore that isn't clean (Ends reach the executor, below); its ledger is one it may build on (Building on a version accepts it; A grant implies a ledger) and can take the new entry (Compaction, below); no rollback or misbehaviour of the service holds its attempts (Sections 7.1 and 7.2); and the obligation isn't doubtful (Doubtful obligations, below). It adds an Allowance to the list once its grant's write is known to have applied, in a local write of its own rather than in the batch that carries the grant, and removes it for good when it sees the Allowance ended, rejected, handed off or conflicted, even before it has added it. No other app can make it pay: a grant can't be forged or moved to another counterparty, changing the terms voids it, and an end it has seen is final. **Manual only:** a payer app may mark an occurrence manual-only on its record, the request record of a one-time request or the period record of a Billing Period, and a recurring request as a whole on its request record (`manual_only`, `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3), as in v0.1 (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Durable Allowance payment accounting"). The mark is never lifted, and no executor pays the occurrence automatically while its record holds it: the executor's batch writes that record at the version it read (step 3, below), so a mark written since makes the batch fail. A Billing Period that has no period record yet, one not started among them, is marked by the write that creates its period record, holding the mark and no message, built on no version, so an executor's batch that creates that record meanwhile makes one of the two fail, and an app whose mark failed reads the record and marks it again. An attempt its user started by hand writes the mark too, as in v0.1, where a manual payment excludes automatic payment of the same occurrence: its batch (Section 7.2 step 1) also writes `manual_only` on the occurrence's record, the request record of a one-time request or, for a Billing Period, its period record, creating that record when the period has none, at the version it read, unless the record holds the mark already. So no attempt made by hand exists without its mark, and no executor, nor an app paying a period by its own policy (Section 8.2, Paying periods), pays that occurrence automatically again, also once the attempt has failed or the service has deleted its claim. The mark withholds automatic attempts and a resume's repeats of automatic attempts (Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties); the repeat of an attempt its user started, the one that wrote the mark included, finishes the user's own payment and is judged as any repeat of a payment the user confirmed (Section 7.2, What a payment method declares). The claim checks still hold an occurrence whose claim shows an attempt its record doesn't mark (Section 7.1, No automatic payment of doubtful claims; Doubtful obligations, below). While the request record of a recurring request holds the mark, no executor accepts that request and none pays one of its Billing Periods: the executor reads that record immediately before the acceptance and before each such dispatch, and a read that fails or doesn't read withholds both. Deferring an occurrence stays in the executor's own state (Section 16).

**Ends reach the executor.** Any app with `paykit_allowance` may end an Allowance (Section 17, question 11). An app that ends an Allowance, hands it off, or records an `allowance_end` from the counterparty also sends `executor_notice` to the executor through its own identity's inbox (Pubky Switch spec, Section 7), so the executor learns of it even if the record is rewritten afterwards. An app that has verified a counterparty's `allowance_end` MUST keep it durably in its own state before it does anything else with it: for a message it receives, in the commit that passes the entry, with no service operation in that step. That kept copy is the stop, not the Allowance record: from then on the app MUST make no new attempt under that Allowance, and a restart, or a record write that failed or is still pending, never lifts it. It still sends `executor_notice`, and records the end in the Allowance record by a later write of its own as soon as it can; until then the identity's other apps may not know of it, the same exposure as an end not yet delivered. It keeps the copy at once whenever it holds the Allowance record, also one whose value doesn't read or is sealed under a scope key it doesn't hold yet: the stop never waits for a scope key, and giving up the entry never lifts it (Section 8.1, Stored entries). It drops the copy once the permanent Allowance record holds the end or the matching withdrawal and rejection have settled the exact pending lifetime (Section 8.3.1). While it can't yet check an end against the record, it keeps every distinct end of the Allowance, none in place of another, each counting under those bounds but the earliest; once the record reads, it takes them in the order the inbox accepted them, as kept replies are ordered (Section 8.1, Replies before their record): the first that names what the record holds ends the Allowance, and the others are reflected or dropped. A copy for an Allowance whose record the app doesn't hold, or whose record it reads and finds without the acceptance the end names, is kept under the bounds of a stop for a request the app doesn't hold (Section 8.1, Messages that cross), counted with those stops, and one dropped for those bounds is dropped as one never processed: the app forgets its pair in the commit that drops it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11) and counts it in its sender's mark of dropped replies, which asks that sender at once (Section 9, Messages dropped for a bound), as for a dropped payee's cancellation (Section 8.1, Messages that cross), so the end comes back and is taken; such a copy may be dropped 30 days after the end's acceptance, judged on the service time it verified, but never while its copy of `allowance_proposals` or `allowances` may lack records or it keeps a stored message of that Allowance to write (Section 8.1, Stored entries); a stop it keeps for such a message counts outside these bounds and is never evicted. The strict ordinary-stop admission rule of Section 8.1, Waiting for admission, applies only where this paragraph bounds a not-yet-admitted ordinary copy. It never postpones the verified counterparty end this paragraph requires the app to keep immediately, and never removes a stop protected outside those bounds. A notice changes only the executor's consent list; ending the Allowance itself is the `allowance_end` exchanged with the counterparty. **After a restore** from backup that isn't clean (Section 7.2, What a restore loses), an executor asks the user to confirm each Allowance on its consent list again before paying under it, since its backup may predate an end. After a restore, an original delivery resumes only with current evidence that it remains pending (Section 7.2, What a restore loses). An own proposal can be sent from its current pending record. A prepared rejection or withdrawal needs the verified result of its original pending deletion. Section 8.3.1 governs those checks and the separate duty to withdraw a surviving proposal. An own end of an accepted Allowance can be recovered from its permanent record. A conflict or retained counterparty end continues to hold the affected sends. Own acceptance can resume only under the original-application and delivery checks of Section 8.3.1. An `executor_notice` queued in a batch the backup kept unanswered isn't queued again after a restore, since no record stores it. Nothing is paid for that: if the batch applied, the end it went with is in the Allowance record, under which an executor consents to nothing, and an executor batch built on the record's earlier version fails (`record_version`); if it didn't, neither the end nor the notice went, and the record shows the Allowance not ended, as Recorded first says a restored app finds. This relies on the record: if a compromised own app rewrote an ended Allowance as running before the restore, the user's confirmation is the only check left. Every executor batch carries `record_version(Allowance record) == r`, so once the record changes, an executor working from the older version fails and re-reads it. When cross-app execution is supported, handing off means the new executor writes its own grant, with its user's consent, replacing the old one; the previous executor stops at its next batch. Paykit coordinates which app pays automatically and what it reserved. Carrying out payments, honouring grants and enforcing the Allowance's limits are the integrating app's job, and the service doesn't check them.

**Blocked counterparties.** Sending an `allowance_proposal`, `allowance_acceptance` or `allowance_rejection` requires `paykit_contacts` (Section 3.1, Outbound authorization). Without it the app refuses with `MissingScope`, signing, recording and queueing nothing. An authorized app sends none of these messages to a counterparty its copy of the deny list holds, or to any while that copy isn't known after it tried to read the lists (Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties): it refuses the user's proposal or answer, signing, recording and sending nothing, and the Allowance stays as it was. Refusing them moves no money in either role: no Allowance exists until it is accepted, and nothing is paid under a proposal, which no app accepts automatically. The user's `allowance_end` is never held or refused for the deny list: it stops payment, so it is recorded and sent at once, as a payee's cancellation is (Section 8.1). This exception applies to its eligible queued retries as well, under the ordinary delivery requirements. An end generated automatically for a proposer's withdrawal crossing the app's acceptance follows the counterparty hold in Ending, below, even if it was queued before the block or before the deny copy became unknown; the per-message decision in Section 8.1 preserves this distinction. As the allower, a refused end would let an executor pay later, or from another own app whose read of the lists works; as the allowee, the allower's executor keeps paying until the end reaches it. An `executor_notice` goes only between the identity's own apps and is never held for a counterparty's block: one that goes with an end goes when the end is recorded, also while the end's message to the counterparty is held (Ending, below).

**Valid terms.** Terms that aren't valid by `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.15 are refused when proposed, dropped when the proposal arrives, make an Allowance record that holds them invalid (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2), and are checked again before each automatic payment. An executor never pays under them.

**Recorded first.** An app records its signed proposal before sending it and verifies that the service applied the write. The recipient records that proposal in its own pending collection. A rejection deletes the recipient's pending record at its exact current write. The rejecting app prepares and retains the original signed rejection before this delete. It releases that original only after verifying the complete applied result. An unknown result retains the duty. A rejection is never reconstructed from the permanent record. The treatment of new acceptance is in Section 8.3.1.

**Conflicted.** Section 8.3.1 governs conflicts in a pending proposal. The following rules govern the permanent record after acceptance. As in v0.1 (`specs/allowances.md`, "Proposal"), one `allowance_id` has one proposal. A second proposal under it, from either side, whose hash differs from that of the proposal the Allowance record holds, makes the Allowance conflicted, whichever came first: neither proposal binds, and nothing is paid under it. A second response does the same: an acceptance or rejection from the proposal's recipient whose hash differs from that of the response the record holds, such as a rejection after the stored acceptance or a distinct second acceptance. A re-sent copy, with the same hash, is the proposal or response itself. v0.1 makes a second response invalid history rather than conflicted (Section 5). A second, different end is processed but not stored (Section 6, One message per role): an end is final, as in v0.1, where the first valid end is kept and a later one changes nothing.

- **Who writes it:** an own app with `paykit_allowance`, executors included (Section 3.1), that verifies such a proposal or response, received or sent, MUST keep it durably in its own state before it does anything else with it, as it keeps a counterparty's `allowance_end` (Ends reach the executor, above); that kept copy is the stop. It writes the message into the record's `conflicted` member (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.4) by a later write of its own as soon as it can, but for a proposal over a record it holds as deleted, which it writes nowhere (Ending, below). Each identity's apps write only their own identity's record, so the counterparty records the conflict in its own.
- **Never cleared:** as in v0.1, no later event makes a conflicted Allowance usable: no message, grant or user confirmation lifts it, and a later write keeps `conflicted` (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.4, Seen roles). The record has no current grant, so no executor pays under it. A conflict the app keeps as a stop, not yet in the record, stays for good as well: a record written later that doesn't conflict with the message kept, such as one an own app brings back after a delete with the proposal that was taken as conflicted over it, lifts nothing.
- **Nothing is sent:** an app sends no acceptance, rejection or end for a conflicted Allowance and writes no grant for it, as in v0.1. The counterparty is told nothing: it sent or received each of the two conflicting messages, so it finds the conflict itself. Apps with `paykit_allowance` show the user the Allowance as conflicted.
- **Attempts already open:** a conflict cancels no payment already started, as at a handoff. An executor makes no new attempt under a conflicted Allowance, and every executor batch carries `record_version(Allowance record) == r`, so a batch built on a version before the write of `conflicted` fails. An attempt whose check before the dispatch (Section 7.2 step 1) finds the Allowance conflicted pays nothing and writes `failed`; one already dispatched finishes, sends its proof and is resolved as Section 7.3 says. An end or a rejection is different: a resume's repeat of a dispatch under the Allowance (Section 7.2, What a payment method declares), whose first call may not have reached the payment method, such as one the app crashed on during the call, is made only while a read of the Allowance record made immediately before the repeat doesn't show it ended or rejected and the executor keeps no stop for it (Ends reach the executor, above); a read that fails, or a record that doesn't read, holds it too. Held, it pays nothing more, its ledger entry stays reserved, and the attempt stays open as potentially paid until the payment method's history or a receipt settles it, since nothing tells whether the first call was made. A handoff or a conflict doesn't hold a repeat. Its ledger entry counts as reserved until its outcome comes, and the executor that held the last grant records that outcome in the ledger, whose writes are still checked against the grant history (Who wrote it, below).

**Ledger.** The ledger record holds an entry for each attempt made under the Allowance: the obligation of the request or, for a Billing Period, of that period (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3), the `attempt_id`, value, asset and time `at`. The value is the amount the payment transfers in the requested asset, never less than the requested amount (Section 7.2 step 1; Section 8.2, Rates and payment time); fees aren't counted. It also holds running totals of spent amounts and payment counts for entries already compacted.

- **Who wrote it:** the ledger's first write is the acceptance mechanism's (Section 8.3.1). Every ordinary later ledger write is accepted only from the executor whose grant the Allowance record held at the version that write was conditioned on. The Allowance record keeps every grant it has held, oldest first, each with the record version that the write adding it built on (the version its writer read, Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3), which the writer knows before the service gives the new one; the last one is the current grant. Each grant's signature covers that version, so a history with a wrong version doesn't verify. Writers only append to it, and an app that has seen an entry ignores a write that changes or drops it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2); for an app that hasn't, a dropped entry only makes later ledger writes fail the check, which stops automatic payment rather than trusting a ledger. To check a ledger version, an app takes the last grant whose write built on a version older than the one the ledger write was conditioned on: it must verify for the identity, the counterparty and the Allowance, name the current terms hash, and belong to the writer. This works for versions the app never saw. A ledger write without that condition, or checked against a history that doesn't verify, isn't accepted either. A ledger written by any other app stops automatic payment under that Allowance until the user confirms again: the executor fails closed instead of trusting lower usage, and after the confirmation writes the ledger again (After a confirmation, below).
- **Building on a version accepts it:** a ledger write built on a ledger version accepts that version, so the executor builds on one only once it passes every check: the kept version (Last ledger version, below), no write of the Allowance record or the ledger by a removed app after its `distrust_from` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.2 step 3), Who wrote it, and the ledger rules (From one version to the next, below). This holds for every ledger write it makes: the executor batch, an outcome, an outcome copied from a claim, a compaction and a release. If the ledger fails them, the executor writes no ledger version on it, and the Allowance needs its user's confirmation again; an outcome batch then records the attempt's outcome on its claim alone.
- **A grant implies a ledger:** the Allower's acceptance batch creates its initial ledger (Section 8.3.1). Before a grant can authorize payment, its executor establishes a current complete anchor: the original unchanged ledger under a proved first grant (wire 5.9), a valid handoff, a successful complete-authority repair, or a resumption by the same executor as specified below. An absent, deleted, unreadable or recovered ledger never means zero. Reacceptance, another grant and user confirmation do not initialize it again. The service refuses ledger deletion. A missing permanent ledger that contradicts its known current history is a rollback (Last ledger version). Other missing or uncertain evidence leaves usage unknown.
- **From one version to the next:** the executor checks each newer ledger version against the last version it accepted (Last ledger version, below) before it builds on it, and builds on none that counts less: it pays nothing automatically under the Allowance while the ledger shown counts less (Building on a version accepts it). It checks the newer version against the terms of the `allowances` record it holds for that Allowance, whose longest period limit decides which entries may have been compacted, by the longest period at each time from the service time of the version it accepted to the newer write's, or, without one, its latest verified service time: an entry may have left if at some such time it was older than the longest period then plus 60 s, since a write between that it didn't see may have compacted it then (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.5). An app that only reads the ledger takes each newer version as the reference client hands it; it checks and flags one that counts less (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2) once the client hands the protocol's check the write's service time, which it doesn't yet (Section 17, question 37). An entry keeps its obligation, `attempt_id`, value, asset and time. Its outcome only goes from none to `done` or `failed`, or from `failed` to `done`. It leaves only by compaction, once old enough: a `done` one into the totals, and one the reader held without an outcome or `failed` into the compacted failed list, or into the totals, since it may have become `done` before it was compacted. A compacted failed attempt leaves that list only into the totals, and the totals never grow by less than what left them. New entries may come in any time.
- **Time of an entry:** each executor batch carries `server_time_in(at, at + 60 s)`, with `at` taken from the server time of the service's latest response and the interval cut short at the next boundary of any anchored limit. The entry therefore lies in exactly one period of every anchored limit. For a rolling limit of length L it counts from `at` until `at + 60 s + L`. The service can't write inside the sealed ledger, so the executor states the time and the condition makes the service vouch for it: whatever an executor's own clock says, its entries are dated within 60 s of when the service applied them, so every app and every later executor counts each payment in the same period.
- **Usage:** each app derives usage over the entries as `specs/allowances.md` defines it, for every period limit, the per-payment limit and the lifetime limit, with server time in place of the wallet's admission time and watermark. Each entry counts by the outcome of the attempt it names:

| Attempt outcome (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2) | Counts as |
|---|---|
| `open` | Reserved |
| `unknown` | Reserved, until reported paid or failed |
| `done` | Spent |
| `failed`, whoever wrote it | Spent, unless the executor's durable journal proves the attempt never dispatched (`failed` isn't final, below) |

- **`failed` isn't final:** a payment reported failed after the payment method's call may still complete (Section 7.2, What a payment method declares). A `failed` attempt counts as spent immediately, while its claim remains and after it is gone, whoever wrote the failure: its owner, another app, or the executor at the user's request. This applies to an entry's own `failed`, an entry without an outcome whose claim shows `failed`, and a compacted failed attempt. It releases only when the executor's durable journal proves that the attempt was ended before the payment method's call and can never be dispatched; an entry at the call, an ambiguous result, a user's confirmation alone and a journal restored from a backup prove none of that. A release requested by the user without that evidence is refused. A claim or ledger entry that shows `done`, or a payer record holding a proof of this attempt naming this Allowance, always spends, even beside such journal evidence. No readable payer record, a record without a proof, and a proof of another attempt or naming no Allowance or another one provide no evidence for a release. The current executor writes `done` into an entry, or moves a compacted failed attempt into the totals, when the claim shows `done` or its copy of the request's or Billing Period's payer record holds that proof, whichever executor made the attempt and whether or not the claim is still kept. The service keeps a failed attempt's claim until 30 days after its deadline (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3), and the ledger keeps the entry or its compacted failed item visible to claim-based counting at least as long. A later `done` from the payee's receipt (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2 row 3) therefore confirms spending already counted; it never consumes capacity another obligation was allowed to use. After that window a receipt can't be recorded on the claim: an executor that gets it marks its entry `done`, or moves the compacted failed item into the totals, all the same. Every app that gets it and holds the failure shows the user that a payment marked failed was paid (Section 7.2). A compacted failed attempt counts by the same rule in the lifetime amount until the executor moves a later `done` into the totals, once.
- **One entry per attempt:** a retry after a failed attempt is a new attempt with its own entry, so every attempt is counted once.
- **Outcomes in the ledger:** the current executor writes each attempt's outcome into its entry as soon as it learns it, from the feed or, once the claim's entries are stubs, by reading the claim again (Switch wire 7.4.8 and 7.5), including outcomes of a previous executor's attempts, in the same batch when it records the outcome itself, so usage doesn't depend on claims that may since have been deleted (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3). It writes `done` from a claim, `failed` only when the attempt's owner wrote it or the user released the amount with the never-dispatched evidence above, and a later `done` over a `failed`, for which it reads the claims of `failed` entries again. An entry without an outcome whose claim is gone counts as spent.
- **Compaction:** when the record nears its size limit, the executor drops entries whose outcome is final and which are older than the longest period limit plus 60 s, by the latest service time it has verified. When a ledger write that adds an entry would take the record over the 256 KiB limit for a shared record value even after compaction, the executor makes no automatic payment under the Allowance and tells its user. It counts the record with an outcome in every entry that has none yet, so the outcome of every attempt it holds can still be written. It adds the paid amounts and counts of `done` ones to the running totals, and keeps each `failed` one's `attempt_id`, obligation and value in the totals, so that a later `done` still counts, once. A ledger with an entry in another asset than the Allowance's, an attempt listed twice, or a value or time that doesn't parse is invalid: usage and compaction refuse it rather than count it as less.
- **Local usage:** computed usage is only a cache, rebuilt from a complete authoritative ledger and, for entries without an outcome, the request claims. The complete ledger value, its authority and pending write described below are durable safety state, never a disposable cache. Every automatic payment requires that authority; repair, confirmation and a later own rewrite cannot bypass an incomplete-history hold.
- **Last ledger version:** the executor keeps the last ledger write it wrote or read with an authenticated version, from the transition proof it got with the write or the verified stub of the feed item (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3), and the write's item hash. A version number alone proves nothing, since the service gives it. Before an automatic payment it reads the ledger record: the write shown must be that one, by item hash, or one signed as built on that version or a later one (its `expected_version`). A verified write built on an earlier version, an authenticated deletion of this permanent ledger, or confirmed absence contradicting its recorded live version in the same uninterrupted history raises the rollback alarm (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1). An unavailable answer or unresolved provider-loss history holds spending. A write whose signature verifies with its writer's certificate, that is the kept write or is signed as built on it or later, but that fails a check by policy, such as its writer's standing, Who wrote it or the ledger rules, only pauses the Allowance: the executor refuses it as untrusted (Building on a version accepts it). A write that fails the checks of Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.3 never reaches the executor. Signed evidence of misbehaviour, such as an item that doesn't match its chain stub or a proof that contradicts the verified chain, is a rollback the reference client raises itself (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1). A write whose failure rests only on the service's word, such as a signature that doesn't verify with its writer's certificate, a missing date, or a version missing or not plausible, is ignored by the client, naming no app, and raises no alarm: the executor holds the write it held before and builds on nothing it wasn't shown, so its next ledger write puts back what it trusts or fails at the service, and nothing is paid on the write ignored. The original ledger can establish the initial checkpoint under the first-grant rule (wire 5.9). Thereafter an executor builds on a ledger authorized by a handoff or by repair as described below, or on the current ledger verified under Resuming the same executor. A version shown by the service alone confers no authority. After any earlier grant, a new executor may establish authority through a `ledger_handoff` that counts for its current grant or successful repair from complete usage authority under that grant (Successful repair, below). Until one of those succeeds it builds on no ledger except for that repair and pays nothing automatically. Merely holding a version changes nothing: this includes an observer copy, one kept when writing its grant and one from an earlier term, as when the grant history goes N, P, N. The earlier complete checkpoint may support repair only under the exact-current-value and uninterrupted-history conditions below. Once one counts, the named write is its kept version (Handoff, below, says how it meets a version it holds), so the write shown must be that one or one signed as built on it or a later one and pass every other check of Building on a version accepts it; from then on it keeps the write it built on. After restore the same executor may resume under the rule below. If it cannot establish the current complete ledger, automatic payment waits for the missing evidence, a valid handoff or a complete-authority repair. The repair requires Complete usage authority; neither a handoff hash alone nor an older backup proves that no later write was applied. So a service that shows it an older version than the last one written can't make it build on that one: against the named write an older one is a rollback, and the repair counts every attempt the counterparty's claims and the identity's own records show. A split view that also spans the previous executor, or hides from the repair the claims it reads, isn't covered (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1, Not covered: a split view).
- **Resuming the same executor.** After an older backup, the same executor in the same uninterrupted grant term and account history may resume from a complete ledger it previously accepted. It reads the current permanent Allowance, ledger and relevant claims from the service. It verifies the original proposal and acceptance, the current grant and the conditioned writer, and binds each ledger value to its signed write, authenticated version, item hash and service time. It accounts for the whole continuation from the backup through the current write, including reservations, releases, outcomes, attempt counts and compacted usage. For example, a current reservation can supply a complete step when its signed `expected_version` names the backup's exact ledger, the service applied its matching claim opening in the same batch, and the ledger value passes the successor checks. A version greater than the backup's, a feed stub or a total alone cannot identify the missing changes.
- The wallet must independently account for every possible dispatch under this Allowance from the checkpoint through resumption, including other installations of the executor, outstanding handoffs to payment methods and changed outcomes for earlier attempts. It reconciles each dispatch with the ledger and claims using its obligation, attempt, Allowance, asset and transfer amount. A payment that the authenticated service history demonstrably omits raises the rollback alarm. A lawful compaction still counts the payment. Unavailable or incomplete history, ambiguous attribution and an untrusted ledger hold automatic payment. The executor counts open reservations in full, durably accepts the verified current ledger and applies every usual consent, occurrence and admission check. A concurrent change of the checked grant or ledger requires a fresh decision. This resumption writes no repair. The separate confirmation and exact-current-value requirements below apply to a repair.
- **After a confirmation:** an Allowance whose ledger the executor didn't accept needs a repair with complete usage authority, which the user confirms with its figures shown. The executor first shows the user what the repair would write: the usage per period limit, amount and count, the lifetime amount, the entries reserved, spent and released, and the amounts counted for a `failed` another app wrote. The user confirms those figures, and the confirmation writes exactly that ledger; if anything it was built from changed in between, the executor refuses and shows the new figures. The repair writes the ledger again, as a merge that never counts less: the last ledger version it accepted, which it keeps with its content, as it was; what the ledger shown adds to it (entries it lacks, a `done`, higher totals); every attempt its journal holds under the Allowance; and every attempt of an app named in the grant history that the claims of the counterparty's obligations show, unless the payment's verified proof names no Allowance or another one. Complete usage authority below is required before any figures can be confirmed. Without it repair refuses, even when every input is empty. A shown unaccepted ledger and surviving attempts may only add to already complete usage; neither establishes that no attempt is missing. The version kept stays as it was: its entries and `totals.failed` items keep their values and times, every reader holding it checks the next version against it, and only a `done` another input shows is taken; own evidence showing one of its attempts at a larger value, or dated at or after the end of that entry's executor window (`at` plus 60 seconds, cut at the next boundary of any anchored limit), refuses the repair. Evidence within that window is the batch the entry already counts. Thus the rewrite counts no less than the identity's own evidence, per attempt, at the service time the attempt was made. For an attempt the version kept doesn't list, the entry takes what counts it most among the inputs: the largest value, the latest time, a time after the repair's counting as the repair's, the obligation of the journal or the claim, and a `done` any input shows. A `failed` the ledger shown writes is never taken, and its `totals.failed` item stays one only for an attempt neither the journal nor a claim shows; otherwise the attempt is an entry, counted by its claim in every limit. So a copy written by a removed app, or a write the app doesn't accept, never counts an attempt the payer's own evidence shows at less than that evidence does. A journal attempt takes the transferred-amount evidence described below and its claim's `opened_at`, or, with no claim showing it, the time it was journaled. The ledger shown adds nothing when its value doesn't read as the Allowance's ledger, or when the reference client refused its write, which it hands without content (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2): the repair then adds the copy the app trusts and builds on the refused write's version; while the value isn't open yet, its scope key not held, the repair waits. The counterparty's obligations are those of its one-time requests in the Allowance's asset, and of its recurring ones every Billing Period listed in `attempted_periods`, every one whose period record the app holds, and every one from the first to the one containing the latest service time verified whose claim the service may still hold: with a payment deadline, those whose deadline plus 30 days hasn't passed; without one, every period, as such a claim stays 400 days after its last entry. A done period leaves `attempted_periods`, and its claim may still show it. A request with more such periods than the repair reads refuses the repair. Every payment a payer or period record's proof shows made under the Allowance counts as `done`, also once its claim is gone, but its amount must be established by the transferred-amount rule below; a request proof carries no amount. An attempt is left out as made otherwise only on the payer identity's own evidence: the executor's own journal entry of it naming no Allowance or another one, or a proof of it in the payer's record naming no Allowance or another one, signed by the attempt's owner while that app isn't removed. A record holds only proofs its payer sent (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3), so the counterparty can't supply one, and a proof another own app signed shows nothing of how the owner made the attempt. A payer record or a claim that can't be read refuses the repair, as does a period record whose request record isn't held, whose amount can't be told, and a ledger divergent or seen so and not confirmed (Section 6). The version kept is checked first, whatever else the write shown is: a write that is neither that version nor built on it, a refused one whose version the client follows being before it included, is a rollback, whose alarm holds every attempt, and isn't repaired; while that alarm stands no Allowance is repaired. The repair writes the outcomes the claims show into the merge, and compacts it near the size limit as an executor batch's ledger is, keeping room for an outcome of every attempt it holds; one that doesn't fit then refuses. The merge may count an attempt twice, never less: an entry the ledger shown lists that the version kept compacted into its totals is added again, since nothing in the inputs tells it apart from one the version kept never held, and a hand attempt by an app the grant history names, with no proof or journal entry of it yet, counts under the Allowance. What it was built from is the Allowance record version, the version kept, the ledger write shown and the version the repair builds on, the journal's attempts, the payer records read and the step of every claim read; the repair's write carries `record_version(Allowance record)`, conditions every payer record read at its version, every period record absent from an obligation it walked at its absence, and every claim read at its step; it builds on that exact current authoritative ledger version and carries `server_time_in(at, end)` with an executor batch's `end`, `at + 60 s` cut at the next boundary of any anchored limit, so the figures the user confirmed are the ones in force when it lands. Only the current executor repairs, and only an Allowance it otherwise pays under: the confirmation is the ledger's alone, so after a restore that isn't clean, or over an Allowance record write the executor ignores, its user confirms the Allowance again first (Ends reach the executor; Executor).
- **Successful repair:** confirmation alone leaves the handoff hold in place. Once the repair's applied result is verified and durably incorporated, the executor keeps its full complete value, authenticated result version and item hash, bound to the exact current grant. That result is its accepted anchor and satisfies the automatic-payment gate as an alternative to a valid handoff; every other ledger, payment and pause check still applies. A pending, refused or applied-but-not-durably-incorporated repair supplies no exit. The repair also retains the exact authenticated base version and item hash on which its first repair since the handoff was conditioned, separately from the result anchor, for the late-handoff checks below. A later `ledger_handoff` is compared to that base, never substituted for the result or allowed to roll usage back. A grant change requires authority for the new term; it does not reuse an earlier term's repair permission without the handoff or complete-checkpoint repair checks. Thus A may hand a complete ledger to B, B may make no ledger write and go offline, and A may resume after the user hands back and confirms a repair of its still-exact-current complete checkpoint: the verified applied repair establishes A's current anchor without waiting for B to return.
- **Complete usage authority:** repair requires the complete usage of the Allowance, including attempts no surviving record enumerates. The executor's durable state keeps the full accepted ledger value, including cumulative `totals.spent`, `totals.count` and every `totals.failed` item, its authenticated version and item hash, the executor term that makes it authoritative, and any prepared ledger write whose result has not been accounted for. An attempt leaves the entries only by the prescribed compaction into those retained totals or failed items. Expiry of claims and activity records, and pruning a journal, never remove that usage. An older accepted ledger is insufficient without its complete accounted-for continuation.
- **Keeping that authority:** each executor instance serializes its ledger writes per Allowance. After the initial ledger (Section 8.3.1), every ordinary advance of this authority must build on the exact current complete value. A write over an unaccounted gap, even this executor's own signed rewrite, cannot manufacture complete authority. Before submitting one, it durably keeps the exact prepared batch and proposed ledger value with the preceding complete ledger. It submits no further ledger write until it has durably incorporated a verified applied result, or a verified definitive refusal proves the batch did not apply. A lost response, an unknown result or a failure without that proof keeps the prepared write pending across restart and retry. This includes executor batches, outcomes (also `failed` and `unknown`), copied outcomes, compactions, releases, repairs and put-backs. A terminal payment or expired claim does not resolve a ledger write. A crash or failed local commit before recording the result keeps that write pending; repair and handoff wait. The retained state needs one complete ledger and at most one prepared write, not lifetime history. If it cannot be stored, no further ledger write or automatic payment is submitted.
- **Concurrent instances and missing continuation:** serialization within an instance is not proof that another instance of the same executor submitted nothing. Before repair, the current ledger must be the exact authenticated complete value this instance holds, or its exact prepared write whose verified applied result it has now incorporated. Repair carries that current version as its `expected_version`, so any concurrent ledger write makes the repair fail and compute again. A higher version, the same signing app or grant, and a signed `expected_version` merely at or after the kept version do not account for an unseen continuation. The anchor and the current write must also belong to the same uninterrupted account history: after provider loss, an older matching copy cannot show that another instance had no later applied write in the lost history. A recovery upload or generation change never proves that continuation. An unaccounted remote write, a missing or deleted current value, or a put-back of an older value refuses repair; confirmation cannot bless it as complete. No current API is assumed to enumerate that missing continuation. A complete value obtained by the valid handoff below may establish the required current anchor, but an older handoff cannot account for subsequent writes by another instance. A complete checkpoint retained from an earlier executor term or in a backup can support a user-confirmed repair only if its prior complete authority is retained, its full value and authenticated anchor still exactly match the current ledger in the same uninterrupted account history, and every known prepared write is resolved. A ledger merely read as an observer has no such prior authority. The current-version CAS fences still-unapplied writes from another instance; it cannot undo a write already lost from history.
- **When complete usage is lost:** repair without this authority writes nothing and leaves the Allowance unavailable for automatic payments, including new obligations and later Billing Periods with no doubtful attempt of their own. Bounded claim and activity retention may leave an empty inventory even though compacted lifetime usage was nonzero. Surviving attempts and their individually known amounts, an unaccepted shown ledger, elapsed period windows, user confirmation, regrant, provider generation changes and older-copy put-back never replace missing inventory or totals. A retained complete value and accounted-for continuation, meeting the current-write or handoff rules above, can restore authority; if every such copy is lost, automatic use of this Allowance is unavailable indefinitely. The host reports incomplete usage history, not zero spending or renewed capacity. Payment by hand remains a separate authorization subject to every occurrence hold; it does not repair or reset this Allowance. A distinct newly authorized Allowance grants new authority under its own identity and limits and existing association rules, never reclassifies old spending. These durable-state and refusal rules are required in the repair implementation; no current repair API or vector supplies a complete-inventory proof.
- **Amounts recovered by a repair:** every included attempt needs durable evidence of the amount transferred, or the exact transfer amount bound before its possible dispatch, in the Allowance's asset, including endpoint rounding and excluding fees (Sections 7.2 and 8.2). A retained accepted ledger entry or `totals.failed` item supplies its accounted value. The executor's own durable journal supplies an amount only when it records that transfer bound for the same attempt, obligation, Allowance and asset; a journal containing only the nominal request or Billing Period amount does not. A claim's outcome and `opened_at`, a payer or period record's proof without an amount, the current endpoint's smallest unit or rates, and the user's confirmation cannot reconstruct an earlier transfer amount. The request's nominal amount is never substituted, even when the attempt is still on its claim, was reported failed, or its proof is the only remaining payment evidence. An unaccepted shown ledger may increase the repair's figures but cannot by itself establish the missing amount of an attempt found through a claim, proof or journal. The repair preserves every accepted larger value and applies the conservative merge above only after each such amount is established. Otherwise it refuses without writing a smaller ledger: the Allowance remains paused for automatic payments, possibly indefinitely if the evidence cannot be recovered. This rule applies equally to one-time and recurring obligations, claim-only, proof-only and journal reconstruction, and after restore or provider loss; it changes neither the default-spent failure rule nor its requirement for durable never-dispatched evidence.

**Paying under an Allowance:**

1. The executor reads the Allowance record, the ledger record and the request's claim.
2. It recomputes usage and checks the terms and limits at the current server time, and the transfer (Section 7.2 step 1): a destination that transfers another amount, or one the method can't tell, or, where the app configures a fee cap for the asset, one whose largest fee is above it, isn't paid automatically, nor is a payment priced by rates, the request's fixed rates or period details' (Section 8.2); with no cap, the fee of the executor's payment is bounded only by the payment method's own limit, if it has one, and fees aren't counted against the Allowance (Ledger, above). It pays nothing automatically while its device clock and the service's differ by more than the clock tolerance (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1, Clock bound). Terms are checked whole against the table of `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.15; a time that doesn't parse fails closed, never as no limit.
3. It sends one batch:
   - the `attempt` on the request's claim;
   - the ledger record with the new entry, at its next version;
   - the activity record showing the payment as being handled;
   - when the activity record is the period record of a Billing Period not listed in `attempted_periods`, a write of the request record that adds it (Section 8.2); a recurring request's record names its Allowance from its acceptance on, so this write never names one;

   with the conditions `record_version(Allowance record) == r`, `server_time_in(at, at + 60 s)` and, on the attempt, the request's `deadline` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2), the version of the request record it read (Section 7.2) and, for a Billing Period, the period's start as its not-before (Section 8.2).
4. It follows Section 7.2. Proofs carry `allowance_id`.

- **Conflicts:** if any check fails, nothing is written and the executor recomputes. The ledger record's version serializes every executor batch for the Allowance; a change to the terms, the response, the end or the grant changes the Allowance record's version; a late batch fails `server_time_in`. If another wallet is already attempting the request, the attempt fails and nothing is added to the ledger.
- **Doubtful obligations:** an executor never attempts an obligation with an `unknown` or orphaned attempt, or whose next attempt would be flagged `possibly_duplicate` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.2). Nor does it attempt one whose claim holds an attempt that no entry or `totals.failed` item of the ledger of the Allowance the occurrence is paid under names, one made by hand or otherwise outside that Allowance, whatever its outcome, as in v0.1, where "Manual payments consume no Allowance capacity but exclude automatic payment of the same occurrence" (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Durable Allowance payment accounting"): once an occurrence has been attempted outside its Allowance, it is paid again only by hand.
- **One Allowance per request:** an executor's acceptance of a subscription under an Allowance (Section 8.2), or the first automatic payment of a one-time request, names its Allowance in the request record, in that batch, and every later automatic payment of the request or of its Billing Periods is made only under that one, as in v0.1, or under the one the user moves a later period to (below); a recurring request whose record names none has no period paid under an Allowance (Section 8.2, Paying periods). When more than one accepted Allowance covers a request whose record names none, which one an executor accepts or pays it under is its own choice, as in v0.1, where the wallet selects one of the candidates (`specs/allowances.md`, "Payment Request integration"): it pays under that one alone, and its batch names it in the request record, so later occurrences, but for a move the user makes (below), and every own app follow that choice. An Allowance that has ended, or expired at its terms' `expires_at`, pays nothing more of the request; the executor never picks another one for it, and no app pays it automatically without one: each occurrence is left to payment by hand, after its user's confirmation, as in v0.1. A period or lifetime limit without room for a payment blocks only the Billing Period it affects, and later periods are checked again against the capacity then, as in v0.1 (`specs/allowances.md`, "One-time and recurring requests"). A handoff changes the Allowance's executor, not the Allowance, so the new executor pays the request's later occurrences under it. Only the user moves a recurring request's later Billing Periods, of a subscription accepted under an Allowance and not cancelled, to another accepted Allowance of the request's counterparty, neither ended nor conflicted, whose terms match the request as at acceptance (Section 8.2, Accepting), as in v0.1 (`specs/allowances.md`, "Authorized recurring reassociation"); its `active_from` may be later, since the move authorizes no payment by itself and the terms' window and capacity are checked when each period is paid, as in v0.1, where "the accepted replacement need not be active yet" (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Durable Allowance payment accounting"): an app adds an entry to the request record's `reassociations` after its user confirms it, naming the Allowance and the first Billing Period it covers, one that hasn't started and is later than every period in `attempted_periods`. A period's Allowance is the one of the last entry starting at or before it, else the named one. Usage already counted stays in the earlier Allowance's ledger. An app that has seen the name or an entry ignores and flags a write that changes or drops it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2; `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3).
- **Limits:** the executor enforces the Allowance limits. Paykit coordinates the reservation (one ledger version, one terms version, server time) and never enforces spending limits at the protocol level, so a faulty or malicious executor can still exceed them.

**Handoff (later release):** outstanding attempts keep counting as reserved until their outcomes arrive. The previous executor may still record the outcomes of its own attempts on their claims, without touching the ledger, which only the current executor writes; the new executor copies those outcomes into the ledger once it builds on a ledger version (below). Any other executor batch of the previous executor fails the Allowance record condition. A handoff doesn't cancel payments already started. When it learns of the handoff, from the `executor_notice` or the Allowance record, the previous executor names its last ledger version for the new one, which it keeps until then: only while it retains complete usage authority, the current ledger exactly matches its complete value and every ledger write it sent has a verified answer incorporated into that state, it writes `ledger_handoff` into the Allowance record, at the version it read, with the version and item hash of its last ledger version (Last ledger version, above) and the grant that handed the Allowance on, signed with its own certificate so that no later writer of the record can change it (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.4). It writes none when it holds no ledger version, or only one a restore from a backup installed, which may predate its own later writes, or when its last ledger version isn't one it wrote or took as its kept version by a `ledger_handoff` since its own grant, such as one it held only as an app that reads the ledger or from an earlier term of its own, so a second handoff before the first one's `ledger_handoff` leaves the next executor the repair, or once the record holds a grant of another app after the one that handed the Allowance on from it. A grant the same app signs again, at a confirmation or after its own restore, is no handoff: that app is still the executor, under the rules of a restore (Ends reach the executor and Last ledger version, both above). A `ledger_handoff` that doesn't verify, or isn't signed by the app the grant history names just before the grant it names, is ignored and flagged (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2); one for a grant other than the one that handed the Allowance to the current executor, or whose app has since been removed, counts for nothing. Until a `ledger_handoff` counts for the grant that handed the Allowance to it, or a complete-authority repair under the current grant succeeds and durably establishes its result anchor, the new executor builds on no ledger except for that repair and pays nothing automatically. An earlier-term version alone grants no permission when the history goes N, P, N (Last ledger version, above): it may omit the previous executor's later payments. Repair is available only with Complete usage authority; it cannot reconstruct missing inventory from surviving records. If the previous executor is offline, removed or lost and no complete authority can be obtained, automatic payment remains unavailable, even with the user's confirmation. Once one counts, it builds on the write it names. Until it has read the named write itself, it holds only its version and item hash: a repair then waits: the version and hash do not supply the complete ledger value or its compacted totals. A version it holds when that `ledger_handoff` reaches it is checked against the named write, by version: before it has written a ledger version since the handoff, it takes the named write as its kept version unless the one it holds is later, and flags a named write before the one it holds, or another write at the same version (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2); once it has, including through repair before the `ledger_handoff`, the named write must be the write its first ledger write since the handoff built on, and one later than that, another write at that version, or any named write where it built on the ledger's absence is a rollback (Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1), since it then built on a version older than the previous executor's last one, while one before it is flagged. When an executor stops being current, apps holding `paykit_allowance` prompt the user to choose another.

**Ending:** an app with `paykit_allowance` that ends an accepted Allowance stores its `allowance_end` in the Allowance record's `end`, at its expected version, and sends it only after that write succeeds, as a payer records its cancellation first (Section 8.1). A proposer's withdrawal, an end naming the proposal alone, can cross the acceptance: the app whose identity accepted then holds a record that can't take it (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.16). That app ends the Allowance itself, unless it is conflicted (Conflicted, above), with an end naming the proposal and the acceptance, stored and sent as above, and the withdrawal is reflected by its `msg_id`, so the Allowance has ended on both sides, as in v0.1, where a withdrawal crossing an acceptance ends it (`specs/allowances.md`, "End"). While sends to the counterparty are held, including for missing contacts authorization (Section 3.1, Outbound authorization; Blocked counterparties, above), the app still makes that end and records it in the Allowance record at once, and queues its `executor_notice` to its own apps, so that every own app, an executor in another app included, finds the Allowance ended and a batch built on the record's earlier version fails; it holds only the end's message to the counterparty, which it sends from the record once sends to the counterparty aren't held, as a message queued again from its record and so only until 30 days after its `created_at`, less the clock tolerance (Section 7.2, What a restore loses); the end in the record stands either way. The counterparty hold applies to each attempted delivery of that generated end, including a queued retry, a kept batch's later delivery and a copy carried by reload or restore. On restore, its recorded effect and own-app notice prove neither that the delivery is still pending nor that it is a user-initiated end (Section 7.2). At the proposer, a pending withdrawal deletes the pending record. When a late acceptance names the proposer's retained signed proposal, it reads the pending key and then the permanent key under Section 8.3.1. A matching permanent proposal and acceptance make that acceptance a repeat. When the reads establish the prior withdrawal, the proposer still owes the withdrawal naming the proposal. An indeterminate read holds the duty. A crossing rejection and withdrawal settle the same pending lifetime without creating a permanent record. Only an app already holding the matched permanent proposal and acceptance can store an end naming both. A proposal, acceptance or rejection whose Allowance record the app holds but can't read is never written over that record: while the app lacks the scope key the record's value is sealed under, its entry waits for that key in the client's store, as Stored entries (Section 8.1) says, and a message the app keeps for a later write waits for it within the 30 days Stored entries gives, since the app never writes over a value it can't open; a value that doesn't read for another reason isn't written over either: the message is kept and tried again as Stored entries (Section 8.1) says, and may be given up after 30 days. An end is kept as the stop all the same (Ends reach the executor, above). The service refuses a delete of a permanent Allowance record. Its signed history remains available for checking stops and conflicts. Before an acceptance, a withdrawal follows the pending deletion rules of Section 8.3.1.

**After provider loss** (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6): the Allowance record holds `confirmed_generation`, the generation in which its user last confirmed it. While that is lower than `recovery_from + 1` in the current `identity.json`, the executor makes no automatic payment under it. After the user confirms again, the executor writes a new grant with the current generation; no other app can end this generation pause. That confirmation establishes no complete usage authority: missing inventory or compacted totals still refuse repair and every new automatic payment under the Allowance. Earlier doubtful obligations stay blocked from automatic payment, and delayed outcomes of earlier attempts still count at their entries' times.

**Reading a newer ledger.** Checking a newer ledger version requires the authenticated service times of both writes (wire 5.5). While the reading app has no such evidence, only the executor checks a newer version, before building on it (Section 17, question 37).

**Integrity:** every ledger write is a writer-signed operation, sealed and bound to its record key, so the service can't forge, alter or move amounts between Allowances. Rollback and incomplete batches are detected as in Pubky Switch spec, Section 10.1.

#### 8.3.1 Pending proposals and acceptance

**Pending proposals.** The ordinary `allowance_proposals` record binds the parties, the Allowance ID and the original signed proposal P. A distinct second proposal with the same parties and ID conflicts. Every later live write in that lifetime preserves the conflict. Only a verified current deletion ends it. An unreadable live value or an indeterminate read preserves it. Proposals have no expiry. The terms' `expires_at` retains its meaning.

**Admission of a new pending proposal.** Before each create, the app counts the current live pending records on its account for that counterparty, counting own and received proposals separately. If it cannot establish the current count for that direction, it waits. If it sees eight or more, it refuses a new own proposal before signing or writing, or processes a new received proposal and tells the host without writing a record or sending a reply. An existing pending ID follows the conflict rule above.

Eight is the count each app uses for admission. Creates for different proposal IDs have separate write conditions. For example, if several apps each see seven pending proposals in one direction for the same counterparty and each creates one distinct record before seeing the others, the account can retain seven plus the number of successful creates in that direction. This rule sets no fixed aggregate maximum per counterparty. All those records stay pending until an acceptance, rejection, withdrawal or permitted discard removes them. Further creates in that direction require observing fewer than eight.

**Reading and writing.** An app reads the pending key first, then the permanent `allowances` key for the same ID and counterparty. Create a pending record only when the permanent key is absent. Enforce that absence in the write batch at version `{0, 0}`. Creating at zero matches only current absence. Creating over a retained pending delete names its current version. A write over a recovery upload also names its exact application through the Switch's `record_current` condition. An unreadable, unavailable or indeterminate result holds the write. An existing permanent record keeps its history and cannot be replaced by a pending proposal.

**Rejection, withdrawal and discard.** Only its recipient rejects P and only its proposer withdraws it. Each names P by its hash, deletes the exact pending version on its own account and sends the original message after the delete applies. An app receiving a matching rejection or withdrawal deletes its own pending record, except when the two-reply rule below requires it to keep the later rejection as a stop. A conflicted pending proposal is never answered or withdrawn automatically. Its user may discard it, or a received proposal from a blocked sender, by deleting its exact current write and sending nothing. A fresh copy of P can create a new pending lifetime after its deletion if the ordinary processed-pair rules admit the incoming message. An absent pending record gives no proposal to accept. A restored app that lost a prepared rejection leaves the proposal pending at the proposer until it is withdrawn or a retained original arrives.

**Two replies to a pending proposal.** Section 8.3, Ends reach the executor, governs expiry of an unmatched stop after 30 days, incomplete listings, and stops protected by a stored message. A matched end follows the retention rule for that lifetime. For the counterparty's signed acceptance Q and distinct signed rejection R of its own P, the app uses their inbox acceptance times and, within one millisecond, their original feed positions. This includes replies kept before the app read P and replies held as stored entries. A repeat retains its original position. Both replies must meet the role and reference checks for P in wire Section 4.16. If Q came first and remains unplaced while P is pending, the app keeps the first subsequent R as a local stop for this pending lifetime and shows P as conflicted. It suppresses further delivery of P, rejection and withdrawal. Q follows its ordinary placement and 30-day give-up rule. Giving up Q or acknowledging its notice leaves the stop in force. An exact discard or an observed verified deletion by another app of this identity retires the stop and the old replies' placement duties together. A later lawful recreation of P starts a new lifetime. A missing read or the reappearance of P alone cannot establish that boundary.

If R came first and P is eligible for rejection, R deletes the exact matching pending version. While that deletion is unresolved, the later Q waits. Once the deletion applies, that lifetime is closed. Q alone cannot reopen it or establish a permanent Allowance. An app that retains its own signed P and receives this late Q follows Restored and late withdrawals below. If the permanent record already holds P and Q, the copy of Q is a repeat and R conflicts with its accepted response under Section 8.3, in either inbox order.

**Delivery.** Before every attempt to append a proposal or prepared rejection to the peer's inbox, the signing app reads those same keys in order and verifies the current sender and counterparty permissions. P goes only from its unchanged, unconflicted pending record when the permanent key is absent. A prepared rejection requires the verified result of its original delete and must still agree with the current records. It stays held if either record is indeterminate. It is withdrawn if the same proposal is pending again or has been accepted. A retry after an unanswered append preserves the original envelope and its delivery rules.

**Restored and late withdrawals.** After a restore, or on receiving a late acceptance Q of its own signed P, the original proposing app (payer or seller) reads the pending key and then the permanent key for that counterparty and Allowance ID. If the permanent record already holds P and Q, Q is a repeat and no withdrawal is due. If neither record is live, the app suppresses the obsolete proposal. To construct a withdrawal naming P, it must also verify the service-confirmed application of its own signed create of P at the pending key, the counterparty's signed rejection naming P, and the service proof that the proposer's own conditional delete on that rejection applied to the exact version it created. The app must establish that this deletion still accounts for the current absence and that the pending record has not since been recreated. When this retirement is proved and delivery is still owed, the proposer sends one signed withdrawal referencing only P under the checks below. Construction from any other retirement remains unavailable.

An already prepared withdrawal resumes from its original signed message and verified applied delete. Before sending either withdrawal, including on retry, the app checks the same two current records, the retirement of that exact lifetime and that delivery is still owed. After an orderly change of service generation it verifies the original applications with the service keys for their generations and establishes continuity through the change on its own account, including when the seller proposed P. Missing evidence, an indeterminate read or a provider loss that broke that history keeps the withdrawal held, including on a fresh installation. A later creation of the same signed P starts a new lifetime. Once the app verifies that recreation, it suppresses the old withdrawal even if the newer lifetime has since been accepted or deleted.

**Live original proposal after the Allower's provider loss.** Suppose the Allower app proposed P and receives the Allowee's signed acceptance Q, and the Allower’s current verified identity document has a non-null `recovery_from` at least as large as P’s signed `allower_generation`. An outage, an unknown generation or a proposal naming a future generation leaves the acceptance held and authorizes no withdrawal. The app verifies Q’s parties, Allowance ID and reference to the signed P and tells its host that this acceptance cannot open a budget. An older backup can establish this from the authenticated live P and matching Q even if it lacks the applied create. The notice grants no authority to withdraw. The app reads its pending key followed by its permanent key before deciding whether it can act. If the permanent key is absent and the pending key still holds the verified application of that app's own original signed create of P, the app withdraws this pending lifetime. A recovery upload must identify the very same original signed write. A matching message hash alone is insufficient. A conflict, a competing obligation, a later lifetime or uncertainty about either read or the original application holds the operation. The app creates no accepted record, zero ledger or grant.

The Allower conditions its delete on the exact installed pending version and application and the permanent key’s continued absence. It retains the signed withdrawal W, which names only P. Before releasing W, the app durably binds P and its verified original create, the matching signed Q, W and the exact applied delete, including its authenticated service proof, to the same parties, Allowance ID and pending lifetime. An interrupted app resumes this obligation with the same W. If it cannot establish that the guarded delete applied, W stays held. The app tells its host which acceptance the withdrawal settles. Original delivery and every retry require current sending authority and the ordinary deny and standing checks for a generated end (Section 8.1).

The Allowee that still holds the matching accepted P and Q records its own signed end naming both before sending it to the Allower as in Section 8.3, Ending. The Allower uses its retained settlement to recognize this return even after reopening with no permanent Allowance record. The end must come from that Allowee to that Allower, name the same Allowance ID and reference exactly the retained P then Q. The app durably retires that settlement before releasing custody of the matching end. Reopening or receiving the same end again preserves the completed outcome and sends no further withdrawal. An unrelated end, different Q or unproved lifetime follows the ordinary end rules and leaves the settlement outstanding. Recognizing the return creates no permanent Allowance, ledger, grant or spending authority and leaves any later lifetime intact. If the replacement simply has no live pending record, the separate retirement and continuity requirements above still apply.

**Acceptance.** Only the recipient of the current signed proposal P can sign its acceptance Q. Q names P's hash and the same Allowance ID. Both parties apply the same rules to live and previously held replies. A conflicting proposal, stop, unavailable key or indeterminate record leaves the Allowance unopened. A proposal previously seen divergent needs its user's confirmation under Section 6, including after a fresh read shows it clean. On the Allower's account, P's signed `allower_generation` must be at most its current generation and strictly greater than any verified `recovery_from`. A proposal from before the Allower's provider loss cannot open a new budget on its replacement service.

When the Allowee proposes, the Allower's wallet obtains the user's approval for this P and for that wallet to execute its terms, signs Q and creates its ledger before sending Q. When the Allower proposes, its wallet obtains the same approval when signing P. The Allowee signs Q and records its accepted copy before sending Q. Only the Allower app that signed P can use that retained decision when the matching Q arrives. An app that signed neither the Allower's P nor its Q cannot take over the approval. A proposal or reply signed by the other party conveys no local user consent. Both flows keep the same Allowance ID and one proposal and answer.

On each party's own service, one batch creates the permanent `allowances` record with the original signed P and Q and deletes the exact current pending P. On the Allower's service this same batch also creates its permanent initial `allowance_ledgers` record bound to Q (wire 5.9). The Allowee writes only its accepted copy and pending deletion. The creates require current absence at application. Only this original batch creates the ledger in an uninterrupted account history. The Allower's approving wallet must hold `paykit_allowance_execute` and its required scopes. The permanent ledger at this key requires executor scope for creation and every update. Subsequent writes also require the executor authority in Section 8.3. The Allowee needs no executor scope for its two operations. The service refuses deletion of the permanent ledger. Later acceptances, deletions, rejections, confirmations and copies of either message preserve the existing usage. An absent previously observed permanent record in that history raises the rollback alarm.

The original ledger uses the fixed, named permanent policy in wire Section 5.1. Its create and all updates require `paykit_allowance_execute`.
The answering app retains the original signed Q and its prepared operations until the service result is known. Before each inbox append it needs the verified result of the whole original accepting batch, current ordered reads of the pending and permanent keys, and a permanent record holding this P and Q without a stop or conflict. A retry uses the original Q. An unknown batch outcome holds it. After restoring an outstanding answer, a retained write alone cannot prove that the other batch members applied. A repeat of the already recorded Q leaves the ledger and its usage in place.

**Execution.** The Allower's approving wallet retains its approval and the original evidence specified in wire 5.9 in its protected app state, including in its backup. The evidence survives an unanswered submission and process exit. This same app can complete its first grant after reopen or restore when it verifies that evidence, current authority and current records. A missing or stale backup that cannot establish the original, a different App ID, or an unresolved acceptance or grant holds execution. No grant or payment follows solely from a restored zero. A separately applied grant and complete current spending history remain necessary before automatic payment. The rules in Sections 7.2 and 8.3 for resuming an existing grant, including fresh confirmation when required after a restore, still apply.

### 8.4 Paying without a request

- **Destinations:** the payee's latest Private Payment List from the `payment_lists` record, or its public endpoints. Private and public payment are separate modes, as in v0.1: the app or the user chooses one explicitly, and a payer never changes from one to the other on its own. The app that published the destination must be current (Section 7.2).
- **Record first:** before paying, the payer writes, in one batch, the `attempt` on the payment's own claim (its obligation is derived from the blinded attempt ID under wire 1.5, and its `deadline` is 10 minutes after the latest service time the app has seen, so its tombstone goes 30 days after that, at the end of its reconciliation window, rather than 400 days after its last entry: there is no re-sent copy for it to stop), a `payer_activity` record for the payment, keyed by the attempt ID, and, for a single-use endpoint, the `payment_lists` record marking it used, at its next version. Each payment without a request takes a fresh attempt ID. Two own apps therefore can't both pay one single-use endpoint, and a crash after paying leaves an attempt the wallet presents as potentially paid and resolves like any other (Section 7.3).
- **Without the switch:** a payment without a request to a public endpoint (Section 4) may go ahead while the payer's own switch doesn't answer. Its obligation is its own, derived from its fresh attempt ID, so no other app can attempt it, and a public endpoint has nothing to reserve. Its journal keeps that attempt ID, and reconciliation and Late recording below use it, so the claim they write is the one the payment would have written through the switch. The app still checks the destination and its own `identity.json` (fresh within 60 s and naming the service the app uses, so a switch the identity moved away from is followed first, Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3), judges the deny list as Section 8.1 says, so it pays only on a copy of the lists read at most 10 minutes before and none while it holds no such copy, journals the payment, checks the transfer, the destination's expiry (Section 7.2), the freshness of its `identity.json` and its copy of the deny list once more on what it holds, reading no network after them, and pays. Once the switch answers again, recording follows Late recording below, so the identity's other apps and the payee see the payment. A payment that the payment method reports failed is taken as having moved no money, and nothing of it is written; the method's history is still asked about it while it keeps it, and one that shows it completed after all is written then, as above. While the restored app is in reconcile mode (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4) the `attempt` waits with every other new attempt. A restored app whose journal holds such a payment, not yet written, takes an `attempt` the claim shows as written, with its step and `deadline`, and otherwise asks its payment method's history, never taking the claim's silence as not paid. The switch's first returning answer does not fix a deadline for an attempt not yet created; the recording batch uses Late recording below. Until then it is in that app's journal only: if the app is restored from a backup taken before that write, the payment is lost from the shared history (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4), though not from the payment method's. Payments of requests, Billing Periods, under an Allowance or to a single-use endpoint still need the switch, since their claim or reservation is what stops a second payment, and so does a payment to any endpoint of a Private Payment List: without its switch the app can't read the payee's `payment_lists` record as the switch holds it now, so it can't tell whether another own app has paid a single-use endpoint of the list's version, which consumes the whole version (Reusable and single-use endpoints, below), or recorded a newer list from the payee, whose latest list is the one paid to (Destinations, above). A public endpoint depends on neither.
- **Late recording:** recording an already dispatched payment never dispatches it again and always uses its original attempt ID and obligation. Once success is authoritatively known, including from later method history, and reconciliation permits a new attempt, the app accounts for every batch that could have recorded that attempt before it prepares another. An uninterrupted durable submission history that shows no batch was submitted establishes that none applied; a journal restored from a backup does not. A submitted batch whose answer is missing stays unresolved: the app retries it unchanged or obtains the authenticated evidence of its result under Switch wire 6.4 and 6.8. Neither an absent claim nor a refusal of a later request proves that an earlier batch never applied; after provider loss, expiry of retained batch evidence or claim forgetting, absence remains insufficient. The app retains the unresolved batch and its operations until it can account for them, following the Switch's restore and provider-loss rules (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 12.4 to 12.6).
  - If no recording batch ever applied, and none already submitted can still apply, the app reads the claim and service time afresh, then prepares the first batch of the original `attempt` and `payer_activity` with a `deadline` 10 minutes after that verified time. This may be later than the switch's first returning answer: late success in method history, reconciliation, quota and submission delays do not consume the recording window before it starts. If this batch is delayed past its deadline, it is not submitted as a new attempt under that expired deadline. A new batch with a newly computed deadline is permitted only once the same non-application and no-longer-pending conditions hold for every earlier batch; the attempt ID and obligation stay unchanged. The app journals each batch before submission. It records the known outcome as Section 7.2 requires, with `done` and the proof only once success is known, without a payment call.
  - If the attempt already exists, the app takes its actual step and pinned deadline, including none, and reads the activity record. It never replaces the attempt or refreshes that pin. An open or unknown attempt can be completed after its deadline; a done attempt is not completed twice. A failed attempt, or a claim that was applied and later forgotten, uses the existing payment-proof and receipt correction rules (Section 7.2, What a payment method declares), not a fresh attempt. While an earlier batch might have applied but its result cannot be established, no new attempt is created and that uncertainty is retained; it is never treated as a failed payment or permission to pay again.
- **After paying:** the payer writes `done` and updates the record, as in Section 7.2, and sends a `payment_proof` with no request reference. The proof is required after paying a single-use endpoint. Only the payee's receipt settles the claim by evidence: the payer matches a receipt to the payment through its record, whose counterparty must be the receipt's sender, since the obligation doesn't name the payee.
- **Unlabelled:** a payment without a request carries no label, so its proof reaches only the payee's apps holding `paykit_request`, which record and credit it (Section 3.4).
- **Blocked payers:** public endpoints are readable by anyone, so a counterparty the payee's deny list holds can still pay one. Once the block applies, the payee's switch refuses its proof (Pubky Switch spec, Section 7), and nothing asks for it again. A payee app holding `paykit_contacts` drops a proof its inbox accepted before the block applied as one never processed, asks for it again once the counterparty is unblocked, and then judges it as any proof (Section 8.1, step 4). Otherwise such a payment shows only in the payee's payment method.
- **Credited once:** the payee records a payment without a request by its settlement: the Payment Endpoint Identifier paid and the settlement ID its payment method defines (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13.3). A second proof of the same settlement at the same endpoint names the same record even under a new `msg_id`. A valid receipt already held for that occurrence shows it was credited, so the app can show that receipt on replay without issuing another. When the credit writes a mark, the mark names this record and prevents another credit of the settlement to a request, a Billing Period or a payment at another endpoint (Section 8.1 step 4). A hand-confirmed receipt without a mark carries only the protection of the receipts each app can verify (Section 13). One transaction is one settlement (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 1.5). A proof whose method gives no settlement ID isn't credited. Each proof type has one spelling of its settlement ID, such as a `bitcoin-txid` proof's `txid` in lower-case hex, and a proof in another spelling isn't credited, so one settlement never names two records (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 1.5).
- **Only what it offered:** a proof that names a Private Payment List of the payee app checking it (`payment_app_id` and `list_version`, `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.10), for a request or without one, is credited by that app only for a payload it offered that payer at that version, while it still keeps the offer. It keeps what each version offered until 30 days after its `expires_at`, the time a payer keeps a proof sent before then to re-send it (Section 9); after that the record of the offer is gone. Within that time, a proof naming a payload or a version it didn't offer that payer isn't credited and gets no receipt; the proof stays in its record, and the payer gets no receipt, its claim showing what its own outcome wrote (Section 7.3). Another own app, which doesn't know what the publishing app offered, checks such a proof by its payment method alone, as any other proof (Section 8.1 and Credited once, above), and so does the publishing app after that time: a late proof of a real payment is still credited and gets its receipt (Section 7.2, What a payment method declares).
- **Reusable and single-use endpoints.** Paying to a reusable endpoint consumes nothing, so the payer can pay again at once. Paying to a single-use endpoint consumes the whole list version, as paying any endpoint did in v0.1 (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "Resolve Private Payment"), until the publisher sends a newer one, even if the payment fails; the payer then asks for a newer list.
- **Giving payloads back:** a publishing app gives a single-use payload back to its payment method only when no payer can pay it: the app can establish that the version carrying it was never sent, the client reports `MessageUndelivered { resend: false }` or other authenticated evidence proves the recipient never accepted that version, a newer version reached that inbox first, or the app was removed from its identity. On `DeliveryUnknown`, the publisher keeps the payload reserved until it establishes that no payer can use it, and reconciles the delivery under Deliveries of unknown outcome. A failed re-send of a previously accepted list leaves that acceptance in force. A version a restore brought back before it was seen delivered is given back only when the app is removed from its identity: otherwise it stays the payment method's to expire, since the device the backup came from may have delivered it.
- **Publishing:** only an app whose current certificate holds plain `paykit_request` publishes lists. The host chooses the recipient and the Payment Endpoint Identifiers it permits the app to publish. A renewal may use fresh single-use payloads supplied by those methods for that recipient (wire 4.13). Adding a recipient or an identifier requires a host choice. After restore, the host reapplies its sharing choices and sets its list before any nonempty publication (Section 8.4.1). The app sends its current list to the counterparties it chooses, for example each new contact, and a newer version whenever its list changes, when it receives a proof for one of its single-use endpoints, or when it receives a `private_payment_list_request` naming a used version, an expired one, or none (`held_version: null`). A change is owed to every counterparty that gets the app's lists, from the commit that makes it until the message carrying it is queued, so a failed queueing or a crash sends it at a later receive. The batch that reserves a version also queues its message (Section 8.4.1), so a crash after that batch sends that message, never another for the same reservation. When the client gives up a version, empty or not, with `DeliveryUnknown`, the app reconciles it under Deliveries of unknown outcome (Section 9) and sends nothing in its place on its own: a newly signed version goes only on the user's authorization that accounts for the possibly delivered earlier version, a new operation, not a replacement. A newer version owed for another reason, such as a list change or an answer to the counterparty's request, still goes. A version that fails to go for another reason, such as the size bound, is tried again hourly for a day, then daily.
- **Answering a list request:** only an app holding plain `paykit_request` can answer, and it answers only a counterparty it sends its lists to; any other gets no list, and nothing is reserved for it (Section 8.4.1, Who causes a counter), but for the withdrawal a restore owes (Withdrawing a list). A request naming the newest version the publishing app sent that counterparty counts as naming a used one, on the payer's word, and gets a newer version: a payer that reserved a single-use endpoint and then failed to pay has consumed that version without a proof. A request naming an older version that hasn't expired, used or not, gets the newest version re-sent, and nothing newly issued. A request naming an expired version, or none, gets a newer version, as above. So that a payer can't use up its single-use payloads, the app sends a counterparty at most one newer version for each version it remembers that counterparty naming (the Paykit SDK is to remember every version it still tracks, and 16 others for 30 days after their answer), and at most a small number of newer versions in any 24 hours in answer to its requests, which it chooses (the Paykit SDK is to use 4). Past the bound of one newer version per version named, a request naming a version already answered gets the newest version sent again, with nothing newly issued, so an answer lost on the way is answered; the app sends the newest version again to a counterparty at most once an hour. Past the daily bound it issues nothing newer: the request is held, and answered with a newer version once the bound allows, unless a newer version sent meanwhile, such as a renewal or a list change, answered it. A proof of one of the single-use endpoints of the newest version it sent that counterparty is answered the same way, within the same daily bound: the publishing app doesn't verify the proof before it answers, so a counterparty sending proofs can't make it issue more fresh payloads than its requests could.
- **Blocked counterparties:** while its copy of the deny list holds a counterparty (Section 8.1), or that copy isn't known after it tried to read the lists, a publishing app holding `paykit_contacts` sends it no list, renewal or answer. Before it sends one, it reads the lists again once its copy is 10 minutes old or more (Section 8.1). A renewal, an answer or a change owed to a counterparty it already sends its lists to is kept, and goes at the first receive after its copy shows that counterparty not blocked and nothing else holds it (Section 7.2, What a restore loses; Publishing; Answering a list request). A list the host chooses to send a counterparty is refused instead, and the host asks again. A nonempty list already queued, including a renewal or answer, is held before delivery while that counterparty is blocked or the deny copy is unknown, through every send path and after reload or restore (Section 8.1, Messages already queued). It goes only once sends are permitted and it is still eligible; its replay deadline is unchanged. A withdrawal goes all the same, as below. Unblocking ends the pause.
- **Withdrawing a list:** when an authorized publisher stops sending a counterparty its lists, it sends at once an empty newer version, held sends or not: it only stops payments to endpoints the app no longer wants paid, which a payer it blocked could otherwise still pay. The publisher may retry an original withdrawal that is still pending. Once the client gives it up, a replacement requires proof that the original was never sent or never reached the recipient, such as `MessageUndelivered { resend: false }`, and follows the schedule below. The publisher never requeues a given-up message. On `DeliveryUnknown`, a newer empty version goes only as Publishing, above, says. A failed re-send of an accepted withdrawal preserves its original acceptance. After a restore, Section 7.2 independently requires a newly signed empty version to supersede any list the publisher may have sent after the backup. The publisher reserves it under Section 8.4.1. Retries of that fresh withdrawal follow the same delivery rules. A clean restore does not prove that a carried withdrawal is newer than the counterparty's list. An eligible retry goes an hour after the withdrawal at the earliest, then hourly for the rest of a day from the withdrawal and daily after, while a version that counterparty may hold can still be paid; one a restore's withdrawal (Section 7.2, What a restore loses) recorded whose message the client never took goes again at once. One the counterparty's switch refused for good isn't sent again, a restored one included. After a restore, until its host sets its list again, an authenticated eligible list request naming a held version from a counterparty the restored state holds nothing of also causes a withdrawal. Every newly constructed withdrawal follows Section 8.4.1, including the wait for the app's own recovery upload after a provider loss, and reserves the next pair from the current per-recipient counter; a carried empty list never supplies that authority. At an unchanged generation, the withdrawals after successive restores take successive pairs of that one counter, whatever the verified times. A genuinely greater verified generation starts its own namespace; a provider change without that advancement does not. After the withdrawal a nonempty list can go only to a recipient the host has chosen for sharing. Resetting the list for chosen recipients cannot turn a withdrawal, counter entry or request into permission to share with another recipient.
- **No usable list:** if the list is missing, expired or used up, the payer sends `private_payment_list_request`, and sends it again at most once in any 24 hours (the reference interval) while it still has no usable list; a request known to be undelivered counts as not sent, and goes again at the payer's next receive. If the client reports terminal unknown delivery, the payer follows Deliveries of unknown outcome in Section 9 and makes no automatic replacement ask. No request goes while the payer's copy of the deny list holds the payee or isn't known: a request that went and didn't reach the payee's inbox goes again at the payer's next receive once that ends, if that is within 24 hours of the request, and otherwise at its next payment; one held unsent goes at its next payment. A list has expired at its `expires_at` or 30 days after the payer's inbox accepted it, whichever comes first, judged by both of the payer's clocks (Sections 5 and 7.2). The payee can't be forced to answer, and doesn't answer past its bounds (Answering a list request, above), so the payer waits for the payee's next list, such as its renewal (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.13), and tells the user it is waiting for the payee's list. It may offer to pay to the payee's public endpoints instead, if there are any, but pays there only when the user chooses to. A list paused as divergent (Section 6, Divergent records) is neither missing, expired nor used up, and these pause rules apply only to a list whose generation the check below admits, since a list of a higher generation neither pauses a payment nor is paid: when every Private Payment List of the payee that would otherwise be usable is paused, a payment by hand waits for its user's confirmation or a choice of public endpoints, and the payer asks no publishing app whose list is paused for a newer one meanwhile, since the upload may hold one. A list of an app that the payee's `identity.json` doesn't list in `apps`, or lists as removed, plays no part, paused or not, nor does a list of a higher generation than that copy (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.13): the payer judges that on a fresh copy, as the destination check takes it, read before it judges any list paused, and a read of that copy that fails refuses the payment, the list taken neither as a listed app's nor as a removed app's. A list of an app that `apps` lists, paused as divergent, pauses the payment whatever certificate its held list was signed under, since the upload may hold one signed under the certificate the app is listed with now.

#### 8.4.1 Reserving Private Payment List versions

**Record policy.** The Switch enforces the permanent policy and its generation at creation, update and deletion (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 10.3 and 10.3.1). A zero-version condition matches only if the service holds neither a value nor a retained delete at application.

**The counter.** A publishing app numbers the lists it sends each recipient with a counter it keeps at its own service: one `private_list_publications` record for its App ID, the recipient identity and the publishing identity's generation (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.8). The record's policy is the Switch's permanent record policy naming that generation (`"permanent": {"generation": g}`): the record is created only in that generation, and no app can delete it while the account is in it. At a consistent hosting service, a key created under this policy stays present throughout its generation, even after every app has read past a later update. The writer of the authenticated `record.put` must be the publishing App ID in the value and key (wire 5.8). This binding applies to the current write, each retained counter used as a floor and every write used to discover a recipient after restore. A value that cannot be opened or bound with its writer to this key withholds publication. A list's `version` is the pair of that generation and the counter its own write sets (wire 4.13). Another recipient's lists never move this counter, so a recipient learns nothing from it about the publisher's other recipients.

**Reserve and queue in one batch.** To send a list to a recipient, the app:

1. checks that its current certificate holds plain `paykit_request`, takes the generation `g` of its own current `identity.json`, verified as for every send (fresh within 60 s, Section 7.2), and reads the counter record of its App ID, the recipient and `g` at its service;
2. takes `c`, the greater of the current counter and the counter of the newest publisher-authenticated write of that record it holds itself. It starts at 0 only on authenticated current absence of the key, when it has no contrary observation;
3. signs the list with `version` `{g, c + 1}`, keeping nothing (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2);
4. sends one batch holding the counter's write, a `record.put` of counter `c + 1` with that message's ID and hash, conditioned on the version it read and the exact current write when a recovery upload requires it (Section 6), or on `{0, 0}` for a current first creation, and the signed message, which the client puts in its outbox only once the switch applies the batch (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11).

The batch reserves the signed pair only if the counter write applies. For compliant writers on a consistent service, every list that reaches the recipient has a pair at or below the current counter for that generation. On a first conditional write known to have lost a race, the client queues nothing. The publisher verifies the winning record or reads it again, rechecks its host decision and permissions, and signs any new publication from the resulting counter.

If the answer may have been lost, reconcile the original batch under the Switch's retry rules. An authenticated result showing that its imported operations already applied confirms the original reservation even if it is returned in a conflict response. Only definitive nonapplication allows a fresh reservation in place of the unresolved one. If the deciding retry window expires without evidence, retain the uncertainty and withhold another publication for that recipient until the current counter and original result can be reconciled. A retry of an applied reservation and the recipient's delivery outcome are separate. Sections 3.1 and 8.4 govern a pending, accepted or given-up delivery.

An eligible unchanged re-send uses the original pair and signed bytes, including when answering a repeated request or an older hint under Section 8.4. A change, renewal, newly issued request answer or newly constructed withdrawal takes a new reservation.

A changed host decision, restore or loss of sending authority applies to retained batches as well as queued messages. If the current certificate holds only labelled selling scopes, a host's first publication is refused before signing or reserving. A renewal to labelled scopes alone also withholds each already owed list, withdrawal and re-send through every delivery path. The app tells its host why and retains the obligation until authorized again and otherwise eligible. If an earlier batch can still apply, the client must suppress any delivery that would no longer be permitted, including one that becomes ready only after the change. The publisher keeps the outstanding withdrawal or updated list until it can reserve and queue the eligible replacement. A withheld or unknown delivery does not release a single-use payload.

A counter at 9007199254740991 is used up: the app sends that recipient no list, a withdrawal included, for the rest of the generation, and tells its host. It never wraps, and never moves the generation itself.

**Ordering.** Versions compare generation first, then counter (wire 4.13). When the service preserves its state and authorized writers follow these rules, every new reservation in the generation exceeds every pair already sent to that recipient. This holds across a restore, repeated restores of the same backup and two instances of one app. Each continues from the counter at its service by a conditional write, and no time enters a version. A change of generation (a move, a key change in place, a provider loss) gives a new key, whose first list `{g', 1}` is above every list of earlier generations; nothing else is needed for it. The Switch permits deletion of a counter after the account passes its generation. The publisher still needs to discover recipients to whom an earlier list could remain payable after a restore. It must retain the earlier record for that purpose until the recipient can be discovered from another durable publication record or every list it could cover is unpayable. An earlier counter is never used to allocate a pair in the new generation.

**Writes by other apps.** The permanent policy permits another own app holding `paykit_request` to write at the key. If the current write is by a different App ID, the publisher reports that writer and holds publication for that key, including withdrawals. It keeps any authenticated floor already held. It never takes a counter, recipient or exhaustion decision from the other app's write, or treats that write as absence. The same checks apply after restore with no local floor. A publisher-authenticated current write is required to resume. Under the named publisher's own signature, a lower counter or the same counter with different message bytes is reported and cannot lower the floor held. A restored app with no earlier observation cannot detect a regression signed by the publisher itself. A value that does not read keeps publication held until a bound write that reads replaces it. A wrong writer can therefore prevent this recipient's publications while its write stands.

**Withdrawal.** A withdrawal is an empty list, reserved the same way. Sending it must never add the recipient to the conversation list at the publisher's switch (Pubky Switch spec, Section 7). The app determines this for the individual signed message from its empty `payment_endpoints` and keeps the decision with any signed, queued or retained delivery before a conversation entry can be written. It applies to direct send, the counter batch and its resolution, reconstruction from signed bytes, reload, restore, flush, retry and re-send, even if the recipient never accepts the withdrawal. If the client cannot enforce that decision on a path, the publisher keeps the withdrawal owed and withholds that delivery. An eligible nonempty first list follows the ordinary conversation rule. An authorized publisher sends an empty list even when the counterparty is blocked or the deny copy is unknown (Section 8.4, Blocked counterparties). After a provider loss it waits, as every write of the app does, until the app's own recovery upload is done (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6).

**Who causes a counter.** An app creates a counter record only for a recipient its host chose to send its lists to (Section 8.4, Publishing), when it publishes to it or answers its request, and for the withdrawal a restore owes (Section 8.4, Withdrawing a list): after a restore, until its host sets its list again, an authenticated eligible list request naming a held version from a counterparty the restored state holds nothing of causes a withdrawal and so a counter, the one case where a counterparty's message leads to one. Otherwise a list request from a counterparty the app sends no lists to gets no list and creates no record, whatever version it names, or none.

**Restore.** The restored app checks its current selling scope before discovering recipients or issuing a withdrawal. A backup, a retained counter or renewed host consent supplies no publishing authority. If the app lacks plain `paykit_request`, discovery and any owed withdrawals wait. Until the host reapplies its sharing choices and sets its list, the restored app sends only withdrawals. It discovers possible previous recipients from its backup and all readable records whose authenticated writer and bound publisher are its App ID at the service, including earlier generations. A recipient added after the backup may have only an earlier-generation record and an unexpired payable list. It withdraws for that recipient using the current generation's key and counter (step 2), preserving any higher floor it holds. An incomplete listing keeps the restore discovery pending. A counter or a withdrawal identifies a recipient for withdrawal only. Even after a second backup and restore it gives no authority to send that recipient a nonempty list.

**Single-use payloads.** If the original batch is proved never to have applied, its unissued payloads may be used in the next list for that recipient or given back under Section 8.4. When the batch outcome is uncertain they stay reserved. Once the batch is known to have applied, give-back requires the delivery evidence in Section 8.4, Giving payloads back. An expired deciding window, a failed re-send or uncertainty about recipient acceptance supplies no such evidence.

**Bounds and privacy.** Each publishing app, recipient and generation holds one permanent key while the account is in that generation, counted against the account's live keys (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3: 100,000 by default), with one sealed counter value (wire 5.8). So one publishing app reaches the limit at about 100,000 recipients in one generation, less what the account's other records hold. The counter record shows the service an opaque key, its writer, its policy and the time of the write. It stores the recipient inside the sealed value. The counter's write is one record write in the batch that queues the list, under the ordinary operation limits (Switch wire 9.4). A requester's `held_version` is a hint, never a counter (wire 4.14).

### 8.5 Two payer apps on one identity

Both receive the request and are notified. The other app learns about the payment through the feed, from the request's claim, whether a wallet or an Allowance executor paid it, and presents that state in its own words. After `attempt`, another wallet is handling the payment. The observing app reports the payment as paid only after SETTLED.

Once the claim's entries are stubs in the feed (Switch wire 7.5), the other app learns SETTLED by reading the claim again (Switch wire 7.4.8), or from the payer's activity record, which carries the proof.

## 9. Delivery and re-sends

Paykit's messages are delivered as the Switch delivers every message (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11). Paykit adds a delivery state:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Paid | For the payer: the obligation is SETTLED. For the payee: it has verified the proof |

- **Sender retention:** the sender keeps the signed plaintext and `accepted_at` of each message it sent for at least 30 days from its first acceptance, to answer `resend_request`; it may keep them longer, but answers a `resend_request` only with messages first accepted in the last 30 days, the time recipients keep processed IDs, so every copy it re-sends is still deduplicated. A re-send doesn't restart this: the sender keeps one entry per `msg_id`, with its first acceptance time and the later ones, and a request naming any of those times selects it. A re-sent message is sealed to the current receive keys and keeps its `msg_id` and signed plaintext.
- **Deduplication of re-sent requests:**
  - A re-sent request, even after that window, has the same obligation ID, so its claim shows whether it was already handled; once a claim without a deadline is gone, 400 days after its last entry, the payer's record of the payment does (Sections 6 and 7.2).
- **Recovery request:** `resend_request` asks the counterparty to re-send messages; answering is best effort. Sending an ask and re-sending its answer both require `paykit_contacts` and a known deny copy that permits the counterparty (Section 3.1, Outbound authorization); receiving an ask needs only a Paykit scope. The counterparty re-sends only kinds that the requesting app's certificate allows it to receive (Section 3.3), and a payer's replies only to an app that would take them under their label (Section 3.4). It limits what it re-sends: one message to the same requester identity at most once an hour, and at most a number of messages per requester identity a day, which it chooses (500 in the reference configuration). It leaves the rest out, and the requester may ask again later, subject to the unknown-delivery rule above.
- **Messages dropped for a bound:** every bound on the received messages an app keeps drops a message as one never processed and records a bounded recovery obligation for the drop. This guarantees the required pair and mark changes, not delivery of an ask or recovery of the payload. The families so bounded are the replies, period details and Allowance answers kept before their record, and the stops kept for a record the app doesn't hold (Section 8.1, Replies before their record and Messages that cross; Section 8.3, Ends reach the executor), with the extension messages not yet taken (Section 3.3) and messages the client drops while waiting for protocol admission (Section 8.1, Waiting for admission), which count in those same dropped-reply marks; the messages kept over a divergent record (Section 8.1, Stored entries); the entries dropped unverified, and those the client drops while they wait for a scope key (Section 8.1, Stored entries); and, dropped for the user's block rather than a bound, the messages whose marks `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1 keeps, which follow the same rule. For an admitted message, in the commit that drops it the app forgets its pair (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11), or, for the user's block, once the read is done, so a re-sent copy is taken, and counts it in its sender's mark of its family. A client admission-custody drop instead commits the client's durable notice with the custody removal, and the app acknowledges that notice only with its required dropped-reply mark and host-notice changes in one local transaction (Section 8.1, Waiting for admission); it does not forget an independently protected pair or effect. An incoming ordinary victim is counted without ever gaining a processed pair. Each family keeps one mark per sender identity, holding the earliest acceptance time among the messages it counts, which a re-send asks from, and the latest, which the mark expires by. The mark asks that sender to re-send what its inbox accepted from its earliest time (`resend_request` with `accepted_from`, and `accepted_until` at its latest time where its family's section says so, `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.12) as soon as sends to that sender aren't held (Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties), whatever records the app holds, and again at most once in any 24 hours by its own clock while it lives; a mark of messages that waited for a scope key asks only once the app holds that key, since a copy sent before would wait for it again, and a mark of the user's block also waits for the conditions `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 4.1 gives. A missing `paykit_contacts` scope leaves the ask unsent, reports `MissingScope` to the host and keeps its mark in the ordinary bounded pool, subject to the same expiry and eviction rule below. It is not a deny-held sender and gets no bound exemption. Until the scope is explicitly granted, no ask is signed or queued; once it is granted, the app first obtains a complete deny copy and asks only if that copy permits the sender. Missing authorization never creates another pending-mark family or holds feed intake. An ask still in the outbox awaits its delivery outcome. A restored mark or outbox entry cannot establish whether a prior ask for its window was given up after the backup. If that outcome is unknown, the app holds automatic repeat asks, including asks triggered by the restore, and reconciles with the host. If the client gives up the ask as possibly delivered, Paykit keeps that outcome under Deliveries of unknown outcome in Section 9. It counts as neither a delivered ask for retiring a mark nor a failed ask to replace automatically, including on the next scheduled retry. A request known to be undelivered permits another ask at most once in any 24 hours, and none is queued while one to that sender is still in the outbox; the 24 hours between delivered asks count from the last one the outbox delivered. For mark retirement, an unresolved terminal ask is an ask the app cannot send. Retiring the mark does not resolve the possibly delivered effect. A mark goes 30 days after its latest time; before that, it goes for a bound on marks only once a `resend_request` covering its latest time was delivered, except a block mark only a restore that isn't clean or a `missed` item made, which goes first, the host told (Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties). A mark that waits only because its sender's sends are held is one per family and sender the deny list holds, and counts toward no bound on marks: the deny list's own limit bounds them (Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.3, Storage rules; Switch wire 9.1: `max_deny`, 100,000 by default). Past a bound on marks with none whose ask was delivered, the mark whose latest time is earliest among those whose ask was refused or can't be sent yet goes, the host told once (Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties), so a bound never holds an entry. When a mark expires or is evicted before an ask covering its latest time was delivered, the app tells the host once per sender and window, with no message content (Section 8.1, Blocked counterparties). This notice reports the retired recovery obligation; it neither retains nor reconstructs the dropped payload. Missing authorization, an unknown deny copy, a block, refused delivery or a full mark pool may leave an obligation undelivered until retirement. Marks do not extend the sender's retention, which starts at the message's first acceptance, and a delivered ask is not proof that a message returned. Recovery remains best effort under the sender's retention and re-send budgets, and a dropped payload may be lost permanently. No ordinary family admits a counted payload above its bound. A required earlier victim whose pair or effect remains protected is retained, and only the incoming admission waits under Section 8.1, Waiting for admission. A payload whose required mark cannot be committed is not dropped, and a real local storage failure leaves the incoming entry unprocessed rather than committing an over-bound pool or a cursor-only success. The same atomic intake rule applies to extensions (Section 3.3). Immediate stops and protected first receipts keep their stated obligations and counting rules. These requirements remain subject to the runtime prerequisites above. Where v0.1 refused a message past its bound and left it in its sender's outbox, pruning nothing but superseded lists, intake from other counterparties unaffected (`specs/paykit-sdk.md`, "StorageAdapter"), v0.2 records recovery for a bound-selected drop, and uses the client's bounded waiting custody or durable drop notice when a protected earlier victim prevents admission, without a capacity decision stopping feed intake.
- **What an app asks for:** the service writes the unopenable, unreadable and `missed` items that lead an app to ask for re-sends, and the app can't check them, so it bounds what it asks on their word. For an entry it can't open, a `missed` item and an unreadable item, it never asks for a time before the time of its cursor, less the clock tolerance; for an unreadable item, also only after a verified departure, for an entry accepted at or before it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2); and never for a time that doesn't parse. A `resend_request` adds no conversation entry (Pubky Switch spec, Section 7).

**Delivery control.** The client inventories every owed message, including kept-batch and restored work, and exposes per-message holds and terminal withdrawal. Each retained outgoing message has its durable original or reconstructed origin, separate from recipient acceptance, preserved through retry, re-sealing and restore. Neither origin nor a hold release grants Paykit send authority (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11). Every delivery still satisfies Section 8.1's current per-message authorization and deny checks; Section 8.4.1's publication and restore barriers remain required.

**Stopped record jobs.** When a delete stops a queued record write after the recipient's switch has accepted the message, the Switch client reports the delete's record version and signed operation hash. Together they identify the delete after a recovery upload. Both the protocol that owns the outgoing message kind and the protocol that owns the record's collection receive the notice. Each distinct owner acknowledges it independently. The collection owner can read the record and decide whether it still needs a write (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 10.3, 11 and 12.6).

**Deliveries of unknown outcome.** When the client gives up a delivery it cannot prove undelivered, it reports `DeliveryUnknown` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 11). The message may have reached its recipient. Paykit must never queue a given-up message again, even with its original signed bytes. While the client retains the give-up mark, `queue_stored` does not queue another delivery. A backup omits sent messages and give-up marks, though it carries unacknowledged unknown-delivery duties. On restore the client can therefore accept a copy it had previously given up. Paykit must durably record the possible delivery before acknowledging the duty and reconcile it from a reply, receipt, the shared records or claims the message would change, or contact with the counterparty. Without evidence the outcome stays unknown, including across restart and restore. Paykit must not send a replacement the recipient could act on twice. A genuinely new operation requires separate user authorization that accounts for the possibly delivered effect. A host that cannot establish which original deliveries remain pending must keep unverifiable restored work out of automatic delivery, including calls that can flush it.

When a write receives `410 moved`, keep the original request. Retry it at the new service only after the verified identity document names that service. If the freeze is cancelled and the old service accepts writes again, retry there. The usual send and payment checks still apply (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 6 and 12.5).

## 10. Encodings

The Switch defines the common encodings (Pubky Switch spec, Section 13). The [Paykit wire format](wire-format.md) defines the hash preimages, domain labels and natural keys for each collection in Section 1.5, and the signed grant and handoff fields in Sections 2, 3.3 and 5.4.

**Size limits.** Messages are at most 64 KiB, and the kinds stored in activity records are at most 16 KiB. A shared record value is at most 256 KiB. The default identity limit is 32 apps. Ledger usage and available space depend on the encoded entries (Section 8.3).

## 11. Threat model

The adversaries, what they can and can't do, and the protections are those of the Pubky Switch spec (Pubky Switch spec, Section 14), whose table states what each can do to payments. Paykit adds:

- **Rejections and cancellations aren't claim states, by choice.** A rejection or cancellation lives in the request record, so one a compromised own app or a lost write removes from the record before an app that never saw it reads the record leaves that app free to pay the request, within an Allowance's limits if it pays automatically. Apps that saw it keep it (Section 8.2; `specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.3, Seen roles), and a payee's cancellation an app verified is a stop it keeps in its own state (Section 8.1, Messages that cross).
- **Limits of the switch's copy:** a payer finds the payee's switch through the Homeserver's document, possibly an old one. After a cooperative move a stale copy names the old switch, which, frozen, answers with its last document and the freeze naming the new one, so the payer follows it to the newer document (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 4.3 and 12.5); it does so for the identity's record age limit, after which no record from before the move is payable. This doesn't help after a provider loss, where the old switch is gone or hostile. One that is gone doesn't answer, so the payer pays only on the user's explicit confirmation, and never automatically. A hostile one can answer with its last document and no freeze, which the payer can't tell from a current answer, so the old document's own limits apply (the record age limit, Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.2). Otherwise a switch that doesn't answer, or a malicious one, can only fail to help: it can't serve a newer document than the signer made, nor lower the one the payer uses. Someone flooding the read can't switch the lookup off silently either: reads are limited per network source, answers may be cached, and a payment the switch couldn't confirm needs the user (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3). Anchoring `identity.json` in PKARR would close the provider-loss case too, at the cost of PKARR space and of coupling every `identity.json` update to it; v0.2 doesn't anchor it (Pubky Switch spec, Section 19, decision 23).

**App IDs across installations.** The integrating wallet chooses a separate App ID per installation or shares one App ID and its keys across installations. Each distinct app needs signer approval and can be revoked individually. Installations using one App ID act as one principal with the same grants. The wallet synchronizes their receive keys, cursor, payment journal and other durable state across reopen and restore, and ensures that only one installation executes each payment attempt (Section 13). Loss of a shared app key affects every installation using it. A restored seed alone does not establish current payment state or fresh signer authority.

## 12. Privacy

What each party learns is in the Pubky Switch spec (Pubky Switch spec, Section 15). Paykit adds:

| Party | Learns |
|---|---|
| A payee's switch, from payers | An anonymous lookup of the payee's `identity.json` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3) shortly before a payer without a fresh copy pays a public endpoint: the reader's network address and the time, so that someone is about to pay the identity, but not who. After a cooperative move the old service, frozen, sees the lookups of payers holding the old document for as long as it answers them (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.5). A cache in front of the service sees what the service would |
| A counterparty | The label of a request a labelled app sent (Section 3.1), which is why a label should be opaque |
| A labelled selling app, of the identity's other payee apps | From `settlements` (Section 3.1), whose every bucket write carries its writer's signed operation and certificate: how many settlements the identity has credited, when, and by which own app, whose certificate names its label; and, for a settlement it can name, such as an on-chain payment to a public endpoint, whether it was credited, and to a one-time request, a Billing Period or a payment without a request, the occurrence itself only as a tag. Never a counterparty, an amount, or a settlement it can't name |
| A selling app, a hosted or labelled one included | Through `paykit_contacts`, which every payee app holds (Section 3.1): the identity's contacts, conversation entries, deny list and the senders the service flags, so whom the identity knows and has blocked |

Each operator is responsible for its privacy policy and records of processing. Disclose the metadata identified in Switch decisions 3 and 6. Push is optional for each app.

## 13. Guarantees

- **No duplicate payment for one obligation**, under these conditions:
  - *Holds for:* the same obligation, with compliant apps and a consistent switch: one that follows Pubky Switch spec, Section 6.5, so that every item it acknowledged is durable and it never serves a state behind what it signed.
  - *Does not cover:*
    - a user who confirms an outcome (`reconcile`, `unknown` or `failed`), or a manual payment the user confirms after being warned that it may already have gone through;
    - a malicious or non-compliant switch, including one that shows different apps different histories after any shared prefix, or loses a write it acknowledged and serves on (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 6.5 and 10.1); on such a split a recurring obligation can be paid once per branch in every Billing Period;
    - a payer that carries out an authorized attempt more than once, for example on two devices of one app (rail-level idempotency is the wallet's responsibility);
    - a payment method that reports a final failure and then completes the payment anyway, or whose declared idempotency or history (Section 7.2) doesn't hold;
    - a payee that issues two obligations for one purpose, or misdates a re-sent request during provider loss;
    - an old provider that keeps authorizing attempts after a provider loss (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.6, residual risk).
- **One credit per settlement:** among credits that write a mark, a settlement is credited to one occurrence at most across all own payee apps, labelled ones included, and all counterparties (Sections 6, Settlement marks, and 8.1 step 4), with compliant apps and a consistent switch. *Does not cover:* a faulty or compromised own app holding `paykit_settlements` that drops a mark from its bucket, which an app that never saw it can't tell from an absent one, or that writes a mark no credit made, which stops that settlement's credit; and a credit the user confirms by hand, shown that it may already be credited, such as one in a full, unreadable or divergent bucket or after a provider loss.
- **Allowance executors** are responsible for their Allowances' limits.
- **Confidentiality, forward secrecy, integrity, isolation, idempotent processing and availability** are the Switch's guarantees (Pubky Switch spec, Section 16), which Paykit inherits.

## 14. Performance targets

**Design rule for implementations.** Allowance acceptance uses the ordered own-service reads and conditional write in Section 8.3.1 before delivery to the counterparty. Private Payment List publication follows the ordering and call costs of Section 8.4.1 (Reserve and queue in one batch). For other actions, each user action needs at most one call to each Pubky Switch involved (the counterparty's, for the message, and the identity's own, for claims and shared state), sent in parallel, before the user sees its result, plus local bookkeeping. Follow-up messages, such as a one-time request's acceptance (Section 7.2 step 2), go in the background. The only other calls allowed are the background and parallel Homeserver calls listed in Section 14, the own-switch reads for payment and automatic acceptance listed in Section 14, and one re-send when the counterparty's inbox answers `stale_key` because a recipient app has just replaced its receive key or the recipient has added an app (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 3.6 and 8.1). A first message to a new counterparty also reads its directory, writes its conversation entry and may deliver the sender's `identity.json`, in one parallel round before the append (Section 14 and Pubky Switch spec, Section 7).

| Action | Target |
|---|---|
| Send a message other than a newly constructed Private Payment List | 1 round trip: the append to the recipient's inbox and the shared record write at the sender's own switch, in parallel; 0.3-0.7 s. A first message to a new counterparty adds one round for the directory read, made in parallel with delivering the sender's `identity.json` and writing its conversation entry |
| Reserve and publish a Private Payment List | A read of the own counter record, then one batch with the conditional counter write and the signed list, then the recipient's delivery calls. A conflict reads the current write from its answer. A message sent again keeps its reservation. No service-time call is added for ordering (Section 8.4.1) |
| Receive a message | Delivered on the open long-poll; the shared record batch makes it durable in 1 round trip |
| Recipient sees it | Under 1 s in the foreground; 1-5 s through push |
| Pay a request | Read the claim and write the conditional `attempt` batch (~0.2-0.3 s for the write), with the payer's own `identity.json` revalidated in parallel as in Section 7.2. Count the payment checks below before money moves. A single-use endpoint of a Private Payment List needs an already-fresh copy of the payee's `identity.json`. Wallet time is additional. The acceptance, `done`, the record update and the proof follow in the background |
| Save a contact | 1 operation: allowlist entry |
| Directory lookup | 1 request when a counterparty is new; afterwards cached while the recipient's `identity.json` names the same switch and generation, and refreshed in the background when an append shows a newer `directory_version` |
| Receive key rotation | 1 request, about once per active day per app by default, after a feed read; never on a user-facing step. A sender whose cached directory predates the rotation pays one extra round (`stale_key`), unless it refreshed the directory in the background (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.1) |
| Add or revoke an app | One operation each for the service, `identity.json` and key bundles, whatever the contact count. A revocation also has own apps deliver the new `identity.json`, in the background, to the switches they wrote to in the last 30 days (Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.2) |
| New app catch-up | 1 list call per collection, then the feed |
| Idle | Foreground: one long-poll every ~25 s. Background: none (push) |

**Payment checks.** For a payment the user starts, with or without a request, read the deny list from the payer's switch when its copy is missing or too old under Section 8.1. Read it afresh before automatic acceptance and before each automatic dispatch, including a repeat. For automatic dispatch of a one-time request, also read the current request record and the `block_floor` record. The switch serves each in a separate call. Count any reads needed to confirm the same current state. Before dispatching a Billing Period by hand or automatically, read the subscription and period records (Section 8.2). On a first dispatch, read both before and after recording in the journal that payment is starting. A payment without a request skips the floor read and follows Section 8.4, including its public-endpoint exception when the payer's switch is unavailable.

**Homeserver calls outside the critical path,** allowed by the design rule: revalidating the app's own `identity.json` in parallel with an `attempt` (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.3); the event stream subscription that watches its own and its counterparties' `identity.json`, held while the app runs in the foreground; and fetching a counterparty's `identity.json` in the background when an event names it, or when a copy it doesn't watch is more than 10 minutes old, to route messages and to check the apps it hears from and pays to.

**Paying a public endpoint.** Measure the lookup under Section 7.2, Check the destination. In every sample, verify that the lookup answered before dispatch or that the payer obtained the user's explicit confirmation and made no automatic payment. Compare the number of sequential requests before dispatch with and without the public-service lookup.

Targets are measured by counting requests per action. Latency is judged on a stated network profile for the user's own actions: sending a request, receiving one, tapping Pay until money can move, a first contact and a cold start. Background processing, such as the payer's follow-ups after paying, the payee's handling of an acceptance and a proof, and idle checks, and delivery on the open long-poll are reported separately.

## 16. Left to apps or later

What the Switch leaves to apps or decides later is in the Pubky Switch spec (Pubky Switch spec, Section 17). Paykit adds:

- **Up to each app:**
  - offline endpoint issuing;
  - deferring an occurrence for later reconsideration, kept in the executor's own state: it grants no authority to pay and binds no other app, as in v0.1 (`specs/allowances.md`, "Payment Request integration").
- **Decided later:**
  - a v0.2 ERC-20 Payment Proof profile (Section 15).
  - Allowances that pay requests priced in another asset, for example a subscription priced in usd paid from a btc Allowance. For v0.2 an Allowance pays only requests in its own asset, as in v0.1 (Section 8.3); a payee that wants automatic payment in another asset bills in that asset at its own rate. A later term can widen this without breaking older executors, which keep refusing.
- **No migration from v0.1.** v0.2 replaces v0.1: v0.1 and v0.2 apps can't pay each other, so the payer and the payee both update to v0.2, and a v0.2 app reads and writes no v0.1 state. What was open under v0.1 is set up again under v0.2:
  - each app requests its v0.2 grant under Section 3.4. Neither the app nor the signer searches for, converts or reissues v0.1 grants.
  - public Payment Endpoints are published in each app's v0.2 record (Pubky Switch spec, Section 4.2);
  - Allowances and recurring requests don't carry over: the payee requests and the payer accepts them again under v0.2, and an Allowance's limits count from its v0.2 acceptance;
  - receipts and the rest of the v0.1 payment history stay where the v0.1 app keeps them, and v0.2 doesn't read them;
  - sellers on paykit-server: each seller approves it as a v0.2 app with three scopes, `paykit_request` or its shop's labelled selling scope `paykit_request:<label>`, and `paykit_settlements` and `paykit_contacts`, which either one requires (Section 3.1), and it sends its open requests again.

## 17. Open questions for the team

Paykit and the Switch share these question numbers. The current rules in the cited sections apply until a decision changes them. Switch questions 25, 26, 29 and 32 remain in Pubky Switch spec, Section 19, including the 24-hour erasure rule in its Section 12.7. Section 16 defers Allowances across assets.

27. **Sender lists above the store limit.** When a list cannot fit in one store commit, the wallet defers new activity under Section 8.1 and wire 4.1. How larger lists should be handled remains open.

28. **Lifting the hold after a restore automatically.** After a restore from a backup that isn't clean, and after a `missed` item (Section 7.2, What a `missed` item loses), an app holds every open request and Allowance until the record shows a cancellation or end or the user confirms (Section 7.2, What a restore loses), since the re-sends a `resend_request` brings can't show that nothing more is coming. Lifting the hold without the user would need a `paykit.resend_answer` from each app that answers, saying that its selection was complete, with nothing left out by its limits or its retention; a MUST to answer; and a way for the requester to know which of the counterparty's apps must answer, since each app keeps only the messages it sent. Even then, a sender re-sends only messages first accepted in the last 30 days (Section 9), so a gap older than that stays uncovered. Skipping the hold after a clean restore is decided: the Switch reports the cursor it holds for an app with every feed page, a held cursor ahead of the backup's position shows lost progress, and a restore the report shows clean resumes without a hold (Section 7.2, What a restore loses; Pubky Switch spec, Section 12.4). The question also covers a request first seen after the restore, which may be a re-send of one the backup missed. Candidates for ending its hold: a mark by its sender that it is a re-send, which a sender can't be relied on to set; the end of the 30-day re-send window after the restore (Section 9); or the user's confirmation of each request. Open: until it is decided, the hold follows every restore that isn't clean, covers requests first seen after it, and ends only by the record or the user's confirmation of each request. A `missed` item gives the last acceptance time of the entries it missed, so its hold covers a request first seen after it only when the request's signed `created_at` is earlier than that time plus the clock tolerance (Section 7.2, What a `missed` item loses).

30. **Growth of `used` in a `payment_lists` record.** A payer's `payment_lists` record keeps every `used` entry it has held, and readers ignore a write that drops one (`specs/v0.2/wire-format.md` Section 5.4), so the record only grows, within the 256 KiB limit for a shared record value (Pubky Switch spec, Section 8.2), and paying one payee app's single-use endpoints for long enough fills it. A pruning rule would let an entry go once no own app may still pay from the list version it belongs to, for example once that version has expired, at its `expires_at` or 30 days after the inbox accepted it (Section 8.4); the question also covers what a payer does when the record is full before then. Open: until it is decided, entries are never dropped, and when the record can't take another entry, the `attempt` batch that carries the mark is refused whole (Section 7.2 step 1, Reserve a single-use endpoint), so no single-use payment happens unmarked: the payment fails closed, and the payee then publishes its lists from a new app, whose record starts empty.

36. **Writing over a refused write.** Section 6 specifies which version the app can build on today. An app refuses a write for passing reasons too, such as a writer's certificate it can't place yet, not only for a faulty writer. Any write it makes there, a stop, a counterparty's message it verified or the record of a payment made included, drops what the refused write holds, and the app that wrote that ignores the new write, since it drops a message it saw. So a third own app that never saw the refused write can act on less than the record held. On the payee side: own app B credits a settlement to request R and writes R with the proof and its receipt; own app A refuses that write; a proof of the same settlement arrives naming R or another request R2, and A writes it over the refused write from its trusted content, so R loses the receipt; own app C, which never saw B's write, finds the settlement's mark in its bucket and credits nothing, so R only loses its receipt. On the payer side: the payee's period details, or a payment's record, placed over a refused write that holds another own app's rejection or a single-use endpoint's `used` mark, let a third own app pay a rejected request or pay that endpoint again. Candidate rules: (i) over a write it refused an app writes nothing: it keeps a stop as its own stop, keeps a counterparty's message it verified as a stored message, writes a payment's outcome on the claim alone, and makes no payment, acceptance or credit decision there, until a later write it accepts replaces the refused one or its user confirms after being shown it; or (ii) its write carries over every stored message of the refused write that verifies on its own, and every `used` entry and `manual_only` mark the refused write holds. Open: until it is decided, this exposure is an accepted risk.
37. **A reading app’s check of a newer ledger version.** How a reader obtains both authenticated service times for the check in Section 8.3 remains open (wire 5.5).

## 18. Conformance and release

Run the [conformance cases](conformance.md) against the wallet and its service and reproduce the [wire vectors](vectors/README.md). The [implementation table](implementation.md) identifies the published checks by rule. An independent implementation built from the wire specification must reproduce the vectors. Identical encodings alone do not demonstrate an authorized payment, durable recovery or service behavior.

**Release criteria**

- **Fail:**
  - warm p95 latency above 2 s for a user's own actions, on a mobile 4G profile (80 ms round trip, 1% loss);
  - any duplicate payment outside the exclusions of Section 13;
  - any stuck state that isn't shown to the user with a way to resolve it;
  - iOS push p95 above 10 s.
- **Pass:** the wallet keeps a doubtful payment paused, explains that it may already have gone through, and gives the user a way to resolve it.
- **Network profile:** latency criteria are judged on a stated network profile. The user's own actions are first contact, send, receive, tap Pay and a cold start (Section 14). Background processing, such as the steps after paying and a payee handling an acceptance and a proof, is reported separately, and so is delivery to an open long-poll, which Section 14's "Recipient sees it" target covers.

**Before freeze**

- a last check of the two retention rules (Pubky Switch spec, Sections 6.3 and 8.3);
- team approval of this spec, including the defaults of the open questions, and the decisions that gate the freeze: the labelled selling scopes and the signer hand-over decided in Section 17 (decisions 22 and 24);
- the same measurements against the Pubky staging Homeserver, with storage on disk, and on real phones and real networks, including iOS push p95 with APNs;
- persistent storage in a production switch;
- an independent human crypto review.

**Production requirements**

- **Secrets:** secret types that aren't `Copy`, zeroize on drop, print nothing in `Debug` and derive no `Serialize`; keys in platform keystores (Pubky Switch spec, Section 3.1); deletion by crypto-shredding; and a test that a deleted key's bytes are gone from every file the app persists.
- **Supply chain:** a required cargo-deny job (advisories with expiring ignores, bans on duplicate crypto crates, sources, licenses) with scheduled runs and a Dependabot entry for cargo; the TLS crates kept current; the Android build's linker settings reviewed; one version of each direct crypto crate, one entry point for randomness and a getrandom backend per target; no patched or vendored pubky in any shipped crate, with a `cargo package` check in CI and a scoped conditional-write API asked of Pubky upstream.
- **A client and server crate split:** the mobile bindings must not link a web server, and the client gets TLS through an explicit feature, not through feature unification with pubky's dependencies.
- **A formal model:** state the at-most-once invariant and its assumptions in Section 13 (deadlines derived exactly, a restored journal never read as "not started", transitions durable before they are signed, service time that never goes back, `failed` only on authoritative evidence); model claims, the journal and recovery in Quint or TLA+; and export the model's traces as conformance tests.
- **Conformance:** freeze the vectors, so that a regenerated diff counts as a wire change; add boundary vectors for rules pinned only by text; build a second implementation on a crypto stack other than Rust's; run Wycheproof and the Ed25519 speccheck cases; compare independently implemented parsers on the same inputs; and build a rejection corpus by mutating valid vectors, first the security bindings, the UUID form and the integer bound, then the rest, negative `stale_key` cases included. Releases are gated on it.
- **Fail-closed code:** no `unwrap`, `expect`, indexing or unchecked arithmetic in the protocol crates, enforced by Clippy, and fuzz targets for the parsers, times and Billing Period arithmetic.
- **Faults against real parts:** crash and failover tests against real storage, an approval end to end through Pubky auth, per-app grants enforced by a testnet Homeserver, and an on-device test of the iOS notification extension, with its choice of entry to show, a feed view of entries only and a fixed generic relay payload.
- **Bindings:** only the Rust core parses and verifies signed bodies; Swift and Kotlin never parse them again. CI builds every binding (`build.sh all`) and checks the bindings' diff.
- **The external crypto audit's scope:** the subset of hpke 0.14 that Paykit uses, strict Ed25519 verification with ed25519-dalek 3 and curve25519-dalek 5, and the approval's companion-claim channel.
- **CI.** Run the end-to-end tests against PostgreSQL 16 and fail the required job if the database is unavailable.
- Require the frozen vectors for each release and report SDK latency and success rates from launch.
